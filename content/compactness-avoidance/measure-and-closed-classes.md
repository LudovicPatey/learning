Weak König's lemma restricted to trees of positive measure, **weak weak König's lemma** $\mathsf{WWKL}$, captures probabilistic arguments: its instances are the infinite binary trees $T$ with $\lambda([T]) > 0$, and its solutions their paths. Since some $\Pi^0_1$ class of positive measure contains only Martin-Löf random sequences (:ref{#cor-mlr-structure}), a Turing ideal satisfies $\mathsf{WWKL}$ only if it contains, for each of its members, a sequence random relative to it. This lesson and the next ask two questions. Does $\mathsf{WWKL}$ admit PA avoidance? Yes, and even strong PA avoidance, by a forcing with closed classes of positive measure; this is the subject of this lesson. Does Ramsey's theorem for pairs imply $\mathsf{WWKL}$? No, but PA avoidance cannot show it, and the next lesson introduces the finer property that does. As in the previous lessons, $D \leq_T Z$ are sets with $Z$ not PA relative to $D$, and we use the relative measure $\lambda(\mathcal B \mid \sigma) = 2^{|\sigma|} \lambda(\mathcal B \cap [\sigma])$ of :ref{to="martin-lof-randomness/upper-cones"}.

## Weak weak König's lemma

:::theorem{#thm-wwkl-strong-pa}
Every closed class $\mathcal Q \subseteq \cs$ with $\lambda(\mathcal Q) > 0$ has a member $G$ such that $Z \oplus G$ is not PA relative to $D$. So $\mathsf{WWKL}$ admits strong PA avoidance.
:::

The class $\mathcal Q$ is not assumed to be effectively closed: the argument never asks a question about $\mathcal Q$. It replaces $\mathcal Q$ by a cylinder in which $\mathcal Q$ has relative measure close to $1$, and asks questions about the measure of the oracles in this cylinder. A positive measure of oracles giving a value, or a small measure of oracles giving each value, can both be exploited inside $\mathcal Q$, because $\mathcal Q$ fills most of the cylinder.

:::proof{of=thm-wwkl-strong-pa}
We build closed classes $\mathcal Q = \mathcal Q_0 \supseteq \mathcal Q_1 \supseteq \cdots$ of positive measure such that for every $e$, $\Phi^{Z \oplus G}_e$ is not $\mathrm{DNC}_2$ relative to $D$ for every $G \in \mathcal Q_{e+1}$. By compactness (:ref{#prop-cantor-compact}), the intersection of the classes $\mathcal Q_e$ is non-empty, and any of its members satisfies the theorem.

Let $\mathcal Q_e$ be given. By the density lemma (:ref{#lem-density}), there is $\sigma$ with $\lambda(\mathcal Q_e \mid \sigma) > \frac{9}{10}$. For $x \in \nat$ and $v < 2$, let $\mathcal U_{x,v}$ be the class of the $Y \in [\sigma]$ with $\Phi^{Z \oplus Y}_e(x)\converge = v$. These classes are $\Sigma^0_1(Z)$, uniformly in $x$ and $v$; the classes $\mathcal U_{x,0}$ and $\mathcal U_{x,1}$ are disjoint. By :ref{#prop-measure-effective} relativized to $Z$, their measures are left-c.e. relative to $Z$, uniformly, so the set
$$
U = \{ (x, v) \in \nat \times \{ 0, 1 \} : \lambda(\mathcal U_{x,v} \mid \sigma) > \tfrac15 \}
$$
is $\Sigma^0_1(Z)$.

1. *$(x, J^D(x)) \in U$ for some $x$ with $J^D(x)$ defined.* Write $v = J^D(x)$. The class $\mathcal U_{x,v}$ is generated by the strings $\tau \succeq \sigma$ with $\Phi^{Z \oplus \tau}_e(x)\converge = v$, and finitely many of them generate a clopen class $\mathcal C \subseteq \mathcal U_{x,v}$ with $\lambda(\mathcal C \mid \sigma) > \frac15$. Let $\mathcal Q_{e+1} = \mathcal Q_e \cap \mathcal C$, a closed class with
$$
\lambda(\mathcal Q_{e+1} \mid \sigma) \geq \lambda(\mathcal Q_e \mid \sigma) + \lambda(\mathcal C \mid \sigma) - 1 > \tfrac{9}{10} + \tfrac15 - 1 = \tfrac{1}{10}.
$$
Every member $G$ satisfies $\Phi^{Z \oplus G}_e(x) = J^D(x)$.
2. *$(x, 0) \notin U$ and $(x, 1) \notin U$ for some $x$.* Let $\mathcal Q_{e+1} = (\mathcal Q_e \cap [\sigma]) \setminus (\mathcal U_{x,0} \cup \mathcal U_{x,1})$, a closed class with $\lambda(\mathcal Q_{e+1} \mid \sigma) > \frac{9}{10} - \frac25 = \frac12$. On every member $G$, $\Phi^{Z \oplus G}_e(x)$ has no value in $\{0, 1\}$.
3. *Otherwise*, for every $x$ some $v < 2$ has $(x, v) \in U$, and no such $v$ equals $J^D(x)$ when it is defined. The selection of :ref{#thm-local-pa-avoidance}, case 3, gives a $Z$-computable function that is $\mathrm{DNC}_2$ relative to $D$, a contradiction.

In the first two cases, $\mathcal Q_{e+1}$ is as required. For the last statement, an instance of $\mathsf{WWKL}$ is a tree $T$ with $\lambda([T]) > 0$, and $[T]$ is a closed class of positive measure, whatever the complexity of $T$.
:::

This is the three-case argument once more, with a question about measures: "$\lambda(\mathcal U_{x,v} \mid \sigma) > \frac15$" plays the role of "$c \mathrel{?{\vdash}} \Phi^{Z \oplus G}_e(x)\converge = v$". It is merging: the two small classes $\mathcal U_{x,0}$ and $\mathcal U_{x,1}$ are removed at once, which is possible because measure is additive. For Jockusch–Soare forcing, the classes removed in case 2 could cover a class of measure $0$ entirely; positive measure is what makes the difference with the class $\mathrm{DNC}_2$.

:::corollary[Kučera]{#cor-pa-null}
The class of the sets $Y$ such that $Z \oplus Y$ is PA relative to $D$ is null. In particular, the class of PA sets is null.
:::

:::proof{of=cor-pa-null}
Let $\mathcal N$ be this class. It is Borel: it is the union over $e$ of the classes of the $Y$ such that $\Phi^{Z \oplus Y}_e$ is total, $\{0, 1\}$-valued and differs from $J^D$ wherever $J^D$ is defined, each of which is a countable intersection of open and closed classes. Suppose that $\lambda(\mathcal N) > 0$. By outer regularity (:ref{#fact-lebesgue-measure}), applied to the complement of $\mathcal N$ with $\varepsilon < \lambda(\mathcal N)$, there is an open class $\mathcal V \supseteq \cs \setminus \mathcal N$ with $\lambda(\mathcal V) \leq 1 - \lambda(\mathcal N) + \varepsilon < 1$. Then $\cs \setminus \mathcal V$ is a closed subclass of $\mathcal N$ of positive measure, and by :ref{#thm-wwkl-strong-pa} it has a member outside $\mathcal N$, a contradiction.
:::

  In case 1 its relative measure exceeds $\frac15$.
- [ ] Because $\mathcal C$ is computable in $Z$.

- [x] That $\frac{9}{10} + \frac15 > 1$, for case 1, and $\frac{9}{10} - 2 \cdot \frac15 > 0$, for case 2; any threshold $t$ with $\frac{1}{10} < t < \frac{9}{20}$ would do.

:::

## Exercises

::::exercise{difficulty=1}
Let $C \not\leq_T Z$. Show, without the forcing of :ref{#thm-wwkl-strong-pa}, that every closed class of positive measure has a member $G$ with $C \not\leq_T Z \oplus G$. Why does the same argument not prove :ref{#thm-wwkl-strong-pa} directly from Sacks's theorem?

:::solution
By Sacks's theorem (:ref{#thm-sacks}) relativized to $Z$, the class of the sets $G$ with $C \leq_T Z \oplus G$ is null; a class of positive measure is not contained in a null class. For PA degrees, the corresponding statement, that the class of the $G$ with $Z \oplus G$ PA relative to $D$ is null, is :ref{#cor-pa-null}, which was deduced from the theorem; Sacks's theorem is about the upper cone of a single set, and the PA sets do not form the upper cone of a single set, since no set is computable in all PA sets except the computable ones (:ref{#cor-pa-basis}).
:::
::::


## Notes and references

That weak weak König's lemma admits strong PA avoidance, by forcing with closed classes of positive measure, is in the chapter *Compactness avoidance* of Patey's monograph, section *Randomness*; the monograph works with the shifted classes $\{ X : \sigma X \in \mathcal Q \}$ where the course uses the relative measure. The fact that the class of PA sets is null is due to Kučera (1985); Downey and Hirschfeldt state it in Section 8.8, before Theorem 8.8.4 (Stephan's theorem that a set that is both PA and Martin-Löf random computes $\emptyset'$, from which Kučera's result also follows).

- A. Kučera, *Measure, Π⁰₁-classes and complete extensions of PA*, in: Recursion Theory Week, Lecture Notes in Mathematics 1141, Springer, 1985, 245–259.
- R. G. Downey and D. R. Hirschfeldt, *Algorithmic Randomness and Complexity*, Springer, 2010, Section 8.8.
- L. Patey, *Lowness and avoidance*, chapter *Compactness avoidance*, section *Randomness*.
