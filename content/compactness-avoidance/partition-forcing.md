The previous lesson defined the Ramsey-type weak König lemma: given a non-empty $\Pi^0_1$ class $\mathcal P$, find an infinite subset of one side of some member of $\mathcal P$ (:ref{#prop-homogeneous-member}). This lesson and the next prove Liu's theorem that such a set can be found that is not PA. The proof is the abstract argument of :ref{#thm-weak-merging-pa}, applied to a notion of forcing in which the reservoir is one side of an unknown member of $\mathcal P$. Its key feature is that no member of a $\Pi^0_1$ class is ever chosen: where the Dzhafarov–Jockusch argument of :ref{#ex-dj-weakly-merging} would choose a partition, the conditions keep all of them and split. This lesson defines the notion of forcing and proves the lemma that makes progress on one requirement; the next lesson builds the homogeneous set.

## Forcing with partitions

The proof of Liu's theorem builds a homogeneous set by a variant of Mathias forcing in which the reservoir is not a set, but one side of an unknown member of a $\Pi^0_1$ class, as in :ref{#prop-homogeneous-member}. Fix sets $D \leq_T Z$ such that $Z$ is not PA relative to $D$. For $k \geq 1$, a function $X : \nat \to k$ is seen as a partition of $\nat$ into the **parts** $X_\nu = X^{-1}(\nu)$, $\nu < k$, and a set $P \subseteq \nat$ as the partition into $P_0 = \overline P$ and $P_1 = P$. Strings are identified with finite sets, as in the course on Ramsey's theorem.

:::definition{#def-partition-forcing}
1. A **condition** is a triple $p = (k, \vec \sigma, \mathcal A)$, with $k \geq 1$, a sequence $\vec \sigma = (\sigma_\nu)_{\nu < k}$ of strings, and a non-empty $\Pi^0_1(Z)$ class $\mathcal A \subseteq k^\nat$ of partitions into $k$ parts. Part $\nu$ of $p$ is **acceptable** if $X_\nu$ is infinite for some $X \in \mathcal A$.
2. A string $\tau$ is **compatible with part $\nu$ of $p$ relative to $X \in \mathcal A$** if $\sigma_\nu \preceq \tau$ and every element of $\tau$ beyond $|\sigma_\nu|$ belongs to $X_\nu$; it is **compatible with part $\nu$ of $p$** if this holds for some $X \in \mathcal A$.
3. A condition $q = (\ell, \vec \tau, \mathcal B)$ **extends** $p$, through a map $f : \ell \to k$, if for every $Y \in \mathcal B$ and every $\mu < \ell$ there is $X \in \mathcal A$ such that $\tau_\mu$ is compatible with part $f(\mu)$ of $p$ relative to $X$, and $Y_\mu \subseteq X_{f(\mu)}$. Part $\mu$ of $q$ **refines** part $f(\mu)$ of $p$.
:::

Each part of a condition is a family of Mathias conditions $(\sigma_\nu, X_\nu)$, one for each $X \in \mathcal A$, and the intended solution is built along one part at each stage, chosen at the end. The class $\mathcal A$ is never replaced by one of its members: this is the difference with the Dzhafarov–Jockusch forcing. Three properties follow from the definition. If $q$ extends $p$ through $f$:

1. a string compatible with part $\mu$ of $q$ is compatible with part $f(\mu)$ of $p$: if $\tau'$ is compatible relative to $Y$, take $X$ for $Y$ and $\mu$; then $\sigma_{f(\mu)} \preceq \tau_\mu \preceq \tau'$, and the new elements lie in $Y_\mu \cup X_{f(\mu)} = X_{f(\mu)}$;
2. if part $\mu$ of $q$ is acceptable, so is part $f(\mu)$ of $p$, since $Y_\mu \subseteq X_{f(\mu)}$;
3. the relation is transitive, with the composite map, by the argument of property 1.

Only two kinds of extensions are used.

- **Part extensions.** Let $\nu < k$, $X \in \mathcal A$, and $\tau$ compatible with part $\nu$ relative to $X$. Replacing $\sigma_\nu$ by $\tau$ and $\mathcal A$ by $\mathcal A_\tau = \{ Y \in \mathcal A : \text{every element of } \tau \text{ beyond } |\sigma_\nu| \text{ is in } Y_\nu \}$ gives a condition extending $p$ through the identity map: $\mathcal A_\tau$ is $\Pi^0_1(Z)$ and contains $X$, and for $Y \in \mathcal A_\tau$ one takes $X = Y$ in the definition.
- **Splitting extensions.** Let $\mathcal B_0, \dots, \mathcal B_{m-1}$ be non-empty $\Pi^0_1(Z)$ subclasses of $\mathcal A$, and $f : k^m \to k$ a map with $f(\mu) \in \{ \mu(0), \dots, \mu(m-1) \}$ for every $\mu \in k^m$. The **refined partition** of $(X_0, \dots, X_{m-1}) \in \mathcal B_0 \times \cdots \times \mathcal B_{m-1}$ is the partition $Y : \nat \to k^m$ with $Y(n) = (X_0(n), \dots, X_{m-1}(n))$, so that $Y_\mu = \bigcap_{s < m} (X_s)_{\mu(s)}$. The refined partitions form a non-empty $\Pi^0_1(Z)$ class $\mathcal B$, and $(k^m, (\sigma_{f(\mu)})_{\mu \in k^m}, \mathcal B)$ extends $p$ through $f$: for $Y$ and $\mu$, take $X = X_s$ with $\mu(s) = f(\mu)$.

A splitting extension does not choose a partition: it keeps all of them, refined, and lets each part of the new condition inherit the stem of one of the parts it comes from. The price is that the number of parts grows from $k$ to $k^m$. This growth is harmless, since the solution follows a single part, chosen at the end by König's lemma.

## Settling a requirement

The requirements are indexed by functionals: the solution $H$ must make $\Phi^{Z \oplus H}_e$ fail to be $\mathrm{DNC}_2$ relative to $D$ for every $e$. Since the solution follows one part, chosen only at the end, every part must be made to satisfy each requirement. Fix a functional $\Phi_e$. Part $\nu$ of $p$ **settles** $e$ if either

- (a) $\Phi^{Z \oplus \sigma_\nu}_e(n)\converge$ for some $n$, with a value not in $\{0, 1\}$ or equal to $J^D(n)$; or
- (b) there is $n$ such that $\Phi^{Z \oplus \tau}_e(n)\diverge$ for every string $\tau$ compatible with part $\nu$ of $p$.

If $q$ extends $p$ through $f$ and part $f(\mu)$ of $p$ settles $e$, then part $\mu$ of $q$ settles $e$: in case (a) because $\sigma_{f(\mu)} \preceq \tau_\mu$, in case (b) by property 1.

:::lemma{#lem-partition-progress}
For every condition $p$ and every $e$, there is an extension of $p$ all of whose parts settle $e$.
:::

:::proof{of=lem-partition-progress}
By induction on the number of parts of $p$ that do not settle $e$. Let $p = (k, \vec \sigma, \mathcal A)$, and let $I$ be the set of these parts, assumed non-empty. Consider the set of finite valuations
$$
U = \{ h : \text{for every } X \in \mathcal A \text{ there are } \nu \in I \text{ and } \tau \text{ compatible with part } \nu \text{ relative to } X \text{ with } \Phi^{Z \oplus \tau}_e \perp h \}.
$$
It is $\Sigma^0_1(Z)$, by compactness. Let $S \subseteq k^{<\nat}$ be a $Z$-computable tree with $[S] = \mathcal A$. For $\xi \in S$, say that $\xi$ **witnesses** $h$ if there are $\nu \in I$ and a string $\tau$ of length at most $|\xi|$ with $\sigma_\nu \preceq \tau$, every element of $\tau$ beyond $|\sigma_\nu|$ in $\xi^{-1}(\nu)$, and $\Phi^{Z \oplus \tau}_e(n)\converge \neq h(n)$ in at most $|\xi|$ steps for some $n \in \mathrm{dom}\, h$. A witness for $\xi$ is a witness for its extensions, so the strings of $S$ that do not witness $h$ form a subtree of $S$. If $h \in U$, this subtree has no path, since every $X \in \mathcal A$ has a prefix that witnesses $h$; so it is finite, by König's lemma. Conversely, if all strings of $S$ of some length $\ell$ witness $h$, then $h \in U$. So $h \in U$ if and only if there is $\ell$ such that every string of $S$ of length $\ell$ witnesses $h$, a $\Sigma^0_1(Z)$ condition.

1. *$U$ contains a correct valuation $h$.* Take any $X \in \mathcal A$, and $\nu \in I$ and $\tau$ as in the definition of $U$. Some $n \in \mathrm{dom}\, h$ has $\Phi^{Z \oplus \tau}_e(n)\converge \neq h(n) = 1 - J^D(n)$: the value is not in $\{0, 1\}$, or equals $J^D(n)$. The part extension replacing $\sigma_\nu$ by $\tau$ makes part $\nu$ settle $e$ by (a), and the other parts are unchanged, so the parts that settled still do. The number of parts that do not settle decreases; apply the induction hypothesis.
2. *There are $k + 1$ pairwise incompatible valuations $h_0, \dots, h_k$ outside $U$.* For $s \leq k$, let $\mathcal B_s$ be the class of $X \in \mathcal A$ such that for every $\nu \in I$ and every $\tau$ compatible with part $\nu$ relative to $X$, $\Phi^{Z \oplus \tau}_e$ is compatible with $h_s$. It is $\Pi^0_1(Z)$, and non-empty since $h_s \notin U$. Every $\mu \in k^{k+1}$ takes $k + 1$ values in $k$, so there are $s < t \leq k$ with $\mu(s) = \mu(t)$; let $f(\mu) = \mu(s)$ for the least such $s$. Let $q$ be the splitting extension given by $\mathcal B_0, \dots, \mathcal B_k$ and $f$. A part $\mu$ of $q$ with $f(\mu) \notin I$ refines a part that settles $e$, so it settles $e$. Let $\mu$ be a part with $f(\mu) = \nu \in I$, and $s < t$ with $\mu(s) = \mu(t) = \nu$. A string $\tau$ compatible with part $\mu$ of $q$, relative to the refined partition $Y$ of $(X_0, \dots, X_k)$, extends $\sigma_\nu$ and has its new elements in $Y_\mu \subseteq (X_s)_\nu \cap (X_t)_\nu$. So $\tau$ is compatible with part $\nu$ of $p$ relative to $X_s \in \mathcal B_s$ and relative to $X_t \in \mathcal B_t$, and $\Phi^{Z \oplus \tau}_e$ is compatible with $h_s$ and with $h_t$. At a number $n$ where $h_s$ and $h_t$ differ, $\Phi^{Z \oplus \tau}_e(n)$ diverges. So part $\mu$ settles $e$ by (b), and all parts of $q$ settle $e$.
3. *Otherwise* $U$ contains no correct valuation, and no $k + 1$ pairwise incompatible valuations lie outside $U$. This contradicts :ref{#lem-liu-valuations} with $W = Z$, since $Z$ computes $D$ and is not PA relative to $D$.
:::

The proof is the one of :ref{#thm-weak-merging-pa}, with $k + 1$ valuations because the condition has $k$ parts, as in :ref{#ex-dj-weakly-merging}. The difference is in case 2: where the Dzhafarov–Jockusch argument picks a member of each class $\mathcal B_s$ in a Scott ideal, the splitting extension keeps all members. This is why the complexity of the reservoirs never enters the argument: the question $U$ is $\Sigma^0_1(Z)$ for every condition, and the only set required not to be PA relative to $D$ is $Z$ itself.

:::quiz[the progress lemma]
In case 2 of the proof of :ref{#lem-partition-progress}, why does the new condition have $k^{k+1}$ parts, and not $k$?

- [x] Because no member of the classes $\mathcal B_s$ is chosen: each part of the new condition records on which part of each of the $k + 1$ partitions an element lies, so that two of these parts coincide for every new part.
- [ ] Because each part of $p$ is split into $k + 1$ pieces.

  Each part of $p$ is split into the parts $\mu$ with $f(\mu) = \nu$, whose number depends on $f$.
- [ ] Because the valuations $h_s$ have $k + 1$ elements in their domains.

  The size of the domains plays no role here; the number of valuations does.

---

Why is the set $U$ $\Sigma^0_1(Z)$, although the members of $\mathcal A$ may be complex?

- [x] Because the definition quantifies over all members of $\mathcal A$, and by compactness the universal quantifier over the $\Pi^0_1(Z)$ class reduces to a quantifier over the strings of $S$ of one length.
- [ ] Because $\mathcal A$ has a $Z$-computable member.

  It need not have one; no member is used.
- [ ] Because $I$ is computable.

  $I$ is a finite set, fixed in the definition; this does not bound the complexity of the quantifier over $\mathcal A$.
:::

## Notes and references

Forcing with partitions follows the chapter *Compactness avoidance* of Patey's monograph, section *Ramsey-type WKL*, which reorganizes the forcing of Liu (2012). Three points differ from the monograph. The extension relation is stated part by part ("for every $Y$ and every part $\mu$ there is $X$"): with a single $X$ for all parts, a splitting extension would not extend its condition, since the part $\mu$ inherits from $X_s$ with $s$ depending on $\mu$; transitivity and the inheritance of compatible strings are then proved. Settling a part is defined by explicit $\Sigma^0_1$ and $\Pi^0_1$ clauses rather than through a forcing relation for parts. The induction of :ref{#lem-partition-progress} runs over all the parts that do not settle $e$, without the monograph's restriction to acceptable parts.

- J. Liu, *RT²₂ does not imply WKL₀*, Journal of Symbolic Logic 77 (2012), 609–620.
- L. Patey, *Lowness and avoidance*, chapter *Compactness avoidance*, section *Ramsey-type WKL*.
