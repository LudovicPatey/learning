The previous lesson defined the Ramsey-type weak König lemma: given a non-empty $\Pi^0_1$ class $\mathcal P$, find an infinite subset of one side of some member of $\mathcal P$ (:ref{#prop-homogeneous-member}). This lesson proves Liu's theorem that such a set can be found that is not PA. The proof is the abstract argument of :ref{#thm-weak-merging-pa}, applied to a notion of forcing in which the reservoir is one side of an unknown member of $\mathcal P$. Its key feature is that no member of a $\Pi^0_1$ class is ever chosen: where the Dzhafarov–Jockusch argument would choose a partition, the conditions keep all of them and split.

## Forcing with partitions

The proof of Liu's theorem builds a homogeneous set by a variant of Mathias forcing in which the reservoir is not a set, but one side of an unknown member of a $\Pi^0_1$ class, as in :ref{#prop-homogeneous-member}. Fix sets $D \leq_T Z$ such that $Z$ is not PA relative to $D$. For $k \geq 1$, a function $X : \nat \to k$ is seen as a partition of $\nat$ into the **parts** $X_\nu = X^{-1}(\nu)$, $\nu < k$, and a set $P \subseteq \nat$ as the partition into $P_0 = \overline P$ and $P_1 = P$. Strings are identified with finite sets, as in the course on Ramsey's theorem.

:::definition{#def-partition-forcing}
1. A **condition** is a triple $p = (k, \vec \sigma, \mathcal A)$, with $k \geq 1$, a sequence $\vec \sigma = (\sigma_\nu)_{\nu < k}$ of strings, and a non-empty $\Pi^0_1(Z)$ class $\mathcal A \subseteq k^\nat$ of partitions into $k$ parts. Part $\nu$ of $p$ is **acceptable** if $X_\nu$ is infinite for some $X \in \mathcal A$.
2. A string $\tau$ is **compatible with part $\nu$ of $p$ relative to $X \in \mathcal A$** if $\sigma_\nu \preceq \tau$ and every element of $\tau$ beyond $|\sigma_\nu|$ belongs to $X_\nu$; it is **compatible with part $\nu$ of $p$** if this holds for some $X \in \mathcal A$.
3. A condition $q = (\ell, \vec \tau, \mathcal B)$ **extends** $p$, through a map $f : \ell \to k$, if for every $Y \in \mathcal B$ and every $\mu < \ell$ there is $X \in \mathcal A$ such that $\tau_\mu$ is compatible with part $f(\mu)$ of $p$ relative to $X$, and $Y_\mu \subseteq X_{f(\mu)}$. Part $\mu$ of $q$ **refines** part $f(\mu)$ of $p$.
:::

Each part of a condition is a family of Mathias conditions $(\sigma_\nu, X_\nu)$, one for each $X \in \mathcal A$, and the intended solution is built along one part at each stage, chosen at the end. The class $\mathcal A$ is never replaced by one of its members: this is the difference with the Dzhafarov–Jockusch forcing. Three properties follow from the definition. If $q$ extends $p$ through $f$:

1. a string compatible with part $\mu$ of $q$ is compatible with part $f(\mu)$ of $p$: if $\tau'$ is compatible relative to $Y$, take $X$ for $Y$ and $\mu$; then $\sigma_{f(\mu)} \preceq \tau_\mu \preceq \tau'$, and the new elements lie in $Y_\mu \cup X_{f(\mu)} = X_{f(\mu)}$;
2. if part $\mu$ of $q$ is acceptable, so is part $f(\mu)$ of $p$, since $Y_\mu \subseteq X_{f(\mu)}$;
3. the relation is transitive, with the composite map, by the argument of property 1.

Only two kinds of extensions are used.

- **Part extensions.** Let $\nu < k$, $X \in \mathcal A$, and $\tau$ compatible with part $\nu$ relative to $X$. Replacing $\sigma_\nu$ by $\tau$ and $\mathcal A$ by $\mathcal A_\tau = \{ Y \in \mathcal A : \text{every element of } \tau \text{ beyond } |\sigma_\nu| \text{ is in } Y_\nu \}$ gives a condition extending $p$ through the identity map: $\mathcal A_\tau$ is $\Pi^0_1(Z)$ and contains $X$, and for $Y \in \mathcal A_\tau$ one takes $X = Y$ in the definition.
- **Splitting extensions.** Let $\mathcal B_0, \dots, \mathcal B_{m-1}$ be non-empty $\Pi^0_1(Z)$ subclasses of $\mathcal A$, and $f : k^m \to k$ a map with $f(\mu) \in \{ \mu(0), \dots, \mu(m-1) \}$ for every $\mu \in k^m$. The **refined partition** of $(X_0, \dots, X_{m-1}) \in \mathcal B_0 \times \cdots \times \mathcal B_{m-1}$ is the partition $Y : \nat \to k^m$ with $Y(n) = (X_0(n), \dots, X_{m-1}(n))$, so that $Y_\mu = \bigcap_{s < m} (X_s)_{\mu(s)}$. The refined partitions form a non-empty $\Pi^0_1(Z)$ class $\mathcal B$, and $(k^m, (\sigma_{f(\mu)})_{\mu \in k^m}, \mathcal B)$ extends $p$ through $f$: for $Y$ and $\mu$, take $X = X_s$ with $\mu(s) = f(\mu)$.

A splitting extension does not choose a partition: it keeps all of them, refined, and lets each part of the new condition inherit the stem of one of the parts it comes from. The price is that the number of parts grows from $k$ to $k^m$. This growth is harmless, since the solution follows a single part, chosen at the end by König's lemma.

## Liu's theorem

Fix a functional $\Phi_e$. Part $\nu$ of $p$ **settles** $e$ if either

- (a) $\Phi^{Z \oplus \sigma_\nu}_e(n)\converge$ for some $n$, with a value not in $\{0, 1\}$ or equal to $J^D(n)$; or
- (b) there is $n$ such that $\Phi^{Z \oplus \tau}_e(n)\diverge$ for every string $\tau$ compatible with part $\nu$ of $p$.

If $q$ extends $p$ through $f$ and part $f(\mu)$ of $p$ settles $e$, then part $\mu$ of $q$ settles $e$: in case (a) because $\sigma_{f(\mu)} \preceq \tau_\mu$, in case (b) by property 1.

:::lemma{#lem-partition-progress}
For every condition $p$ and every $e$, there is an extension of $p$ all of whose parts settle $e$.
:::

:::proof{of=lem-partition-progress}
By induction on the number of parts of $p$ that do not settle $e$. Let $p = (k, \vec \sigma, \mathcal A)$, and let $I$ be the set of these parts, assumed non-empty. Consider the set of finite valuations
$$
U = \{ h : \text{for every } X \in \mathcal A \text{ there are } \nu \in I \text{ and } \tau \text{ compatible with part } \nu \text{ relative to } X \text{ with } \Phi^{Z \oplus \tau}_e \perp h \}.
$$
It is $\Sigma^0_1(Z)$, by compactness. Let $S \subseteq k^{<\nat}$ be a $Z$-computable tree with $[S] = \mathcal A$. For $\xi \in S$, say that $\xi$ **witnesses** $h$ if there are $\nu \in I$ and a string $\tau$ of length at most $|\xi|$ with $\sigma_\nu \preceq \tau$, every element of $\tau$ beyond $|\sigma_\nu|$ in $\xi^{-1}(\nu)$, and $\Phi^{Z \oplus \tau}_e(n)\converge \neq h(n)$ in at most $|\xi|$ steps for some $n \in \mathrm{dom}\, h$. A witness for $\xi$ is a witness for its extensions, so the strings of $S$ that do not witness $h$ form a subtree of $S$. If $h \in U$, this subtree has no path, since every $X \in \mathcal A$ has a prefix that witnesses $h$; so it is finite, by König's lemma. Conversely, if all strings of $S$ of some length $\ell$ witness $h$, then $h \in U$. So $h \in U$ if and only if there is $\ell$ such that every string of $S$ of length $\ell$ witnesses $h$, a $\Sigma^0_1(Z)$ condition.

1. *$U$ contains a correct valuation $h$.* Take any $X \in \mathcal A$, and $\nu \in I$ and $\tau$ as in the definition of $U$. Some $n \in \mathrm{dom}\, h$ has $\Phi^{Z \oplus \tau}_e(n)\converge \neq h(n) = 1 - J^D(n)$: the value is not in $\{0, 1\}$, or equals $J^D(n)$. The part extension replacing $\sigma_\nu$ by $\tau$ makes part $\nu$ settle $e$ by (a), and the other parts are unchanged, so the parts that settled still do. The number of parts that do not settle decreases; apply the induction hypothesis.
2. *There are $k + 1$ pairwise incompatible valuations $h_0, \dots, h_k$ outside $U$.* For $s \leq k$, let $\mathcal B_s$ be the class of $X \in \mathcal A$ such that for every $\nu \in I$ and every $\tau$ compatible with part $\nu$ relative to $X$, $\Phi^{Z \oplus \tau}_e$ is compatible with $h_s$. It is $\Pi^0_1(Z)$, and non-empty since $h_s \notin U$. Every $\mu \in k^{k+1}$ takes $k + 1$ values in $k$, so there are $s < t \leq k$ with $\mu(s) = \mu(t)$; let $f(\mu) = \mu(s)$ for the least such $s$. Let $q$ be the splitting extension given by $\mathcal B_0, \dots, \mathcal B_k$ and $f$. A part $\mu$ of $q$ with $f(\mu) \notin I$ refines a part that settles $e$, so it settles $e$. Let $\mu$ be a part with $f(\mu) = \nu \in I$, and $s < t$ with $\mu(s) = \mu(t) = \nu$. A string $\tau$ compatible with part $\mu$ of $q$, relative to the refined partition $Y$ of $(X_0, \dots, X_k)$, extends $\sigma_\nu$ and has its new elements in $Y_\mu \subseteq (X_s)_\nu \cap (X_t)_\nu$. So $\tau$ is compatible with part $\nu$ of $p$ relative to $X_s \in \mathcal B_s$ and relative to $X_t \in \mathcal B_t$, and $\Phi^{Z \oplus \tau}_e$ is compatible with $h_s$ and with $h_t$. At a number $n$ where $h_s$ and $h_t$ differ, $\Phi^{Z \oplus \tau}_e(n)$ diverges. So part $\mu$ settles $e$ by (b), and all parts of $q$ settle $e$.
3. *Otherwise* $U$ contains no correct valuation, and no $k + 1$ pairwise incompatible valuations lie outside $U$. This contradicts :ref{#lem-liu-valuations} with $W = Z$, since $Z$ computes $D$ and is not PA relative to $D$.
:::

The proof is the one of :ref{#thm-weak-merging-pa}, with $k + 1$ valuations because the condition has $k$ parts, as in :ref{#ex-dj-weakly-merging}. The difference is in case 2: where the Dzhafarov–Jockusch argument picks a member of each class $\mathcal B_s$ in a Scott ideal, the splitting extension keeps all members. This is why the complexity of the reservoirs never enters the argument: the question $U$ is $\Sigma^0_1(Z)$ for every condition, and the only set required not to be PA relative to $D$ is $Z$ itself.

In case 2 of the proof of :ref{#lem-partition-progress}, why does the new condition have $k^{k+1}$ parts, and not $k$?

- [x] Because no member of the classes $\mathcal B_s$ is chosen: each part of the new condition records on which part of each of the $k + 1$ partitions an element lies, so that two of these parts coincide for every new part.
- [ ] Because each part of $p$ is split into $k + 1$ pieces.

  Each part of $p$ is split into the parts $\mu$ with $f(\mu) = \nu$, whose number depends on $f$.
- [ ] Because the valuations $h_s$ have $k + 1$ elements in their domains.

  The size of the domains plays no role here; the number of valuations does.

---

Why is the set $U$ $\Sigma^0_1(Z)$, although the members of $\mathcal A$ may be complex?

- [x] Because the definition quantifies over all members of $\mathcal A$, and by compactness the universal quantifier over the $\Pi^0_1(Z)$ class reduces to a quantifier over the strings of $S$ of one length.
- [ ] Because $\mathcal A$ has a $Z$-computable member.

- [ ] Because $I$ is computable.

:::theorem[Liu]{#thm-rwkl-pa-avoidance}
Let $D \leq_T Z$ be sets such that $Z$ is not PA relative to $D$. Every non-empty $\Pi^0_1(Z)$ class $\mathcal P \subseteq \cs$ has an infinite homogeneous set $H$, that is, $H \subseteq P^{-1}(i)$ for some $P \in \mathcal P$ and $i < 2$, such that $Z \oplus H$ is not PA relative to $D$. In particular, $\mathsf{RWKL}$ admits PA avoidance.
:::

:::proof{of=thm-rwkl-pa-avoidance}
Let $p_0 = (2, (\varepsilon, \varepsilon), \mathcal P)$, where a set $P \in \mathcal P$ is seen as the partition $(P_0, P_1)$. Build a sequence of conditions $p_0 \geq p_1 \geq \cdots$, with maps $f_s$ through which $p_{s+1}$ extends $p_s$, $p_s = (k_s, \vec \sigma^s, \mathcal A_s)$, as follows.

- At the steps $2e + 1$, apply :ref{#lem-partition-progress} to $e$.
- At the steps $2n + 2$, consider the parts of the current condition one after the other. If part $\nu$ is acceptable at its turn, choose $X$ in the current class with $X_\nu$ infinite and $x \in X_\nu$ with $x > n$ and $x \geq |\sigma_\nu|$, and apply the part extension with $\tau = \sigma_\nu \cup \{ x \}$; since $X$ remains in the class, part $\nu$ remains acceptable. A part that is not acceptable at its turn remains not acceptable afterwards, by property 2. After the pass, every acceptable part has an element larger than $n$ in its stem.

*The path.* Every condition has an acceptable part, since a partition of $\nat$ into finitely many parts has an infinite part. Consider the tree whose nodes at level $s$ are the acceptable parts of $p_s$, the parent of $\mu$ being $f_s(\mu)$, which is acceptable by property 2. It is infinite and finitely branching, so by König's lemma (:ref{#lem-konig}) it has an infinite branch $(\nu_s)$, with $f_s(\nu_{s+1}) = \nu_s$. The stems $\sigma^s_{\nu_s}$ form an increasing sequence of strings, by the definition of extension; let $H$ be their union.

*$H$ is infinite.* After step $2n + 2$, the part $\nu_s$ is acceptable, so its stem has an element larger than $n$.

*$H$ is homogeneous.* By transitivity, $p_s$ extends $p_0$ through the composite map, which sends $\nu_s$ to a color $c < 2$ independent of $s$. Applying the definition of extension to a member of $\mathcal A_s$ gives $P \in \mathcal P$ such that $\sigma^s_{\nu_s}$ is compatible with part $c$ of $p_0$ relative to $P$, that is, $\sigma^s_{\nu_s} \subseteq P^{-1}(c)$. Every finite subset of $H$ is contained in some $\sigma^s_{\nu_s}$, so it is contained in $P^{-1}(c)$ for some $P \in \mathcal P$, and it is homogeneous with color $c$. By :ref{#prop-homogeneous-member}, $H \subseteq P^{-1}(c)$ for some $P \in \mathcal P$.

*$Z \oplus H$ is not PA relative to $D$.* Fix $e$, and let $s$ be the step $2e + 1$, after which every part of $p_s$ settles $e$, in particular part $\nu_s$. In case (a), $H$ extends $\sigma^s_{\nu_s}$, so $\Phi^{Z \oplus H}_e(n)$ converges to a value not in $\{0, 1\}$ or equal to $J^D(n)$. In case (b), every prefix $\tau$ of $H$ with $|\tau| \geq |\sigma^s_{\nu_s}|$ is a prefix of some $\sigma^t_{\nu_t}$ with $t \geq s$, which is compatible with part $\nu_s$ of $p_s$ by property 1; hence $\tau$ is compatible too, and $\Phi^{Z \oplus \tau}_e(n)$ diverges. By the use principle, $\Phi^{Z \oplus H}_e(n)$ diverges. In both cases $\Phi^{Z \oplus H}_e$ is not $\mathrm{DNC}_2$ relative to $D$.

For the last statement, an instance of $\mathsf{RWKL}$ computable in $Z$ is a $Z$-computable tree $T$, and $[T]$ is a non-empty $\Pi^0_1(Z)$ class.
:::

The construction is not effective: acceptability is not decidable, and the branch is chosen by König's lemma at the end. Only the existence of the solution matters. The proof uses compactness twice, in a way that does not produce PA sets: in the definition of $U$, to bring a question about all members of a class down to $\Sigma^0_1(Z)$, and at the end, to choose the branch of parts and a path through $\mathcal P$, outside of any computation.

:::quiz[Liu's theorem]
Why is the solution $H$ built along a branch of acceptable parts chosen at the end, rather than along one part fixed at the start?

- [x] Because splitting extensions replace each part by several parts, and which of them has infinitely many elements depends on members of the classes that are never chosen; König's lemma finds a branch along which all parts are acceptable.
- [ ] Because the parts are not ordered.

  The parts are indexed by numbers; the issue is which ones remain infinite.
- [ ] Because $H$ must be computable in $Z$.

  $H$ is not computable in $Z$ in general; the construction is not effective.

---

In the proof that $H$ is homogeneous, why is the color $c$ the same for all $s$?

- [x] Because $f_s(\nu_{s+1}) = \nu_s$, so the composite map to the parts of $p_0$ sends all the parts $\nu_s$ of the branch to the same part of $p_0$.
- [ ] Because $\mathcal P$ has a single member.

  $\mathcal P$ may have many members; different $s$ may give different members $P$.
- [ ] Because $H$ is infinite.

  Infinity is proved separately and does not determine the color.
:::

## Exercises

::::exercise{#exr-rwkl-subset difficulty=2}
Using :ref{#thm-rwkl-pa-avoidance} and :ref{#prop-homogeneous-member}, show that for all $D \leq_T Z$ with $Z$ not PA relative to $D$ and every non-empty $\Pi^0_1(Z)$ class $\mathcal P \subseteq \cs$ and infinite set $X \leq_T Z$, there are $P \in \mathcal P$, $i < 2$ and an infinite set $H \subseteq X \cap P^{-1}(i)$ such that $Z \oplus H$ is not PA relative to $D$.

:::solution
Let $p_X$ be the increasing enumeration of $X$, computable in $Z$, and $\mathcal Q = \{ P \circ p_X : P \in \mathcal P \}$, where $(P \circ p_X)(k) = P(p_X(k))$. It is non-empty, and it is a $\Pi^0_1(Z)$ class. Let $S$ be a $Z$-computable tree with $[S] = \mathcal P$. If $Q \notin \mathcal Q$, the strings of $S$ that agree with $Q$ at the positions $p_X(j)$ below their length form a subtree of $S$ without path, hence finite, so some level $p_X(n) + 1$ of $S$ contains no string agreeing with $Q \uh (n + 1)$ at the positions $p_X(0), \dots, p_X(n)$; conversely, this condition excludes $Q$ from $\mathcal Q$. It is $\Sigma^0_1(Z)$, so the complement of $\mathcal Q$ is $\Sigma^0_1(Z)$. By the theorem, $\mathcal Q$ has an infinite homogeneous set $K$, with $Z \oplus K$ not PA relative to $D$, and $K \subseteq (P \circ p_X)^{-1}(i)$ for some $P \in \mathcal P$. Then $H = p_X[K]$ is an infinite subset of $X \cap P^{-1}(i)$, computable in $Z \oplus K$, so $Z \oplus H$ is not PA relative to $D$.
:::
::::

::::exercise{difficulty=2}
Show that the forcing of this lesson also gives cone avoidance for $\mathsf{RWKL}$ directly: if $C \not\leq_T Z$, every non-empty $\Pi^0_1(Z)$ class has an infinite homogeneous set $H$ with $C \not\leq_T Z \oplus H$. Which case of the proof becomes simpler?

:::solution
Let part $\nu$ settle $e$ if $\Phi^{Z \oplus \sigma_\nu}_e(x)\converge \neq C(x)$ for some $x$, or if there is $x$ such that no string $\tau$ compatible with part $\nu$ has $\Phi^{Z \oplus \tau}_e(x)\converge = C(x)$. Define $U$ as the set of pairs $(x, v)$ such that for every $X \in \mathcal A$, some $\nu \in I$ and $\tau$ compatible with part $\nu$ relative to $X$ have $\Phi^{Z \oplus \tau}_e(x)\converge = v$; it is $\Sigma^0_1(Z)$ by the same compactness argument. If $(x, 1 - C(x)) \in U$, a part extension settles a part. If $(x, C(x)) \notin U$, the class of $X \in \mathcal A$ for which no $\tau$ compatible with a part $\nu \in I$ relative to $X$ gives $C(x)$ is non-empty, and replacing $\mathcal A$ by it settles all parts of $I$ at once; no splitting is needed, since a single negative answer suffices. Otherwise $C \leq_T Z$. The rest of the proof is unchanged. This also follows from :ref{#thm-rwkl-pa-avoidance} and :ref{#thm-pa-implies-cone}.
:::
::::

## Notes and references

The theorem that every non-empty $\Pi^0_1$ class has a homogeneous set that is not PA is implicit in Liu's proof (2012) that Ramsey's theorem for pairs does not imply weak König's lemma; Flood (2012) used Liu's argument to show that $\mathsf{RWKL}$ does not imply $\mathsf{WKL}_0$. The proof given here, with partitions that split along extensions, follows the chapter *Compactness avoidance* of Patey's monograph, section *Ramsey-type WKL*; the definition of extension is stated part by part, which makes the transitivity and the inheritance of compatible strings explicit, and the infinity of the solution is obtained by a separate density step. The monograph notes that a shorter proof exists, using that the class $\mathrm{DNC}_2$ is $\Pi^0_1$ (Monin and Patey, 2019), but that the proof given here is the one whose combinatorics extend to the avoidance of arbitrary closed classes (:ref{to="compactness-avoidance/measure-and-closed-classes"}).

- J. Liu, *RT²₂ does not imply WKL₀*, Journal of Symbolic Logic 77 (2012), 609–620.
- S. Flood, *Reverse mathematics and a Ramsey-type König's lemma*, Journal of Symbolic Logic 77 (2012), 1272–1280.
- B. Monin and L. Patey, *Pigeons do not jump high*, Advances in Mathematics 352 (2019), 1066–1095.
- L. Patey, *Lowness and avoidance*, chapter *Compactness avoidance*, section *Ramsey-type WKL*.
