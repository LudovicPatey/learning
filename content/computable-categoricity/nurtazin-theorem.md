The generic copy used to characterize relative categoricity is not computable, so it says nothing about the computable copies. To compare plain and relative categoricity, the copy must be built effectively. This lesson does it in the setting where the construction is cleanest, that of decidable copies, and proves Nurtazin's theorem: a decidable structure is computably categorical for decidable copies if and only if the types of its tuples are generated by a c.e. list of formulas over finitely many parameters. The construction is a finite injury argument in which a requirement does not diagonalize at a moment of its choosing, but watches the other requirements and profits from their actions. The same proof, with atomic diagrams in place of elementary ones, gives Goncharov's theorem for computable copies of structures whose two-quantifier diagram is decidable.

## The theorem

Recall the notions of the previous lesson: a decidable presentation, a structure effectively atomic over a tuple, and categoricity for decidable copies (:ref{#def-effectively-atomic}).

:::theorem[Nurtazin]{#thm-nurtazin}
A decidable structure $\mathcal A$ is computably categorical for decidable copies if and only if it is effectively atomic over some tuple.
:::

**Intuition.** One direction is :ref{#prop-atomic-decidable-cc}. For the other, assume that $\mathcal A$ is computably categorical for decidable copies. We build a decidable copy $\mathcal B$ and try to make every computable function $\Phi_e$ fail to be an isomorphism from $\mathcal B$ onto $\mathcal A$; some $\Phi_e$ is an isomorphism, and its failure to be defeated will produce the generating formulas. Since $\mathcal B$ must be decidable, it is the pull-back of an enumeration $g$ of $\mathcal A$ obtained as a limit of finite tuples $g_s$, which may change, but whose elementary diagrams only grow: whatever $g_s$ has said about the first-order properties of its positions is never retracted.

The requirement for $\Phi_e$ works as follows. Its tuple $\bar p$ is the part of $g$ fixed by the requirements of higher priority. Whenever $\Phi_e$ converges on a tuple of positions $\bar n$, the requirement records what $g$ has said so far about these positions: a formula $\chi_{\bar n}(\bar p, \bar x)$ that the tuple at positions $\bar n$ satisfies, and will keep satisfying. It bets that $\chi_{\bar n}$ generates the type of that tuple. If the bet is right for every $\bar n$, these formulas witness effective atomicity. If a requirement of lower priority wants to say something new about the positions $\bar n$ that does not follow from $\chi_{\bar n}$, the bet may be wrong, and this is an opportunity: two tuples both satisfy $\chi_{\bar n}$ and differ on the new statement; one of them differs from the image $\Phi_e(\bar n)$, and moving $g$ so that it puts this tuple at positions $\bar n$ defeats $\Phi_e$.

:::proof{of=thm-nurtazin}
The first direction is :ref{#prop-atomic-decidable-cc}. For the second, fix an effective list $(\theta_i)$ of the first-order formulas in the variables $x_0, x_1, \ldots$, in which $\theta_i$ mentions only variables $x_j$ with $j < i$. For a tuple $\bar q$ of length $n$, let $E(\bar q) \in 2^n$ record the truth in $\mathcal A$ of $\theta_0, \ldots, \theta_{n-1}$ when $x_j$ names $q_j$; it is computable from $\bar q$, as $\mathcal A$ is decidable. For a string $\sigma$ of length $n$, let $\varepsilon_\sigma(y_0, \ldots, y_{n-1})$ be the conjunction of the formulas $\theta_i$ with $\sigma(i) = 1$, the negations of those with $\sigma(i) = 0$, and the inequalities $y_i \neq y_j$. For a tuple $\bar p$ of length $k \leq n$ and a tuple $\bar n$ of numbers below $n$, let
$$
\psi_{\bar n, \sigma}(\bar p, \bar x) \equiv \exists y_k \ldots \exists y_{n-1}\, \big( \varepsilon_\sigma(\bar p, y_k, \ldots, y_{n-1}) \wedge \bar x = (\bar p\, y_k \ldots y_{n-1})_{\bar n} \big),
$$
where $(\bar y)_{\bar n}$ is the tuple of the entries of $\bar y$ at the positions $\bar n$. We use two observations. (i) If $\bar q \supseteq \bar p$ and $E(\bar q)$ extends $\sigma$, then $\psi_{\bar n, \sigma}(\bar p, \bar q_{\bar n})$ holds, witnessed by the first $n$ entries of $\bar q$. (ii) The formula $\psi_{\bar n, \sigma}$ depends only on $\bar p$, $\bar n$ and $\sigma$; two tuples extending $\bar p$ with the same string $E$ give the same formula.

*Requirements.* $P_e$: the $e$-th element of $\mathcal A$ is in the range of $g$. $R_e$: if $\Phi_e$ is an isomorphism from $\mathcal B$ onto $\mathcal A$, then $\mathcal A$ is effectively atomic over some tuple. The priority order is $P_0, R_0, P_1, R_1, \ldots$. A requirement is active or inactive; at each stage, the requirement of highest priority that is inactive is activated, provided that the requirement just above it is active and, if that one is a $P$, has acted. When $R_e$ is activated at stage $s$, it receives the base length $\ell_e = |g_s|$, its base $\bar p_e = g_s \restriction \ell_e$, and an empty set $S_e$ of bets. Activating $P_e$ sets it to "waiting".

*Stage $s + 1$.* Start from $g_s$.
1. *Bets.* For each active $R_e$ that has not acted, and each tuple $\bar n$ of numbers below $|g_s|$ not yet considered such that $\Phi_{e,s}$ is defined at $0, \ldots, \ell_e - 1$ and at the entries of $\bar n$, add the bet $(\bar n, E(g_s))$ to $S_e$. The formula of the bet is $\chi_{\bar n} = \psi_{\bar n, E(g_s)}(\bar p_e, \bar x)$.
2. *Action.* Let $P_e$ be the waiting requirement of highest priority, if any, and $a$ the $e$-th element of $\mathcal A$. If $a$ is in the range of $g_s$, $P_e$ has acted, and $g_{s+1} = g_s$. Otherwise let $\bar h = g_s a$. For every $j < e$ such that $R_j$ is active and has not acted, and every bet $(\bar n, \sigma) \in S_j$, check whether
$$
\mathcal A \models \forall \bar x\, \big( \psi_{\bar n, \sigma}(\bar p_j, \bar x) \to \psi_{\bar n, E(\bar h)}(\bar p_j, \bar x) \big),
$$
which is decidable since $\mathcal A$ is decidable. If all checks pass, let $g_{s+1} = \bar h$; $P_e$ has acted. If some check fails, let $j$ be least such that a check of $R_j$ fails, for the bet $(\bar n, \sigma)$; $R_j$ acts as follows.
3. *Action of $R_j$.* The tuple $\bar c_1 = \bar h_{\bar n}$ satisfies $\psi_{\bar n, \sigma}(\bar p_j, \bar x)$ and $\chi(\bar p_j, \bar x) = \psi_{\bar n, E(\bar h)}(\bar p_j, \bar x)$, by (i), since $E(\bar h)$ extends $E(g_s)$, which extends $\sigma$. Since the check failed, search for a tuple $\bar c_2$ satisfying $\psi_{\bar n, \sigma}(\bar p_j, \bar x)$ and not $\chi(\bar p_j, \bar x)$. Let $\bar u = \Phi_j(0, \ldots, \ell_j - 1)$ and $\bar v = \Phi_j(\bar n)$, which are defined since the bet was placed. Of $\bar c_1$ and $\bar c_2$, one, call it $\bar c$, satisfies $\chi(\bar p_j, \bar c) \iff \neg \chi(\bar u, \bar v)$, where $\chi(\bar u, \bar v)$ is the same formula with $\bar u$ in place of the parameters; this is decided in $\mathcal A$. By the invariant below, $\bar c$ satisfies $\psi_{\bar n, E(g_s)}(\bar p_j, \bar x)$; search for a witness: a tuple $\bar q \supseteq \bar p_j$ of length $|g_s|$ with $E(\bar q) = E(g_s)$ and $\bar q_{\bar n} = \bar c$. Let $g_{s+1} = \bar q$. Mark $R_j$ as having acted, and make inactive all requirements of lower priority than $R_j$.

Every search terminates, and the construction is computable.

*The invariant.* At every stage, for every active $R_j$ that has not acted and every bet $(\bar n, \sigma) \in S_j$, $\mathcal A \models \forall \bar x\, ( \psi_{\bar n, \sigma}(\bar p_j, \bar x) \to \psi_{\bar n, E(g_s)}(\bar p_j, \bar x))$. It holds when the bet is placed, with $\sigma = E(g_s)$. When some $P_e$ acts, the active requirements $R_j$ are those with $j < e$, since $R_e$ and the requirements below it are inactive while $P_e$ waits, and the checks are exactly the invariant for $g_{s+1}$. When $R_j$ acts, $g_{s+1}$ extends $\bar p_j$, hence $\bar p_i$ for $i < j$, and $E(g_{s+1}) = E(g_s)$, so by (ii) the formulas $\psi_{\bar n, E(g_{s+1})}(\bar p_i, \bar x)$ are unchanged for the remaining requirements $R_i$, $i < j$.

*The copy is decidable.* The strings $E(g_s)$ are nested: a $P$-action extends $g_s$ by one element, which does not change the truth of $\theta_i$ for $i < |g_s|$, and an $R$-action keeps $E$. Each position of $g$ changes finitely often (below), so $g = \lim_s g_s$ is an injective map from $\nat$ to $A$, onto by the requirements $P_e$, and the elementary diagram of $\mathcal B = g^{-1}(\mathcal A)$ is $\bigcup_s E(g_s)$, which is computable: for $s$ large, $g_s$ agrees with $g$ on the positions mentioned by $\theta_i$, and $E(g_s)(i)$ no longer changes.

*Finite injury.* Each $R_j$ acts at most once after each activation, and when it acts it changes only positions at or above $\ell_j$. By induction on priority: once the requirements above $P_e$ no longer act, $P_e$ is no longer made inactive; each of its failed attempts makes some $R_j$ with $j < e$ act, which happens finitely often, so $P_e$ eventually acts; then $R_e$ is activated for the last time, with a base that never changes again, and acts at most once more. The positions below $\ell_{e+1}$ are then fixed, since later actions concern positions at or above the base lengths of lower requirements.

*$R_e$ is satisfied.* Consider $R_e$ after its last activation, with base $\bar p = \bar p_e$ and $\bar u = \Phi_e(0, \ldots, \ell_e - 1)$. If $R_e$ acts afterwards, putting $\bar c$ at positions $\bar n$, then $g$ extends the tuple $\bar q$ chosen then, since the lower requirements only act at higher positions. If $\Phi_e$ were an isomorphism from $\mathcal B$ onto $\mathcal A$, then $\Phi_e \circ g^{-1}$ would be an automorphism of $\mathcal A$ mapping $\bar p \bar c$, the values of $g$ at the positions $0, \ldots, \ell_e - 1$ and $\bar n$, to $\bar u \bar v$; but $\chi(\bar p, \bar c)$ and $\chi(\bar u, \bar v)$ have different truth values. So $\Phi_e$ is not an isomorphism.

If $R_e$ never acts afterwards and $\Phi_e$ is an isomorphism, let $\Phi$ be the set of formulas $\chi_{\bar n}$ of the bets placed after the last activation; it is c.e. Every tuple $\bar b$ of $\mathcal A$ is $g_{\bar n}$ for some $\bar n$, and since $\Phi_e$ is total, a bet $(\bar n, \sigma)$ is placed at some stage $s_1$. For $s \geq s_1$, $g_s \supseteq \bar p$ and $E(g_s)$ extends $\sigma$, so $\chi_{\bar n}((g_s)_{\bar n})$ holds by (i); for $s$ large, $(g_s)_{\bar n} = \bar b$, so $\bar b$ satisfies $\chi_{\bar n}$. Let $\theta(\bar p, \bar x)$ be true of $\bar b$. The formula obtained from $\theta$ by putting $x_0, \ldots, x_{\ell_e - 1}$ for $\bar p$ and the variables at positions $\bar n$ for $\bar x$ is some $\theta_i$, true of $g$; so $E(g_s)(i) = 1$ for $s$ large, and then $\psi_{\bar n, E(g_s)}(\bar p, \bar x)$ implies $\theta(\bar p, \bar x)$ in $\mathcal A$. By the invariant, $\chi_{\bar n}$ implies $\psi_{\bar n, E(g_s)}(\bar p, \bar x)$, hence $\theta$. So $\chi_{\bar n}$ generates the type of $\bar b$ over $\bar p$, and $\Phi$ witnesses that $\mathcal A$ is effectively atomic over $\bar p$.

Finally, $\mathcal B$ is a decidable copy of $\mathcal A$; since $\mathcal A$ is computably categorical for decidable copies, some $\Phi_e$ is a computable isomorphism from $\mathcal B$ onto $\mathcal A$, and the previous paragraph applies to it.
:::

:::quiz[Nurtazin's theorem]
Why can the proof not use the generic copy of :ref{#thm-rcc-scott}?

- [x] The hypothesis only concerns decidable copies, so the copy built must be decidable, hence produced by a computable construction; the generic copy is not even computable.
- [ ] Because a generic copy would not be isomorphic to $\mathcal A$.

  A generic enumeration of $\mathcal A$ gives a copy of $\mathcal A$; the problem is its complexity.
- [ ] Because decidable structures have no generic copies.

  Every structure has generic copies; they are not decidable.

---

What is the purpose of the check before a $P$-requirement extends $g$?

- [x] To keep the bets of the higher requirements valid: as long as each formula $\chi_{\bar n}$ implies everything said later about the positions $\bar n$, it generates the type in the limit; a failed check is an opportunity to defeat $\Phi_j$.
- [ ] To make sure that the new element is not already in the range.

  That is tested before the check.
- [ ] To keep the copy decidable.

  Decidability comes from the strings $E(g_s)$ being nested, which holds whether or not the checks pass.

---

When $R_j$ acts, why does $g_{s+1}$ have the same string $E$ as $g_s$, rather than that of $\bar h$?

- [x] The elementary diagram of $\mathcal B$ is the union of the strings $E(g_s)$, so they must be nested; moving the tuple at positions $\bar n$ must not retract anything already said, and the witness $\bar q$ of $\psi_{\bar n, E(g_s)}$ guarantees it.
- [ ] Because $\bar h$ might not extend $\bar p_j$.

  $\bar h$ extends $g_s$, hence $\bar p_j$; what matters is the elementary diagram.
- [ ] To avoid cancelling the requirements of lower priority.

  They are cancelled anyway.
:::

The proof is a **finite injury** argument of a new kind: a requirement does not diagonalize at a chosen moment, but watches the actions of the lower requirements and either profits from one of them or certifies, in the limit, a definability property. The two outcomes, defeat of $\Phi_e$ and effective atomicity, are those of the generic construction, but now obtained effectively.

## Computable copies: Goncharov's theorems

For computable copies, the same construction works if the structure is effective enough for the checks to be decidable. A formula is **$\forall\exists$** if it has the form $\forall \bar x\, \exists \bar y\, \varphi$ with $\varphi$ quantifier-free, and a computable structure is **$\forall\exists$-decidable**, or **2-decidable**, if the truth of $\forall\exists$ formulas about its tuples is decidable.

:::theorem[Goncharov]{#thm-goncharov-2-decidable}
Let $\mathcal A$ be a computable structure that is $\forall\exists$-decidable. Then $\mathcal A$ is computably categorical if and only if it is relatively computably categorical.
:::

We admit the theorem; its proof is that of :ref{#thm-nurtazin}, with atomic diagrams in place of elementary diagrams, so that the copy built is computable rather than decidable. The formulas $\psi_{\bar n, \sigma}$ become existential, and the checks, implications between existential formulas, become $\forall\exists$ questions, decided by the hypothesis; the bets then certify a c.e. existential Scott family (Montalbán, Theorem VIII.13; Ash–Knight, Theorem 12.3). The hypothesis cannot be weakened much: Kudinov (1996) built a computably categorical structure that is $\forall$-decidable but not relatively computably categorical. Downey, Kach, Lempp and Turetsky (2013) measured what remains under this weaker hypothesis: a $\forall$-decidable computably categorical structure has a c.e. Scott family of $\Sigma^c_2$ formulas over some tuple, though not necessarily of existential ones. Without any hypothesis, the two notions differ, by :ref{#thm-goncharov-cc-not-rcc}.

::::exercise{#ex-nurtazin-dlo difficulty=1 objective="Check effective atomicity on a structure with quantifier elimination."}
Show that the standard presentation of $(\mathbb Q; <)$ is decidable and effectively atomic over the empty tuple, and describe the generating formulas.

:::solution
By quantifier elimination for dense linear orders without endpoints, every formula is equivalent, effectively, to a quantifier-free one, and quantifier-free formulas are decided in the standard presentation; so it is decidable. The type of a tuple $\bar a$ is generated by the formula giving its order type: every formula true of $\bar a$ is equivalent to a quantifier-free one, a Boolean combination of comparisons between the entries, whose truth is determined by the order type. These formulas form a computable set, and every tuple satisfies one.
:::
::::

## Notes and references

:ref{#thm-nurtazin} is due to Nurtazin (1974); the proof follows Montalbán, Theorem VIII.11, with the requirements organized slightly differently: a requirement only acts when a check fails, which suffices. In model-theoretic terms, $\mathcal A$ is effectively atomic over $\bar p$ when $(\mathcal A, \bar p)$ is an atomic model whose generating formulas can be listed; Ash and Knight state the theorem in this form (Corollary 12.5). :ref{#thm-goncharov-2-decidable} is Goncharov (1975); Kudinov's example is from 1996.
