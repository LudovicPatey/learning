The continuous degrees are the degrees of points of the Hilbert cube, the total degrees those of points of Cantor space, and all enumeration degrees those of points of a power of the Sierpiński space (lesson on continuous degrees). Kihara, Ng and Pauly turned this observation into a classification: each second-countable space determines a class of enumeration degrees, and topological properties of spaces, from the separation axioms to metrizability, correspond to computability-theoretic properties of degrees. This lesson proves one instance of the correspondence, for the graph-cototal degrees, and describes the resulting zoo.

## Degrees of points

Recall that a represented space is a second-countable $T_0$ space $X$ with a listing $(B_i)$ of a base, and that a name of a point $x$ is an enumeration of $N_x = \{ i : x \in B_i \}$; the degree of $x$ is $\deg_e(N_x)$, and $\mathcal D_X$ is the set of degrees of points of $X$. A space is $T_1$ if its points are closed, $T_2$ (Hausdorff) if distinct points have disjoint neighborhoods, and $T_{2.5}$ if distinct points have neighborhoods with disjoint closures.

On $\nat$, the **cofinite topology** has as open sets the empty set and the complements of finite sets. It is $T_1$, since $\{ m \}$ is closed, and not Hausdorff, since any two nonempty open sets meet. Let $\nat_{\mathrm{cof}}^\nat$ be the product of countably many copies, with the base of the sets $B_{\langle n_1, F_1 \rangle, \ldots} = \{ y : y(n_j) \notin F_j \text{ for each } j \}$, for finitely many pairs of a coordinate $n_j$ and a finite set $F_j$.

:::proposition[Kihara, Ng, Pauly]{#prop-cofinite-graph-cototal}
The degrees of points of $\nat_{\mathrm{cof}}^\nat$ are exactly the graph-cototal degrees.
:::

:::proof{of=prop-cofinite-graph-cototal}
A point is a total function $g : \nat \to \nat$, and it belongs to a basic open set if and only if $g(n_j) \notin F_j$ for each of its finitely many conditions. So $N_g$ is enumerated from the set $\overline{G_g} = \{ \langle n, m \rangle : g(n) \neq m \}$, by listing the conditions all of whose excluded values are in $\overline{G_g}$; conversely, $\langle n, m \rangle \in \overline{G_g}$ if and only if the basic set given by the single condition $(n, \{ m \})$ contains $g$. So $N_g \equiv_e \overline{G_g}$, and the degrees of points are the degrees of the complements of graphs of total functions.
:::

Neighborhoods in the cofinite topology carry only negative information about a coordinate, "$g(n)$ is not $m$". This is the topological meaning of graph-cototality.

:::quiz[degrees of points]
Why is the degree of a point of $\nat_{\mathrm{cof}}^\nat$ not total in general, while the degree of a point of the Baire space $\nat^\nat$ is?

- [x] A neighborhood of $g$ in the Baire space fixes finitely many values of $g$, so names give the graph of $g$; in the cofinite topology, a neighborhood only excludes finitely many values, so names give the complement of the graph.
- [ ] Because the cofinite topology is not second-countable.

  It is second-countable on $\nat$, and so is the product.
- [ ] Because $\nat_{\mathrm{cof}}^\nat$ has fewer points.

  Both spaces have the total functions as points; the topologies differ.
:::

## The zoo

Kihara, Ng and Pauly identify many other classes, among them (with the space realizing them):
- the **total** degrees: Cantor space, Baire space, the reals, and more generally countable-dimensional separable metric spaces (Kihara and Pauly);
- the **continuous** degrees: the Hilbert cube, the universal separable metric space;
- the **semi-computable** degrees: the reals with the lower topology, generated by the intervals $(q, \infty)$, a $T_0$ space that is not $T_1$;
- the **graph-cototal** degrees ($T_1$, :ref{#prop-cofinite-graph-cototal}); the **cylinder-cototal** degrees, degrees of complements of the sets of prefixes of total functions, given by the cocylinder topology on $\nat^\nat$ ($T_1$, not $T_2$); the **telograph-cototal** degrees, given by a power of the telophase space ($T_1$, not $T_2$);
- the **doubled co-d-CEA** degrees ($T_2$, not $T_{2.5}$) and the **co-d-CEA** degrees (submetrizable, not metrizable), defined through sets that are differences of sets c.e. in a total oracle.

Two general theorems stand out. An enumeration degree is **cototal** if and only if it is the degree of a point of a computably $G_\delta$ second-countable space, a space in which closed sets are effectively countable intersections of open sets. And **every** enumeration degree is the degree of a point of a decidable submetrizable space, in particular of a decidable $T_{2.5}$ space: separation axioms up to $T_{2.5}$ alone do not restrict degrees, while metrizability does.

The classes are separated by **relative quasi-minimality**: a degree is $\mathcal T$-quasi-minimal, for a collection $\mathcal T$ of spaces, if it contains a set $A$ such that every point of a space of $\mathcal T$ whose name is enumeration reducible to $A$ has a c.e. name. Kihara, Ng and Pauly build, for instance, a cylinder-cototal degree that is quasi-minimal for the telograph-cototal degrees, and a continuous degree that is neither telograph-cototal nor cylinder-cototal. Jacobsen-Grocott (2024) answered several of their questions; among his results, the cylinder-cototal degrees are quasi-minimal for all $T_2$ spaces.

::::exercise{#ex-sierpinski-cototal difficulty=1 objective="Compare the Sierpiński space with the cofinite topology."}
Show that the Sierpiński space $\{ 0, 1 \}$, with the open sets $\emptyset$, $\{ 1 \}$ and $\{ 0, 1 \}$, is $T_0$ but not $T_1$, and that the cofinite topology on $\nat$ is $T_1$ but not $T_2$.

:::solution
In the Sierpiński space, the open set $\{ 1 \}$ separates $1$ from $0$, so the space is $T_0$; but every open set containing $0$ contains $1$, so $\{ 1 \}$ is not closed, and the space is not $T_1$. In the cofinite topology, $\{ m \}$ is closed, as the complement of the open cofinite set $\nat \setminus \{ m \}$, so the space is $T_1$; two nonempty open sets are cofinite and intersect, so no two points have disjoint neighborhoods, and the space is not $T_2$.
:::
::::

## Notes and references

The study of the degrees of points of represented spaces was started by Kihara and Pauly and developed by Kihara, Ng and Pauly, whose paper contains :ref{#prop-cofinite-graph-cototal}, the zoo, the characterization of cototality by computably $G_\delta$ spaces, and a list of open questions. Jacobsen-Grocott's thesis (Wisconsin, 2024) continues this work and also treats the hyperenumeration degrees.

- T. Kihara, K. M. Ng and A. Pauly, *Enumeration degrees and non-metrizable topology*, arXiv:1904.04107 (2019, revised 2020).
- T. Kihara and A. Pauly, *Point degree spectra of represented spaces*, Forum of Mathematics, Sigma (2022).
- J. Jacobsen-Grocott, *Structural and topological aspects of the enumeration and hyperenumeration degrees*, PhD thesis, University of Wisconsin–Madison, 2024.

:::::exercises
::::exercise{#ex-baire-total-points difficulty=1 objective="Compute the degree of a point of the Baire space."}
Show that a point $g$ of the Baire space $\nat^\nat$, with the base of cylinders, has the degree $\deg_e(G_g)$, which is total.

:::solution
A name of $g$ enumerates the cylinders containing $g$, that is, the prefixes of $g$; the set of prefixes is enumeration equivalent to the graph $G_g$, since a prefix is a finite set of pairs of the graph and conversely each pair $\langle n, m \rangle$ is in the graph if and only if some prefix of length $n + 1$ ends with $m$. The graph of a total function is total (:ref{#ex-graph-degree}).
:::
::::

::::exercise{#ex-lower-topology-t0 difficulty=1 objective="Check the separation axioms for the lower topology on the reals."}
Show that the reals with the lower topology, generated by the intervals $(q, \infty)$, form a $T_0$ space that is not $T_1$.

:::solution
For $x < y$, a rational $q$ with $x < q < y$ gives an open set $(q, \infty)$ containing $y$ but not $x$: the space is $T_0$. The open sets are unions of intervals $(q, \infty)$, so every open set containing $x$ contains every $y > x$. Hence no open set contains $x$ and not $x + 1$, and the space is not $T_1$.
:::
