The arithmetized completeness theorem gives end extensions that satisfy a prescribed theory, but it controls only a bounded part of the elementary diagram. To obtain *elementary* end extensions of a model $M$ of $\mathrm{PA}$, one adds to $M$ a single new element $d$ and the elements definable from it, so that elementarity is automatic, and one chooses the type of $d$ over $M$ so that no new element appears below an old one. This lesson sets up the method. The type of $d$ is **definable** if, for each formula $\varphi(v, \bar z)$, $M$ can define the set of $\bar z$ such that $\varphi(d, \bar z)$ holds; this property makes the extension **conservative**, and conservative extensions of models of $\mathrm{PA}$ are end extensions. The existence of definable unbounded types, the theorem of Gaifman and Phillips, is the subject of the next lesson.

## Types and the extension they generate

Throughout, $M$ is a model of $\mathrm{PA}$, and $\mathcal L_A(M)$ is the set of formulas of $\mathcal L_A$ with parameters from $M$. The Tarski–Vaught test of first-order logic is used without comment: a subset $N$ of a structure $K$ is the domain of an elementary substructure if, whenever $K \models \exists y\ \varphi(y, \bar c)$ with $\bar c \in N$, some witness lies in $N$.

:::definition{#def-ee-type}
A **complete $M$-type** $p(\bar v)$, in a finite tuple of variables $\bar v$, is a set of formulas $\varphi(\bar v)$ of $\mathcal L_A(M)$ such that every finite subset of $p$ is satisfied in $M$ by some tuple, and for every $\varphi(\bar v) \in \mathcal L_A(M)$, $\varphi \in p$ or $\neg \varphi \in p$. A tuple $\bar d$ of an elementary extension $K$ of $M$ **realizes** $p$ if $K \models \varphi(\bar d)$ for every $\varphi \in p$; the type of $\bar d$ over $M$ is written $\mathrm{tp}(\bar d / M)$. A complete $M$-type $p(v)$ in one variable is **unbounded** if it contains $v > a$ for every $a \in M$.
:::

By compactness, every complete $M$-type is realized in some elementary extension of $M$. If $\bar a$ is a tuple of $M$, $\mathrm{tp}(\bar a / M)$ is a complete $M$-type, realized in $M$; an unbounded type is realized only by elements above $M$.

:::definition{#def-ee-mp}
Let $K \succcurlyeq M$ and $A \subseteq K$. An element $b \in K$ is **definable from $A$** if $K \models \forall x\ (\eta(x, \bar a) \leftrightarrow x = b)$ for some formula $\eta$ of $\mathcal L_A$ and some tuple $\bar a$ of $A$. Let $\mathrm{dcl}_K(A)$ be the set of these elements. If $\bar d \in K$ realizes a complete $M$-type $p$, let $M(p) = \mathrm{dcl}_K(M \cup \{ \bar d \})$; when the realization matters, we write $M(p / \bar d)$.
:::

:::lemma{#lem-ee-mp}
Let $p(\bar v)$ be a complete $M$-type realized by $\bar d$ in $K \succcurlyeq M$.

1. $\mathrm{dcl}_K(A) \preccurlyeq K$ for every $A \subseteq K$; in particular $M \preccurlyeq M(p) \preccurlyeq K$.
2. Every element of $M(p)$ is $f(\bar d)$ for a function $f : M^k \to M$ definable in $M$ with parameters, where $f(\bar d)$ denotes the value at $\bar d$ of the function defined in $K$ by the same formula.
3. For definable functions $f_1, \dots, f_m$ and a formula $\psi(x_1, \dots, x_m)$, $M(p) \models \psi(f_1(\bar d), \dots, f_m(\bar d))$ if and only if the formula $\psi(f_1(\bar v), \dots, f_m(\bar v))$ belongs to $p$.
4. Up to an isomorphism that is the identity on $M$, $M(p)$ does not depend on $K$ and $\bar d$.
:::

:::proof{of=lem-ee-mp}
1. The set $\mathrm{dcl}_K(A)$ contains $A$. If $K \models \exists y\ \varphi(y, \bar c)$ with $\bar c$ definable from $A$, by formulas $\eta_i(x, \bar a)$, then $K \models \mathrm{PA}$, as $K \equiv M$, and the least $y$ with $\varphi(y, \bar c)$ exists; it is defined by "$y$ satisfies $\varphi$ with the elements defined by the $\eta_i$, and no smaller element does", a formula with parameters $\bar a$. By the Tarski–Vaught test, $\mathrm{dcl}_K(A) \preccurlyeq K$. For $A = M$, $\mathrm{dcl}_K(M) = M$, and $M \preccurlyeq K$; so $M \preccurlyeq M(p)$, both being elementary substructures of $K$ with $M \subseteq M(p)$.

2. Let $b$ be defined by $\eta(x, \bar m, \bar d)$, with $\bar m \in M$. Let $f(\bar v)$ be the least $x$ with $\eta(x, \bar m, \bar v)$ if there is one, and $0$ otherwise; it is a function on $M$, defined with the parameters $\bar m$. The same definition gives in $K$ a function whose value at $\bar d$ is $b$, since $b$ is the only element satisfying $\eta(x, \bar m, \bar d)$.

3. The formula $\psi(f_1(\bar v), \dots, f_m(\bar v))$ abbreviates $\exists \bar x\ (\bigwedge_j \chi_j(x_j, \bar v) \wedge \psi(\bar x))$, with $\chi_j$ the defining formula of $f_j$; it holds of $\bar d$ in $K$ if and only if it belongs to $p = \mathrm{tp}(\bar d / M)$, and $M(p) \preccurlyeq K$.

4. Let $\bar d'$ realize $p$ in $K' \succcurlyeq M$. Send $f(\bar d)$ to $f(\bar d')$. By item 3 with $\psi$ equal to $x_1 = x_2$, this is well defined and injective, and with the atomic formulas it preserves the operations and the order; it is onto $M(p / \bar d')$ by item 2 and is the identity on $M$, given by constant functions.
:::

Item 3 is an analogue of Łoś's theorem: $M(p)$ is the quotient of the set of definable functions $f : M^k \to M$ by the relation $f(\bar v) = g(\bar v) \in p$. For $M = \nat$ and $p$ in one variable, this is the **definable ultrapower** of $\nat$ (Hájek and Pudlák, Section IV.1(b)), which goes back to Skolem's construction of a non-standard model: the sets $\{ a : \varphi(a) \}$ with $\varphi(v) \in p$ form an ultrafilter on the algebra of arithmetic sets, and the elements of the ultrapower are classes of arithmetic functions.

## Definable types

:::definition{#def-ee-definable-type}
Let $\mathrm{Def}(M)$ be the set of subsets of the powers $M^k$ that are definable in $M$ with parameters. A complete $M$-type $p(\bar v)$ is **definable** if, for every formula $\varphi(\bar v, \bar z)$ of $\mathcal L_A$,
$$
\{ \bar z \in M : \varphi(\bar v, \bar z) \in p \} \in \mathrm{Def}(M).
$$
An extension $K \supseteq M$ of $\mathcal L_A$-structures is **conservative** if $X \cap M^k \in \mathrm{Def}(M)$ for every $X \in \mathrm{Def}(K)$, $X \subseteq K^k$.
:::

The formula $\varphi$ has no parameters, but allowing parameters changes nothing: for $\varphi(\bar v, \bar z, \bar m)$ with $\bar m \in M$, the set of $\bar z$ is the section at $\bar m$ of the set of pairs $(\bar z, \bar w)$ with $\varphi(\bar v, \bar z, \bar w) \in p$. A definable type is given by a **defining scheme**: for each $\varphi$, a formula $\mathrm d\varphi(\bar z)$ of $\mathcal L_A(M)$ that defines the set of $\bar z$. The type is determined by its defining scheme.

:::example{#ex-ee-realized-definable}
If $\bar a \in M$, then $\mathrm{tp}(\bar a / M)$ is definable, with $\mathrm d \varphi(\bar z) = \varphi(\bar a, \bar z)$, and $M(\mathrm{tp}(\bar a / M)) = M$. The extension $M \subseteq M$ is conservative. Both properties become non-trivial for types not realized in $M$.
:::

:::proposition{#prop-ee-definable-conservative}
A complete $M$-type $p$ is definable if and only if $M(p)$ is a conservative extension of $M$.
:::

:::proof{of=prop-ee-definable-conservative}
Let $M(p) = M(p / \bar d)$.

($\Rightarrow$) Let $X \subseteq M(p)^k$ be defined in $M(p)$ by $\theta(\bar z, \bar b)$ with $\bar b \in M(p)$. By :ref{#lem-ee-mp}, $\bar b = \bar f(\bar d)$ for definable functions $\bar f$, and for $\bar z \in M$, $M(p) \models \theta(\bar z, \bar f(\bar d))$ if and only if $\theta(\bar z, \bar f(\bar v)) \in p$. The formula $\theta(\bar z, \bar f(\bar v))$ is a formula $\varphi(\bar v, \bar z, \bar m)$, with $\bar m$ the parameters of the definitions of $\bar f$. So $X \cap M^k = \{ \bar z \in M : \varphi(\bar v, \bar z, \bar m) \in p \}$, which belongs to $\mathrm{Def}(M)$ since $p$ is definable.

($\Leftarrow$) For $\varphi(\bar v, \bar z)$, $\{ \bar z \in M : \varphi(\bar v, \bar z) \in p \} = \{ \bar z \in M : M(p) \models \varphi(\bar d, \bar z) \}$, the trace on $M$ of a set definable in $M(p)$; it is in $\mathrm{Def}(M)$ by conservativity.
:::

:::quiz[definable types and conservative extensions]
In the direction ($\Rightarrow$), why is it necessary that every element of $M(p)$ be the value $f(\bar d)$ of a function definable in $M$?

- [x] A set definable in $M(p)$ may use parameters from $M(p)$; writing them as $f(\bar d)$ turns the defining formula into a formula about $\bar d$ with parameters in $M$, to which the definability of $p$ applies.
- [ ] To know that $M(p)$ is an elementary extension of $M$.

  Elementarity is item 1 of :ref{#lem-ee-mp} and is not used in this direction.
- [ ] Because the defining scheme of $p$ only applies to formulas without parameters.

  The scheme extends to formulas with parameters in $M$, as noted after the definition; the issue is parameters outside $M$.
:::

:::proposition{#prop-ee-conservative-end}
Let $K \supseteq M$ be a conservative extension with $K \models \mathrm{PA}^-$. Then $M \subseteq_e K$.
:::

:::proof{of=prop-ee-conservative-end}
Let $b \in K \setminus M$. By conservativity, $S = \{ z \in M : K \models z < b \}$ is definable in $M$. It contains $0$, since $b \neq 0$. If $z \in S$, then $K \models z + 1 \leq b$ by :ref{#lem-pam-basic}, and $z + 1 \neq b$ since $z + 1 \in M$; so $z + 1 \in S$. By induction in $M$, for the formula that defines $S$, $S = M$: every element of $M$ is below $b$.
:::

The hypothesis $M \models \mathrm{PA}$ is used for an induction on a formula of arbitrary complexity, with parameters. With :ref{#prop-ee-definable-conservative}, the method of the next lesson is fixed.

:::corollary{#cor-ee-definable-end}
If $p(v)$ is a definable complete $M$-type that is not realized in $M$, then $M(p)$ is a proper elementary end extension of $M$, and it is conservative.
:::

:::proof{of=cor-ee-definable-end}
$M(p) \succcurlyeq M$ by :ref{#lem-ee-mp}, conservative by :ref{#prop-ee-definable-conservative}, an end extension by :ref{#prop-ee-conservative-end}, and proper since the realization $d$ of $p$ is not in $M$.
:::

## Types that are not definable

Over the standard model, every proper elementary extension is an end extension (:ref{#prop-standard-part}), so the corollary has content only through conservativity. Types that code a given set show that it can fail.

:::example{#ex-ee-coding-type}
Let $X \subseteq \nat$, and let $\varepsilon(v, z)$ be the bounded formula $z \in v$ of :ref{#def-ack-membership}. The set
$$
p_0(v) = \{ v > a : a \in \nat \} \cup \{ \varepsilon(v, \bar k) : k \in X \} \cup \{ \neg \varepsilon(v, \bar k) : k \notin X \}
$$
is finitely satisfied in $\nat$: given $a$ and finitely many $k < m$, the number $2^{a + m} + \sum_{k \in X, k < m} 2^k$ satisfies the corresponding formulas. Extend it to a complete $\nat$-type $p_X$, by compactness and completion of the theory $\mathrm{Th}(\nat, n)_{n \in \nat} \cup p_0(d)$. Then $\{ k : \varepsilon(v, \bar k) \in p_X \} = X$. If $X$ is not arithmetic, $p_X$ is not definable, since the definable subsets of $\nat$ are the arithmetic sets (:ref{#thm-definable-arithmetic}). So $\nat(p_X)$ is a proper elementary end extension of $\nat$ that is not conservative: the set coded by the realization $d$ of $p_X$ is $\mathrm{Ack}(d) \cap \nat = X$, and $X \in \mathrm{SSy}(\nat(p_X))$.
:::

On the other hand, there are models of $\mathrm{PA}$ all of whose elementary end extensions are conservative (Wong, Lecture 9, citing Section 2.2.2 of Kossak and Schmerl). For such models, definable types are the only route to elementary end extensions, which explains the detour through definability in the proof of the MacDowell–Specker theorem.

## Exercises

::::exercise{#ex-ee-cod-def difficulty=2}
Let $K$ be a proper elementary end extension of $M$. Show that $\mathrm{Def}(M) \cap \mathcal P(M) \subseteq \mathrm{Cod}(K/M)$, and that $K$ is conservative if and only if $\mathrm{Cod}(K/M) \subseteq \mathrm{Def}(M)$.

:::solution
Let $S \subseteq M$ be defined by $\theta(x, \bar m)$, and $b \in K \setminus M$. Since $K \models \mathrm{PA}$, the set $\{ x < b : \theta(x, \bar m) \}$ of $K$ is coded by some $c \in K$, by :ref{#thm-friedman-coded} applied with $n$ the complexity of $\theta$. For $x \in M$, $x < b$, and $K \models \theta(x, \bar m)$ if and only if $x \in S$, by elementarity; so $\mathrm{Ack}(c) \cap M = S$. If $K$ is conservative, then $\mathrm{Ack}(c) \cap M$ is the trace of a set definable in $K$, hence in $\mathrm{Def}(M)$. Conversely, assume $\mathrm{Cod}(K/M) \subseteq \mathrm{Def}(M)$, and let $X \subseteq K$ be defined by $\theta(x, \bar b)$ with $\bar b \in K$. With $b \in K \setminus M$ as before, some $c$ codes $\{ x < b : \theta(x, \bar b) \}$ in $K$, and $X \cap M = \mathrm{Ack}(c) \cap M \in \mathrm{Def}(M)$. For subsets of $M^k$, use pairing.
:::
::::

::::exercise{#ex-ee-unbounded-nonrealized difficulty=1}
Show that a complete $M$-type $p(v)$ that is not realized in $M$ and contains a formula $v < b$ with $b \in M$ is not definable.

:::solution
If $p$ were definable, $M(p)$ would be conservative by :ref{#prop-ee-definable-conservative}, hence an end extension by :ref{#prop-ee-conservative-end}. Its element $d$ realizing $p$ satisfies $d < b$ with $b \in M$, so $d \in M$, and $p = \mathrm{tp}(d / M)$ is realized in $M$, a contradiction.
:::
::::

## Notes and references

Definable types were introduced by Gaifman (1976) in the study of models of $\mathrm{PA}$, and conservative extensions by Phillips (1974); both notions became central in stability theory (Wong, Lecture 9, Further reading). :ref{#prop-ee-definable-conservative} and :ref{#prop-ee-conservative-end} are Wong's Propositions 9.5 and 9.6. Wong builds $M(p)$ as the prime model of a complete theory through a term model (Proposition 9.3) and notes that it is the definable closure; we use the definable closure directly. The definable ultrapower of $\nat$ is Section IV.1(b) of Hájek and Pudlák, who attribute it to Skolem (1933).

- H. Gaifman, *Models and types of Peano's arithmetic*, Annals of Mathematical Logic 9 (1976), 223–306.
- R. G. Phillips, *Omitting types in arithmetic and conservative extensions*, in Victoria Symposium on Nonstandard Analysis, Lecture Notes in Mathematics 369, Springer, 1974, 195–202.
- T. L. Wong, *Model theory of arithmetic*, lecture notes, Lecture 9.
- P. Hájek and P. Pudlák, *Metamathematics of First-Order Arithmetic*, Springer, 1993, Chapter IV, Section 1(b).
- R. Kossak and J. H. Schmerl, *The Structure of Models of Peano Arithmetic*, Oxford Logic Guides 50, Clarendon Press, 2006.
