To show that $\mathrm{PA}$ does not prove $\mathrm{PH}$ by the method of indicators, we must find, in a model $M$ of $\mathrm I\Sigma_1$, a cut that satisfies all of $\mathrm{PA}$. Semiregularity gave $\Sigma_1$ induction; full induction requires that the cut "looks the same" from many points. The tool is a set $H = \{ h_0 < h_1 < \cdots \}$ of **diagonal indiscernibles**: two tuples of elements of $H$ above $h_i$ satisfy the same bounded formulas with parameters below $h_i$. The cut $I$ generated by the $h_i$ with standard $i$ is then a model of $\mathrm{PA}$: an unbounded quantifier over $I$ can be replaced by a quantifier bounded by some $h_j$, and indiscernibility shows that the choice of $h_j$ does not matter. This turns every formula into a bounded formula of $M$, where the least number principle holds.

This lesson constructs diagonal indiscernibles from min-homogeneous sets (:ref{#lem-ph-regressive}) and proves that their cut satisfies $\mathrm{PA}$.

## Diagonal indiscernibles

Throughout, $M$ is a model of $\mathrm I\Sigma_1$, and formulas are $\Delta^*_0$ formulas (:ref{#def-delta0-star}), with a distinguished first variable: we write $\chi(x; y_1, \dots, y_t)$, where $x$ is the **parameter** and $y_1, \dots, y_t$ are the **indices**.

:::definition{#def-diagonal-indiscernibles}
Let $H = \{ h_0 < h_1 < \cdots < h_{N-1} \}$ be a coded subset of $M$ and $\Phi$ a set of formulas. $H$ is **diagonally indiscernible** for $\Phi$ if for every $\chi(x; y_1, \dots, y_t) \in \Phi$, every $i < N$, every $x$ with $x + 1 < h_i$, and all increasing $t$-tuples $\bar y, \bar z$ of elements of $H$ above $h_i$,
$$
M \models \chi(x; \bar y) \leftrightarrow \chi(x; \bar z).
$$
:::

The word "diagonal" refers to the parameters: they may lie anywhere below $h_i$ when the indices are above $h_i$, so that, in the end, all elements of the cut can serve as parameters.

:::example{#ex-diagonal-indiscernibles}
For $\chi(x; y_1) \equiv \exists u < y_1\ (u + u = y_1)$, "$y_1$ is even", diagonal indiscernibility (with $i = 0$) says that all elements of $H$ above $h_0$ have the same parity. For $\chi(x; y_1) \equiv \exists u < y_1\ (u \cdot x = y_1)$, it says that for each $i$ and each $x$ with $2 \leq x$ and $x + 1 < h_i$, either $x$ divides all elements of $H$ above $h_i$, or none of them.
:::

## Construction from a regressive coloring

Let $R = (R_j)_{j < e}$ be a family of relations $R_j \subseteq [0, b]^{1 + m}$, coded in $M$; we read $R_j(x; \bar y)$ as a formula with parameter $x$ and $m$ indices. On $[[1, b]]^{2m+1}$, define the coloring $f_R$ as follows: for $s = \{ y_0 \} \cup \bar y \cup \bar z$, where $y_0 < \bar y < \bar z$ and $\bar y, \bar z$ are the next $m$ and the last $m$ elements of $s$,
$$
f_R(s) = \begin{cases} x + 1 & \text{for the least } x \text{ with } x + 1 < y_0 \text{ such that } R_j(x; \bar y) \not\leftrightarrow R_j(x; \bar z) \text{ for some } j < e, \\ 0 & \text{if there is no such } x. \end{cases}
$$
It is regressive. A min-homogeneous set makes $f_R$ vanish, because of a counting argument: a nonzero value $x + 1$ at the minimum $h$ would make all blocks above $h$ pairwise different at the same parameter $x$, and there are at most $2^e$ patterns of truth values.

:::lemma{#lem-diag-from-regressive}
Let $H$ be min-homogeneous for $f_R$, and let $H_2$ be $H$ without its $(2^e + 2) m$ largest elements. Then for every $h \in H_2$, every $x$ with $x + 1 < h$, every $j < e$ and all increasing $m$-tuples $\bar y, \bar z$ from $H \setminus \{ \text{the } m \text{ largest elements} \}$ above $h$, $R_j(x; \bar y) \leftrightarrow R_j(x; \bar z)$.
:::

:::proof{of=lem-diag-from-regressive}
*Step 1.* Let $h \in H_2$, and let $\bar u < \bar u'$ be $m$-tuples from $H$ above $h$, the first entirely below the second. We show $f_R(\{ h \} \cup \bar u \cup \bar u') = 0$. Suppose the value is $x + 1$. Above $h$ there are at least $(2^e + 2) m$ elements of $H$; choose among them $L = 2^e + 1$ consecutive blocks $\bar u_1 < \bar u_2 < \cdots < \bar u_L$ of $m$ elements. For $p < q$, the set $\{ h \} \cup \bar u_p \cup \bar u_q$ has minimum $h$, so by min-homogeneity $f_R$ takes the value $x + 1$ on it: some $j < e$ has $R_j(x; \bar u_p) \not\leftrightarrow R_j(x; \bar u_q)$. So the vectors $(R_j(x; \bar u_p))_{j < e} \in \{ 0, 1 \}^e$, for $p = 1, \dots, L$, are pairwise distinct, which is impossible since $L > 2^e$.

*Step 2.* Let $\bar w$ be the $m$ largest elements of $H$. For $\bar y$ as in the statement, $\bar y$ lies entirely below $\bar w$, and Step 1 gives $f_R(\{ h \} \cup \bar y \cup \bar w) = 0$: for every $x$ with $x + 1 < h$ and every $j < e$, $R_j(x; \bar y) \leftrightarrow R_j(x; \bar w)$. The same holds for $\bar z$, and the two equivalences give the claim.
:::

Step 1 is the **pigeonhole principle** for $2^e + 1$ objects in $2^e$ boxes, applied in $M$; it holds for non-standard $e$, which the next lesson needs so that $R$ contains every standard formula. Step 2 compares $\bar y$ and $\bar z$ with a common block above both, since $f_R$ only compares a block with a block entirely above it.

:::quiz[diagonal indiscernibles from a regressive coloring]
In the proof of :ref{#lem-diag-from-regressive}, why does $f_R$ return $x + 1$ rather than $x$?

- [x] The value $0$ must mean "no difference"; if $f_R$ returned $x$, the value $0$ would be ambiguous between a difference at the parameter $0$ and no difference at all.
- [ ] To make $f_R$ regressive.

  Returning $x$ would also be regressive; the shift requires the restriction $x + 1 < y_0$ to keep regressiveness.
- [ ] Because min-homogeneity fails for the value $0$.

  Min-homogeneity concerns all values alike.

:::

## Growth

Diagonal indiscernibility for a single quantifier-free formula forces the elements of $H$ to grow fast. Let
$$
\sigma(x; y_1, y_2) \equiv x \cdot x + y_1 \leq y_2 \wedge y_2 < (x + 1) \cdot (x + 1) + y_1,
$$
which says that $x$ is the integer part of $\sqrt{y_2 - y_1}$.

:::lemma{#lem-diag-growth}
Let $H = \{ h_0 < \cdots < h_{N-1} \}$ be diagonally indiscernible for $\{ \sigma \}$. If $i < j < k < l$ and at least ten elements of $H$ lie above $h_i$, then $h_k - h_j \geq (h_i - 1)^2$. Consequently $h_{i+3} > h_i^2$ whenever $h_i \geq 1$ and at least ten elements of $H$ lie above $h_{i+1}$.
:::

:::proof{of=lem-diag-growth}
Let $d$ be the integer part of $\sqrt{h_k - h_j}$, so that $\sigma(d; h_j, h_k)$ holds. Suppose $d + 1 < h_i$. By indiscernibility, $\sigma(d; y_1, y_2)$ holds for every pair $y_1 < y_2$ of elements of $H$ above $h_i$. If $d \leq 2$, all these pairs differ by less than $9$, which fails for the first and the tenth element above $h_i$. If $d \geq 3$, then $h_l - h_j = (h_l - h_k) + (h_k - h_j) \geq 2 d^2 \geq (d + 1)^2$, contradicting $\sigma(d; h_j, h_l)$. So $d + 1 \geq h_i$, and $h_k - h_j \geq d^2 \geq (h_i - 1)^2$. For the consequence, apply this to $i + 1 < i + 2 < i + 3 < i + 4$: $h_{i+3} - h_{i+2} \geq (h_{i+1} - 1)^2 \geq h_i^2$, since $h_{i+1} \geq h_i + 1$.
:::

## The cut of the indiscernibles

We use the iterated pairing $\langle v_1, \dots, v_\ell \rangle = \langle \langle v_1, \dots, v_{\ell-1} \rangle, v_\ell \rangle$ of :ref{#fact-pairing}, which is increasing in each argument. For a formula in prenex form $\varphi(\bar v) = Q_1 u_1 \cdots Q_k u_k\ \theta(\bar v, \bar u)$, with $\theta$ quantifier-free, and a $k$-tuple $\bar y$, let
$$
\varphi^{[\bar y]}(\bar v) \equiv Q_1 u_1 < y_1\ \cdots\ Q_k u_k < y_k\ \theta(\bar v, \bar u), \qquad \varphi^\sharp(x; \bar y) \equiv \exists \bar v \leq x\ \big(x = \langle \bar v \rangle \wedge \varphi^{[\bar y]}(\bar v)\big).
$$
Both are $\Delta^*_0$ formulas, bounds being variables. The formula $\varphi^\sharp$ packs the tuple $\bar v$ into the single parameter $x$.

:::theorem[Paris, Harrington]{#thm-indiscernible-cut}
Let $H = \{ h_0 < \cdots < h_{N-1} \}$ be a coded subset of $M$ with $N$ non-standard and $h_0 \geq 3$, diagonally indiscernible for every standard $\Delta^*_0$ formula. Then
$$
I = \{ u \in M : u < h_i \text{ for some standard } i \}
$$
is a proper cut of $M$, closed under addition and multiplication, and $I \models \mathrm{PA}$.
:::

:::proof{of=thm-indiscernible-cut}
*The cut.* $I$ is an initial segment, closed under successor. For standard $i$, at least ten elements of $H$ lie above $h_{i+1}$, so $h_{i+3} > h_i^2$ by :ref{#lem-diag-growth}; if $u, v < h_i$, then $u + v$ and $u \cdot v$ are below $h_i^2 < h_{i+3}$. So $I$ is closed under the operations, hence a model of $\mathrm I\Delta_0$ (:ref{#lem-cut-model}). It is proper, since $h_{N-1} > h_i$ for every standard $i$. Similarly, if $x, u < h_i$, then $\langle x, u \rangle + 1 < 4 h_i^2 < h_i^4 < h_{i+6}$.

Say that a tuple $\bar v$ is **at level $i$** if $\langle \bar v \rangle + 1 < h_i$; every tuple from $I$ is at some standard level, and then at all higher levels.

*Claim.* For every prenex formula $\varphi(\bar v)$ with $k$ quantifiers, every standard $i$ and every $\bar v$ at level $i$: $I \models \varphi(\bar v)$ if and only if $M \models \varphi^{[\bar y]}(\bar v)$ for some, equivalently every, increasing $k$-tuple $\bar y$ of elements of $H$ above $h_i$ with standard indices.

The two versions of the right-hand side are equivalent by diagonal indiscernibility for $\varphi^\sharp$, with the parameter $x = \langle \bar v \rangle$: $M \models \varphi^{[\bar y]}(\bar v) \leftrightarrow \varphi^\sharp(x; \bar y)$. We prove the claim by induction on $k$, for all prenex formulas with $k$ quantifiers at once. For $k = 0$, $\varphi$ is quantifier-free, and its truth in $I$ and in $M$ agree, since $I$ is a substructure. Since the negation of a prenex formula with $k$ quantifiers is logically equivalent to a prenex formula with $k$ quantifiers, obtained by exchanging $\exists$ and $\forall$, and both sides of the claim commute with negation, it suffices to treat $\varphi(\bar v) = \exists u\ \psi(\bar v, u)$, with $\psi$ prenex with $k$ quantifiers.

($\Rightarrow$) Let $u \in I$ with $I \models \psi(\bar v, u)$. The tuple $(\bar v, u)$ is at some standard level $j > i$, and $u < h_j$. Let $\bar z$ be a $k$-tuple of elements of $H$ above $h_j$ with standard indices. By the induction hypothesis, $M \models \psi^{[\bar z]}(\bar v, u)$, so $M \models \varphi^{[(h_j, \bar z)]}(\bar v)$, and $(h_j, \bar z)$ is a tuple above $h_i$.

($\Leftarrow$) Choose the tuple $\bar y = (h_j, \bar z)$ with $j > i$ standard and $\bar z$ above $h_{j+6}$, with standard indices. By hypothesis $M \models \psi^{[\bar z]}(\bar v, u)$ for some $u < h_j$. Then $\langle \bar v, u \rangle + 1 < h_{j+6}$, since $\langle \bar v \rangle$ and $u$ are below $h_j$; so $(\bar v, u)$ is at level $j + 6$, and the induction hypothesis gives $I \models \psi(\bar v, u)$. As $u \in I$, $I \models \varphi(\bar v)$.

*Induction in $I$.* Every formula is logically equivalent to a prenex formula, so it suffices to prove the least number principle in $I$ for a prenex formula $\varphi(u, \bar v)$ with parameters $\bar v$ from $I$. Suppose $I \models \varphi(d, \bar v)$. Let $i$ be a standard level of $(d, \bar v)$ and $\bar y$ a tuple above $h_i$ with standard indices. The formula $\delta(u) \equiv \varphi^{[\bar y]}(u, \bar v)$ is bounded, with parameters in $M$, and $M \models \delta(d)$ by the claim. By the least number principle for bounded formulas in $M$, there is a least $d_0 \leq d$ with $M \models \delta(d_0)$. Every $d' \leq d$ is in $I$, and $(d', \bar v)$ is at level $i$ since pairing is increasing; so by the claim, $I \models \varphi(d', \bar v)$ if and only if $M \models \delta(d')$. Hence $d_0$ is the least element of $I$ satisfying $\varphi(\cdot, \bar v)$. The least number principle for all formulas implies the induction scheme over $\mathrm{PA}^-$: if induction failed for $\psi$, the least element satisfying $\neg \psi$ would give a contradiction. So $I \models \mathrm{PA}$.
:::

The claim is a **truth reduction**: truth in $I$ is computed in $M$ by bounded formulas, with indiscernibles as bounds. Only bounded induction in $M$ is used; the strength of $\mathrm{PA}$ in $I$ comes from the indiscernibles.

:::quiz[the cut of the indiscernibles]
In the direction ($\Leftarrow$) of the claim, why is the tuple chosen with $\bar z$ above $h_{j+6}$ rather than just above $h_j$?

- [x] The witness $u$ is only known to be below $h_j$; the pair $(\bar v, u)$ is then at level $j + 6$, and the induction hypothesis requires the indices $\bar z$ to lie above the level of $(\bar v, u)$.
- [ ] Because the formula $\psi$ has $k$ quantifiers.

  The number of quantifiers fixes the length of $\bar z$, not its position.
- [ ] Because $h_{j+6}$ is non-standard.

  Every $h_j$ with standard $j$ is in $I$; non-standard elements of $H$ are not used as indices in the claim.

:::

## Notes and references

The use of indiscernibles to build cuts satisfying $\mathrm{PA}$ is due to Paris and Harrington (1977); diagonal indiscernibles and the regressive formulation are due to Kanamori and McAloon (1987). The growth lemma and the packing of parameters into one variable are written for this course, after the same arguments in these papers. Hájek and Pudlák obtain the cut differently, through "Paris sequences" and restrainable cuts (Chapter IV, Section 3(b)–(c), Lemma 3.17 and Theorem 3.23), and their Chapter II, Section 2(d) treats strong indiscernibles.

- J. Paris and L. Harrington, *A mathematical incompleteness in Peano arithmetic*, in Handbook of Mathematical Logic, North-Holland, 1977, 1133–1142.
- A. Kanamori and K. McAloon, *On Gödel incompleteness and finite combinatorics*, Annals of Pure and Applied Logic 33 (1987), 23–41.
- P. Hájek and P. Pudlák, *Metamathematics of First-Order Arithmetic*, Springer, 1993, Chapter II, Section 2(d), and Chapter IV, Section 3.
