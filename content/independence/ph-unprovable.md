The previous lessons provide the three ingredients of the method of indicators for Peano arithmetic: a notion of largeness whose standard degrees are provable in $\mathrm{PA}$ (:ref{#thm-ph-true}); regressive colorings with large min-homogeneous sets (:ref{#lem-ph-regressive}); and cuts satisfying $\mathrm{PA}$ generated by diagonal indiscernibles (:ref{#thm-indiscernible-cut}). This lesson assembles them. The only new point is to obtain indiscernibility for all standard formulas at once inside a model of $\mathrm I\Sigma_1$: a satisfaction table with a non-standard number of formulas (:ref{#lem-sat-tables}) evaluates them all, and a single coloring handles them simultaneously.

## The indicator

:::definition{#def-ph-indicator}
$\iota_{\mathrm{PH}}(x, y, c)$ is the formula $x \leq y \wedge c \leq y \wedge \forall n \leq c\ \big([x, y] \to_* (c)^n_c\big)$.
:::

The relation $[x, y] \to_* (c)^n_c$ is $\Delta_1$ in $\mathrm I\Sigma_1$, and so is $\iota_{\mathrm{PH}}$, by Parsons' lemma (:ref{#lem-fo-collection-closure}); we use its $\Sigma_1$ form. Including all arities up to $c$ makes the formula monotone in $c$ without lowering arities, which would cost relative largeness. By item 3 of :ref{#ex-relatively-large}, $\mathrm I\Sigma_1$ proves that $\iota_{\mathrm{PH}}(x, y, c)$ implies $\iota_{\mathrm{PH}}(x', y', c')$ for $x' \leq x$, $y \leq y'$ and $c' \leq c$. The first two conjuncts are the normalization of :ref{#def-indicator}.

:::theorem[Paris, Harrington]{#thm-ph-indicator}
$\iota_{\mathrm{PH}}$ is an indicator for $\mathrm{PA}$ (:ref{#def-indicator}).
:::

:::proof{of=thm-ph-indicator}
Let $M$ be a countable non-standard model of $\mathrm I\Sigma_1$, and $a \leq b$ in $M$ with $a$ non-standard.

*(2 ⇒ 1).* Let $I \models \mathrm{PA}$ be a cut with $a \in I$ and $b \notin I$. For standard $n$, $\mathrm{PA} \vdash \mathrm{PH}_{n'}$ for each $n' \leq n$ (:ref{#thm-ph-true}); taking the maximum of $x$, $n$ and the finitely many bounds, and using monotonicity, $\mathrm{PA} \vdash \forall x\ \exists y\ \iota_{\mathrm{PH}}(x, y, n)$. So $I \models \iota_{\mathrm{PH}}(a, y, n)$ for some $y \in I$, hence $M \models \iota_{\mathrm{PH}}(a, y, n)$ (:ref{#cor-sigma1-upward}), and $M \models \iota_{\mathrm{PH}}(a, b, n)$ by monotonicity, since $y < b$. This holds for every standard $n$; overspill (:ref{#cor-overspill}) gives a non-standard $c$ with $M \models \iota_{\mathrm{PH}}(a, b, c)$.

*(1 ⇒ 2).* Let $M \models \iota_{\mathrm{PH}}(a, b, c)$ with $c$ non-standard. Replacing $c$ by $\min(c, a)$, which is non-standard, we may assume $c \leq a$, by monotonicity. All the following constructions take place in $M$.

*Parameters.* Let $m$ be the largest number with $2^{2m} \leq c$; it is non-standard. Let $n = 2m + 1$, $k = (2^m + 3) m$, $a' = a - n$ and $b' = b - n$. Since $n \leq \log_2 c + 1 \leq a / 2$, $a'$ is non-standard. The numbers $n + 1$, $2^{n-1} = 2^{2m}$ and $k + n - 1 = (2^m + 5) m$ are at most $2^{2m} \leq c$, so by monotonicity of the relation (:ref{#ex-relatively-large})
$$
[a' + n, b' + n] = [a, b] \to_* (k + n - 1)^{n+1}_{2^{n-1}} .
$$

*The formulas.* By :ref{#lem-sat-tables}, there is a satisfaction table $q$ for $(m + 1, b + 1)$: it evaluates every code $j < m + 1$ of a $\Delta^*_0$ formula at every assignment of length $m + 1$ with values at most $b$. For $j < m$ and $x, y_1, \dots, y_m \leq b$, let $R_j(x; \bar y)$ hold if $j$ is the code of a $\Delta^*_0$ formula and $q(j, \langle x, y_1, \dots, y_m \rangle) = 1$, the assignment giving $x$ to $v_0$ and $y_i$ to $v_i$. The family $R = (R_j)_{j < m}$ is coded in $M$. If $\chi(v_0; v_1, \dots, v_t)$ is a standard $\Delta^*_0$ formula, its code is standard, hence below $m$, and by :ref{#cor-snowing} (a $\Delta^*_0$ formula is $\Sigma^*_1$, with an empty block of unbounded quantifiers, and $\mathrm{Sat}_{\Sigma_1}$ agrees with $\mathrm{Sat}_0$ on it) and the uniqueness of tables (:ref{#lem-sat-tables}), $R_{\ulcorner \chi \urcorner}(x; \bar y) \leftrightarrow M \models \chi(x; y_1, \dots, y_t)$; the entries $y_{t+1}, \dots, y_m$ play no role.

*Indiscernibles.* The coloring $f_R$ of $[[a', b']]^n$, defined as before :ref{#lem-diag-from-regressive} with $e = m$ relations of $m$ indices, is coded in $M$ and regressive, since $a' \geq 1$. By :ref{#lem-ph-regressive}, applied in $M$, it has a min-homogeneous set $H$ with at least $k$ elements. Let $H_2$ be $H$ without its $(2^m + 2) m$ largest elements; it has at least $m$ elements. By :ref{#lem-diag-from-regressive}, for $h \in H_2$, $x + 1 < h$ and $m$-tuples $\bar y, \bar z$ above $h$ that avoid the $m$ largest elements of $H$, $R_j(x; \bar y) \leftrightarrow R_j(x; \bar z)$ for every $j < m$. Given a standard formula $\chi$ with $t \leq m$ indices and $t$-tuples $\bar y, \bar z$ from $H_2$ above $h$, extend both by the same $m - t$ elements of $H \setminus H_2$ that are not among the $m$ largest; this gives $\chi(x; \bar y) \leftrightarrow \chi(x; \bar z)$. So $H_2$ is diagonally indiscernible for every standard $\Delta^*_0$ formula.

*The cut.* $H_2$ has non-standard size and least element $h_0 \geq a' \geq 3$. By :ref{#thm-indiscernible-cut}, the cut $I$ generated by the elements of $H_2$ with standard index satisfies $\mathrm{PA}$. Its elements are below the elements of $H_2$ with non-standard index, all at most $b' < b$, so $b \notin I$. Finally $h_3 > h_0^2 \geq (a / 2)^2 > a$ by :ref{#lem-diag-growth}, so $a \in I$.
:::

The proof uses induction in $M$ in three places: bounded induction and the pigeonhole principle for the counting arguments, $\Sigma_1$ induction for the existence of the satisfaction table, and again only the least number principle for bounded formulas for the truth reduction in $I$, whose strength comes from the indiscernibles. The model $M$ need not satisfy $\mathrm{PA}$: a model of $\mathrm I\Sigma_1$ in which some interval is relatively large to a non-standard degree contains a model of $\mathrm{PA}$ as a cut.

:::quiz[the indicator for PA]
In the proof of :ref{#thm-ph-indicator}, why is a satisfaction table with a non-standard number $m$ of formulas used, rather than a separate coloring for each standard formula?

- [x] A single set $H_2$ must be indiscernible for all standard formulas at once. Each coloring is an element of $M$ and concerns the formulas it evaluates; only a non-standard family covers all standard formulas, and only a truth definition makes such a family an element of $M$.
- [ ] Because standard formulas cannot be evaluated in $M$.

  Each standard formula defines its relation in $M$ directly; the problem is the infinitely many formulas.
- [ ] Because the table makes the coloring regressive.

  Regressiveness comes from the definition of $f_R$, whatever the relations $R_j$ are.

---

Why are the parameters chosen with $2^{2m} \leq c$?

- [x] :ref{#lem-ph-regressive} needs $2^{n-1} = 2^{2m}$ colors for colorings of arity $n + 1$, and :ref{#lem-diag-from-regressive} needs homogeneous sets of size about $2^m m$; all these must be at most the largeness $c$.
- [ ] To make $m$ standard.

  $m$ must be non-standard, so that the family $R$ contains every standard formula.
- [ ] So that $a' = a - n$ is positive.

:::

## Unprovability

:::corollary[Paris, Harrington]{#cor-ph-unprovable}
$\mathrm{PA}$ does not prove the Paris–Harrington principle, although it proves $\mathrm{PH}_n$ for each standard $n$, and $\mathrm{PH}$ is true.
:::

:::proof{of=cor-ph-unprovable}
In $\mathrm I\Sigma_1$, $\mathrm{PH}$ implies $\forall x\ \forall c\ \exists y\ \iota_{\mathrm{PH}}(x, y, c)$: given $x$ and $c$, $\mathrm{PH}$ gives for each $n \leq c$ some $y_n$ with $[x, y_n] \to_* (c)^n_c$; by $\Sigma_1$ collection, which $\mathrm I\Sigma_1$ proves (:ref{#thm-fo-parsons}), these $y_n$ have a common bound $B$, and $y = \max(B, c, x)$ works by monotonicity. By part 1 of :ref{#thm-indicator-main} and :ref{#thm-ph-indicator}, $\mathrm{PA}$ does not prove this sentence, hence not $\mathrm{PH}$. The other statements are :ref{#thm-ph-true}.
:::

$\mathrm{PH}$ is a $\Pi_2$ sentence of finite combinatorics. It is not a disguised consistency statement, although it turns out to be equivalent to one: Paris and Harrington proved, in $\mathrm{PA}$, that $\mathrm{PH}$ is equivalent to the consistency of $\mathrm{PA}$ together with all true $\Pi_1$ sentences (the equivalence is provable in $\mathrm I\Sigma_1$), and Paris refined this level by level: $\mathrm I\Sigma_1$ proves that, for every $u \geq 1$, the principle for colorings of $(u + 1)$-sets is equivalent to the consistency of $\mathrm I\Sigma_u$ together with the true $\Pi_1$ sentences (Hájek and Pudlák, Theorem II.2.2 and Corollary II.2.3). Consequently the principle for colorings of $(u + 1)$-sets is provable in $\mathrm I\Sigma_{u+1}$ and not in $\mathrm I\Sigma_u$. We admit these refinements.

## Growth

:::corollary{#cor-ph-envelope}
Let $g(x)$ be the least $y$ with $[x, y] \to_* (x)^n_x$ for every $n \leq x$. Then $g$ is a computable total function, and for every function $f$ provably total in $\mathrm{PA}$ there is $n_0$ with $f(x) < g(x)$ for all $x \geq n_0$. In particular, $g$ is not provably total in $\mathrm{PA}$.
:::

:::proof{of=cor-ph-envelope}
$g$ is total since $\mathrm{PH}$ is true, and computable by search, since the relation is decidable. Let $f$ be provably total in $\mathrm{PA}$. By part 2 of :ref{#thm-indicator-main}, there is $n_0$ with $\neg \iota_{\mathrm{PH}}(x, f(x), n_0)$ for every $x$. Let $x \geq n_0$, and suppose $f(x) \geq g(x)$. Then $\iota_{\mathrm{PH}}(x, g(x), x)$ holds, since $g(x) \geq x$ ($[x, y]$ must contain a set of $x$ elements), and by monotonicity $\iota_{\mathrm{PH}}(x, f(x), n_0)$: a contradiction. So $f(x) < g(x)$. If $g$ were provably total, this would give $g(x) < g(x)$.
:::

The function $g$ thus grows faster than every function provably total in $\mathrm{PA}$. Lesson 8 measures this growth on the scale of the hierarchy of $\alpha$-large sets: by the theorem of Ketonen and Solovay, $g$ grows like the function $F_{\varepsilon_0}$ of an ordinal hierarchy.

## Exercises

::::exercise{difficulty=2}
Show that $\mathrm{PA} + \mathrm{PH}$ proves that $g$ is total, and that $\mathrm{PA} \nvdash \forall x\ \exists y\ \iota_{\mathrm{PH}}(x, y, x)$.

:::solution
In $\mathrm{PA} + \mathrm{PH}$, given $x$, each $n \leq x$ has some $y_n$ with $[x, y_n] \to_* (x)^n_x$; $\Sigma_1$ collection gives a common bound, which works for all $n \leq x$ by monotonicity, and the least such $y$, which is at least $x$, exists by the least number principle. So $g$ is total. If $\mathrm{PA}$ proved $\forall x\ \exists y\ \iota_{\mathrm{PH}}(x, y, x)$, the least such $y$, which is $g(x)$, would be given by a $\Sigma_1$ formula provably defining a total function, contradicting :ref{#cor-ph-envelope}.
:::
::::

::::exercise{difficulty=2}
Let $M$ be a countable non-standard model of $\mathrm{PA}$ and $a \in M$ non-standard. Suppose $M \models \mathrm{PH}$. Show that there is a cut $I \subsetneq M$ with $a \in I$, $I \models \mathrm{PA}$ and $I \models \neg \mathrm{PH}$.

:::solution
$M \models \iota_{\mathrm{PH}}(a, b, a)$ for some $b$, and :ref{#thm-ph-indicator} gives a cut $I \models \mathrm{PA}$ with $a \in I$ and $b \notin I$. Take $b$ least with $\iota_{\mathrm{PH}}(a, b, a)$. Then $I$ does not satisfy $\iota_{\mathrm{PH}}(a, y, a)$ for any $y \in I$, by the argument of part 1 of :ref{#thm-indicator-main}; so $I \models \neg \mathrm{PH}$, since $\mathrm{PH}$ implies $\exists y\ \iota_{\mathrm{PH}}(a, y, a)$ in $\mathrm I\Sigma_1$.
:::
::::

## Notes and references

The unprovability of $\mathrm{PH}$ is due to Paris and Harrington (1977), who proved it through the equivalence with the reflection principle, with a model-theoretic proof of the unprovability by indiscernibles. The formulation through indicators is due to Paris and Kirby; Hájek and Pudlák give an indicator for $\mathrm{PA}$ through relative largeness in Chapter IV, Theorem 3.23, with Corollary 3.24 for unprovability and envelopes, and an indicator for $\mathrm B\Sigma_{k+1}$ in Theorem IV.3.20. The construction of the indiscernibles with a satisfaction table follows Kanamori and McAloon (1987), who work with diagonal indiscernibles for formulas of bounded complexity. Equivalences with consistency statements are in Hájek and Pudlák, Chapter II, Section 2.

- J. Paris and L. Harrington, *A mathematical incompleteness in Peano arithmetic*, in Handbook of Mathematical Logic, North-Holland, 1977, 1133–1142.
- A. Kanamori and K. McAloon, *On Gödel incompleteness and finite combinatorics*, Annals of Pure and Applied Logic 33 (1987), 23–41.
- P. Hájek and P. Pudlák, *Metamathematics of First-Order Arithmetic*, Springer, 1993, Chapter II, Section 2, and Chapter IV, Section 3(c).
