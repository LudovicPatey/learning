The halting probability $\Omega$ depends on the choice of the optimal machine, as the halting set depends on the enumeration of the machines. For the halting set, Myhill's theorem removes the dependence: all creative sets are computably isomorphic. This lesson proves the analogue for $\Omega$. Every random left-c.e. real is Solovay complete (Kučera and Slaman), and every Solovay complete left-c.e. real is the halting probability of some optimal machine (Calude, Hertling, Khoussainov and Wang). So the random left-c.e. reals, the Ω-like reals and the halting probabilities of optimal machines are the same reals, and every property of $\Omega$ that is invariant under Solovay equivalence holds for all of them.

The proof of the Kučera–Slaman theorem is a single test. To show that $\beta \leq_S \alpha$, one bets that the approximation of $\alpha$ moves at least as much as that of $\beta$, up to a factor $2^n$. Each time the bet fails, an interval of length $2^{-n}$ times the movement of $\beta$ is placed just above the current approximation of $\alpha$; the intervals have total length at most $2^{-n}\beta$. If the bet fails at almost every stage, $\alpha$ is covered, which a random real does not allow for every $n$.

## Ω-like reals are random

A left-c.e. real $\alpha$ is **Ω-like** if $\beta \leq_S \alpha$ for every left-c.e. real $\beta$; by :ref{#thm-omega-complete} and transitivity, this holds if and only if $\Omega \leq_S \alpha$. By :ref{#cor-above-random}, every Ω-like real is random, because $\Omega$ is.

The randomness of $\Omega$ does not depend on the optimal machine: the proof of :ref{#thm-omega} uses only the optimality of $U$, so for every optimal prefix-free machine $V$, the halting probability $\Omega_V$ is random and Turing equivalent to $\emptyset'$. So halting probabilities of optimal machines are random left-c.e. reals.

## Random left-c.e. reals are Ω-like

We use the correspondence between intervals and classes: for rationals $a < b$, the class $\{ X : a < 0.X < b \}$ is generated by the strings $\sigma$ with $[0.\sigma, 0.\sigma + 2^{-|\sigma|}] \subseteq (a, b)$, so it is $\Sigma^0_1$ uniformly in $a$ and $b$, and its measure is at most $b - a$. A union of intervals of total length $\ell$ thus gives a $\Sigma^0_1$ class of measure at most $\ell$.

:::theorem[Kučera, Slaman]{#thm-kucera-slaman}
Every Martin-Löf random left-c.e. real is Ω-like. More precisely, if $\alpha$ is a random left-c.e. real and $\beta$ is a left-c.e. real, with approximations $(\alpha_s)$ and $(\beta_s)$, there are $n$ and a computable increasing sequence of stages $s_0 < s_1 < \cdots$ such that
$$
\beta_{s_i} - \beta_{s_{i-1}} \leq 2^n (\alpha_{s_{i+1}} - \alpha_{s_i}) \quad \text{for every } i \geq 1.
$$
:::

:::proof{of=thm-kucera-slaman}
We may assume that $\beta \leq 1$, replacing $\beta$ by $2^{-k}\beta$ if necessary. For each $n$, build a $\Sigma^0_1$ class $\mathcal U_n$ in stages, as a finite union of open intervals at each stage. At stage $s$, if $\alpha_s$ is not in $\mathcal U_n$ as built so far, call $s$ an **active** stage, let $t$ be the last active stage before $s$ ($t = 0$ if there is none), and add the interval, possibly empty,
$$
\big( \alpha_s,\ \alpha_s + 2^{-n}(\beta_s - \beta_t) \big).
$$
Otherwise do nothing. The construction is computable, uniformly in $n$.

*The classes form a Martin-Löf test.* The active stages form a sequence $t_0 < t_1 < \cdots$, and the lengths of the intervals are $2^{-n}(\beta_{t_0} - \beta_0)$, $2^{-n}(\beta_{t_1} - \beta_{t_0})$, and so on, a telescoping sum. So the total length is at most $2^{-n} \beta \leq 2^{-n}$, and $\lambda(\mathcal U_n) \leq 2^{-n}$.

*Infinitely many active stages.* Since $\alpha$ is random, there is $n$ with $\alpha \notin \mathcal U_n$. Fix such an $n$. If there were only finitely many active stages, then from some stage on, every $\alpha_s$ would belong to a fixed finite union of open intervals with rational endpoints. The limit $\alpha$ would be in the closure of this union, and not an endpoint, since $\alpha$ is irrational (a random real is not computable). So $\alpha$ would be in the union, hence in $\mathcal U_n$, a contradiction.

*The inequality.* Let $s_0 < s_1 < \cdots$ be the active stages, a computable sequence. At stage $s_i$, for $i \geq 1$, the interval $(\alpha_{s_i}, \alpha_{s_i} + 2^{-n}(\beta_{s_i} - \beta_{s_{i-1}}))$ is added. At stage $s_{i+1}$, the approximation $\alpha_{s_{i+1}} > \alpha_{s_i}$ is not in $\mathcal U_n$, in particular not in this interval; so $\alpha_{s_{i+1}} \geq \alpha_{s_i} + 2^{-n}(\beta_{s_i} - \beta_{s_{i-1}})$, which is the inequality.

*Ω-likeness.* Summing the inequality for $i \geq k$, with $k \geq 1$,
$$
\beta - \beta_{s_{k-1}} \leq 2^n (\alpha - \alpha_{s_k}).
$$
Given $m$, let $k \geq 1$ be least with $s_k \geq m$, and $g(m) = s_{k-1}$. Then $\beta - \beta_{g(m)} \leq 2^n(\alpha - \alpha_{s_k}) \leq 2^n(\alpha - \alpha_m) < 2^{n+1}(\alpha - \alpha_m)$. By :ref{#prop-solovay-approximations}, $\beta \leq_S \alpha$.
:::

:::quiz[the Kučera–Slaman theorem]
In the proof of :ref{#thm-kucera-slaman}, why is the interval placed just above $\alpha_s$ rather than around $\alpha_s$?

- [x] Because $\alpha$ is approximated from below: an interval above $\alpha_s$ is crossed by the later approximations unless they stop in it, and crossing it forces the approximation of $\alpha$ to move by its length.
- [ ] Because intervals around $\alpha_s$ would not be $\Sigma^0_1$.

  Open intervals with rational endpoints give $\Sigma^0_1$ classes wherever they are placed.
- [ ] To keep the total length of the intervals below $2^{-n}$.

  The total length is bounded by the telescoping sum of the movements of $\beta$, wherever the intervals are placed.

---

Where is the randomness of $\alpha$ used?

- [x] To find $n$ with $\alpha \notin \mathcal U_n$; without randomness, $\alpha$ could belong to every $\mathcal U_n$, and the approximation of $\alpha$ could stop moving while that of $\beta$ continues.
- [ ] To show that the classes $\mathcal U_n$ have small measure.

  The bound on the measure holds for every left-c.e. real $\alpha$; it only depends on $\beta$.
- [ ] To show that $(\alpha_s)$ converges to $\alpha$.

  This is the definition of an approximation.
:::

So for left-c.e. reals, randomness is a property of the speed of approximations: a left-c.e. real is random if and only if its approximation converges at least as slowly as that of every other left-c.e. real, up to a constant factor.

## Halting probabilities of optimal machines

:::theorem[Calude, Hertling, Khoussainov, Wang]{#thm-chkw}
Let $\alpha \in (0, 1)$ be a random left-c.e. real. Then $\alpha = \Omega_V$ for an optimal prefix-free machine $V$.
:::

The idea is to write $\alpha$ as $2^{-c}\Omega + \gamma$ with $\gamma$ left-c.e. Then $V$ simulates $U$ on the descriptions beginning with a fixed string of length $c$, which contributes $2^{-c}\Omega$ to its halting probability and makes it optimal, and uses the rest of its domain to add $\gamma$. That $\gamma = \alpha - 2^{-c}\Omega$ is left-c.e. is where the theorem of Kučera and Slaman is used: the approximation of $\alpha$ moves at least as much as that of $2^{-c}\Omega$.

:::proof{of=thm-chkw}
By :ref{#thm-kucera-slaman} with $\beta = \Omega$, there are $n$ and stages $s_0 < s_1 < \cdots$ with $\Omega_{s_i} - \Omega_{s_{i-1}} \leq 2^n(\alpha_{s_{i+1}} - \alpha_{s_i})$ for $i \geq 1$. Let $a_i = \alpha_{s_{i+1}}$ and $b_i = \Omega_{s_i}$, two computable non-decreasing sequences with limits $\alpha$ and $\Omega$, such that $b_i - b_{i-1} \leq 2^n (a_i - a_{i-1})$ for $i \geq 1$. We may assume that $\alpha_s > 0$ for every $s$, dropping finitely many terms, since $\alpha > 0$. Choose $c \geq n$ with $2^{-c} b_0 \leq a_0$ and $\alpha + 2^{-c} \leq 1$, which is possible since $a_0 > 0$ and $\alpha < 1$.

Then $\gamma = \alpha - 2^{-c}\Omega$ is the limit of $a_i - 2^{-c} b_i$, and
$$
(a_i - 2^{-c} b_i) - (a_{i-1} - 2^{-c} b_{i-1}) = (a_i - a_{i-1}) - 2^{-c}(b_i - b_{i-1}) \geq (1 - 2^{n - c})(a_i - a_{i-1}) \geq 0.
$$
So $\gamma$ is left-c.e., and $\gamma \geq a_0 - 2^{-c} b_0 \geq 0$. As in the proof of :ref{#thm-left-ce-halting}, write $\gamma = \sum_i 2^{-n_i}$ for a computable sequence $(n_i)$. The requests $(\varepsilon, c)$, issued first, and $(\varepsilon, n_i)$ for all $i$, form a bounded request set, of weight $2^{-c} + \gamma \leq 2^{-c} + \alpha \leq 1$. Let $\rho$ be the string of length $c$ assigned to the first request by the construction of :ref{#thm-kc}, and $D$ the set of strings assigned to the other requests; $\{\rho\} \cup D$ is prefix-free, and the weight of $D$ is $\gamma$. Let $V(\rho\tau) = U(\tau)$ for every $\tau$, and $V(\sigma) = \varepsilon$ for $\sigma \in D$. The domain of $V$ is $\rho\, \dom U \cup D$, which is prefix-free, and
$$
\Omega_V = 2^{-c}\Omega + \gamma = \alpha.
$$
$V$ is optimal, since $K_V(\sigma) \leq K(\sigma) + c$ for every $\sigma$.
:::

Combining the three results, for a left-c.e. real $\alpha \in (0, 1)$, the following are equivalent:

1. $\alpha$ is Martin-Löf random;
2. $\alpha$ is Ω-like;
3. $\alpha = \Omega_V$ for some optimal prefix-free machine $V$.

## Consequences

:::corollary{#cor-random-left-ce}
1. Any two random left-c.e. reals $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are Solovay equivalent, and $K(\alpha \uh n) =^+ K(\beta \uh n)$.
2. (Demuth) If $\alpha$ is a random left-c.e. real and $\beta$ is a left-c.e. real with $\alpha + \beta < 1$, then $\alpha + \beta$ is random.
3. Every random left-c.e. real is weak truth-table equivalent to $\emptyset'$.
:::

:::proof{of=cor-random-left-ce}
1. Both are Ω-like by :ref{#thm-kucera-slaman}, so each is Solovay below the other, and :ref{#thm-solovay-complexity} applies in both directions.
2. $\Omega \leq_S \alpha \leq_S \alpha + \beta$, by :ref{#thm-kucera-slaman} and :ref{#prop-solovay-join}, so $\alpha + \beta$ is random by :ref{#cor-above-random}.
3. Every left-c.e. real is weak truth-table reducible to $\emptyset'$ (lesson :ref{to="approximations"}). Conversely, by :ref{#thm-chkw}, $\alpha = \Omega_V$ for an optimal prefix-free machine $V$, and the domain of $V$ is weak truth-table reducible to $\Omega_V$ by :ref{#prop-omega-m-domain}. The proof of :ref{#thm-omega} computes $\emptyset'$ from the domain of an optimal machine by querying the strings of length at most $e + 1 + d$ to decide whether $e \in \emptyset'$, a computable bound; so $\emptyset' \leq_{wtt} \dom V \leq_{wtt} \alpha$.
:::

Part 1 concerns the oscillations of complexity. The complexity $K(\alpha \uh n)$ of a random sequence oscillates between about $n$ and $n + K(n)$, and part 1 says that the oscillations are the same, up to a constant, for all random left-c.e. reals: they are all simple and all complex at the same lengths. Part 2 contrasts with :ref{#ex-sum-not-random}: the sum of two random reals need not be random, but adding a left-c.e. real to a random left-c.e. real keeps it random.

::::exercise[Sums of random reals]{#ex-sum-not-random difficulty=1 objective="See that the randomness of sums of left-c.e. reals is special to left-c.e. reals."}
Show that if $\alpha$ is random, then $\beta = 1 - \alpha$ is random, while $\alpha + \beta = 1$ is not. Show that if $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are random left-c.e. reals, then $\alpha\beta$ is random, and $\alpha + \beta$ is random if it is less than $1$.

:::solution
The map $X \mapsto$ the binary expansion of $1 - 0.X$ is, on non-dyadic reals, the bitwise complement, which preserves the uniform measure and maps $\Sigma^0_1$ classes to $\Sigma^0_1$ classes uniformly; so it maps random sequences to random sequences, and $1 - \alpha$ is random. The rational $1$ is computable, hence not random. If $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are random left-c.e. reals, then $\Omega \leq_S \alpha \leq_S \alpha\beta$ by :ref{#thm-kucera-slaman} and :ref{#ex-solovay-product}, so $\alpha\beta$ is random by :ref{#cor-above-random}; the sum is :ref{#cor-random-left-ce} (2).
:::

## Notes and references

The Kučera–Slaman theorem is from their 2001 paper; Kučera and Slaman note that a version is in Demuth's work (1975). The characterization of Ω-like reals as halting probabilities is due to Calude, Hertling, Khoussainov and Wang (2001), who also showed that halting probabilities of optimal machines are Ω-like; Solovay had shown that Ω-like reals are random. Downey and Hirschfeldt present these results in Section 9.2 (Proposition 9.2.1, Theorems 9.2.2 and 9.2.3), and Nies in Section 3.2. Kučera and Slaman also showed that the measures $\lambda(\mathcal U_n)$, $n \geq 1$, of the components of a universal Martin-Löf test are Ω-like.

- A. Kučera and T. A. Slaman, *Randomness and recursive enumerability*, SIAM Journal on Computing 31 (2001), 199–211.
- C. S. Calude, P. H. Hertling, B. Khoussainov and Y. Wang, *Recursively enumerable reals and Chaitin Ω numbers*, Theoretical Computer Science 255 (2001), 125–149.
- O. Demuth, *On constructive pseudonumbers*, Commentationes Mathematicae Universitatis Carolinae 16 (1975), 315–331.
- R. G. Downey and D. R. Hirschfeldt, *Algorithmic Randomness and Complexity*, Springer, 2010, Section 9.2.
- A. Nies, *Computability and Randomness*, Oxford University Press, 2009, Section 3.2.

::::exercise{#ex-omega-halves-optimal difficulty=1 objective="Apply the characterization of halting probabilities of optimal machines."}
Show that $\Omega/2$ and $\Omega/2 + 1/4$ are halting probabilities of optimal prefix-free machines, and that $\Omega/2 + 1/4 \equiv_S \Omega$.

:::solution
Both reals are left-c.e. and in $(0, 1)$. They are Solovay equivalent to $\Omega$, by :ref{#ex-solovay-examples} (2) for $\Omega/2$ and :ref{#ex-solovay-rational-shift} for the translation by $1/4$; so they are Ω-like, hence random by :ref{#cor-above-random}. By :ref{#thm-chkw}, each is $\Omega_V$ for an optimal prefix-free machine $V$.
:::

::::exercise{#ex-omega-like-turing difficulty=1 objective="Derive Turing completeness of Ω-like reals from Solovay completeness."}
Show without :ref{#thm-chkw} that every Ω-like real is Turing equivalent to $\emptyset'$.

:::solution
If $\alpha$ is Ω-like, then $\Omega \leq_S \alpha$, so $\emptyset' \leq_T \Omega \leq_T \alpha$ by :ref{#thm-omega} and :ref{#prop-solovay-turing}. Conversely, $\alpha$ is left-c.e., so $\alpha \leq_T \emptyset'$, by :ref{#prop-cut-expansion}: its left cut is c.e.
:::

::::exercise{#ex-optimal-different-omega difficulty=1 objective="See that halting probabilities of optimal machines take many values."}
Show that the halting probabilities of the optimal prefix-free machines are dense in $[0, 1]$.

:::solution
Let $0 \leq a < b \leq 1$ be rationals. Choose $c$ with $2^{-c+1} < b - a$, and a dyadic rational $d$ with $a < d < b - 2^{-c}$, which exists since this interval has length more than $2^{-c}$. The real $2^{-c}\Omega$ is left-c.e. and random, by :ref{#cor-above-random}, since $\Omega \leq_S 2^{-c}\Omega$ (:ref{#ex-solovay-examples}). The real $\alpha = d + 2^{-c}\Omega$ lies in $(d, d + 2^{-c}) \subseteq (a, b)$, so $\alpha < 1$, and it is random by :ref{#cor-random-left-ce} (2), applied to the random left-c.e. real $2^{-c}\Omega$ and the left-c.e. real $d$. By :ref{#thm-chkw}, $\alpha$ is the halting probability of an optimal prefix-free machine.
:::

::::exercise[Slow convergence]{#ex-slow-convergence difficulty=2 objective="Express the randomness of a left-c.e. real as the slowness of its approximation, with a Solovay test."}
Let $\alpha$ be a random left-c.e. real with approximation $(\alpha_s)$, and let $h$ be a computable function. Show that there is a constant $c$ with $\alpha - \alpha_{h(n)} \geq 2^{-K(n) - c}$ for every $n$.

:::solution
Let $K_s$ be the approximation of $K$ from above. For each $n$, the open intervals $(\alpha_{h(n)}, \alpha_{h(n)} + 2^{-K_s(n)})$ increase with $s$, and their union is $I_n = (\alpha_{h(n)}, \alpha_{h(n)} + 2^{-K(n)})$. The corresponding classes of sequences are $\Sigma^0_1$ uniformly in $n$, as unions of uniformly $\Sigma^0_1$ classes, and have measure at most $2^{-K(n)}$. Since $\sum_n 2^{-K(n)} \leq 1$, they form a Solovay test. As $\alpha$ is random, $\alpha$ belongs to only finitely many $I_n$, by :ref{#thm-solovay}. So $\alpha - \alpha_{h(n)} \geq 2^{-K(n)}$ for all but finitely many $n$; for the finitely many others, $\alpha - \alpha_{h(n)} > 0$, and a constant $c$ takes care of them.

In words, a random left-c.e. real cannot be approximated, at the computable stages $h(n)$, with a precision better than $2^{-K(n)}$, the probability of $n$. For a computable real, the precision $2^{-n}$ can be reached at computable stages (:ref{#ex-computable-modulus}).
:::
