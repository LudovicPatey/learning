Ramsey's theorem for pairs has no low basis, and not even $\Delta^0_2$ solutions (:ref{#thm-jockusch-no-delta2}). Cholak, Jockusch and Slaman proved that it admits a weakly low basis, and therefore low₂ solutions. Through the decomposition of :ref{#thm-rt22-decomposition}, the proof splits into cohesiveness, treated in the previous lesson, and the pigeonhole principle for $\Delta^0_2$ sets, treated in this lesson; the next lesson combines them. For the pigeonhole principle, the reservoirs cannot be computable: the arguments of strong cone avoidance and of Seetapun's theorem use reservoirs in a Scott ideal, chosen as one side of a partition taken from the ideal. This lesson runs the Dzhafarov–Jockusch forcing effectively, with reservoirs named by their indices in a low code of a Scott ideal (:ref{#cor-scott-codes}), the forcing question answered by $\emptyset'$, and the choice of the side of the partition made by a set PA relative to $\emptyset'$ (:ref{#lem-pa-choice}).

## The pigeonhole principle for Δ⁰₂ sets

:::theorem[Cholak, Jockusch, Slaman]{#thm-d22-weakly-low}
Let $Z$ be a set, $A$ a $\Delta^0_2(Z)$ set, and $P$ a set PA relative to $Z'$. There is an infinite set $G$, contained in $A$ or in $\overline A$, with $(G \oplus Z)' \leq_T P$. So $\mathsf D^2_2$ admits a weakly low basis.
:::

We write the proof for $Z = \emptyset$; the general case is discussed after it. Write $A^0 = A$ and $A^1 = \overline A$. By :ref{#cor-scott-codes}, fix a low code $M = \bigoplus_n Z_n$ of a Scott ideal $\mathcal M$, which is a member of the Scott class $\mathcal S_\emptyset$, with the index functions $\mathrm{join}$, $\mathrm{red}$ and $\mathrm{pa}$ of :ref{#thm-scott-class}. If some infinite subset $H$ of $A^0$ or of $A^1$ belongs to $\mathcal M$, then $H$ is low, so $H' \leq_T \emptyset' \leq_T P$, and the theorem holds. We assume from now on:

(H) no infinite subset of $A^0$ or of $A^1$ belongs to $\mathcal M$.

**Conditions** are triples $(\sigma_0, \sigma_1, X)$ where $X \in \mathcal M$ is infinite with $\min X \geq |\sigma_0|, |\sigma_1|$, and the finite set of elements of $\sigma_i$ is contained in $A^i$, for each $i < 2$. A condition $(\tau_0, \tau_1, Y)$ extends $(\sigma_0, \sigma_1, X)$ if $(\tau_i, Y)$ extends $(\sigma_i, X)$ as Mathias conditions for each $i$ (:ref{#def-mathias-condition}). The **members of side $i$** of $(\sigma_0, \sigma_1, X)$ are the sets satisfying the Mathias condition $(\sigma_i, X)$. This is the disjunctive notion of forcing of the proof of :ref{#thm-dj-cone-avoidance} (see :ref{#def-disjunctive-notion}), with the ideal $\mathcal M$ fixed. A condition is **coded** by $\langle \sigma_0, \sigma_1, a \rangle$, where $a$ is an $M$-index of $X$.

Two operations on codes are used repeatedly. From an $M$-index $a$ of $X$ and a number $n$, an $M$-index of $X \setminus \{0, \dots, n\}$ is computable, namely $\mathrm{red}(a, e_n)$ for an index $e_n$, computable from $n$, of the functional that removes the numbers up to $n$ from its oracle. From $M$-indices $a$ of $X$ and $c$ of $B$, $M$-indices of $X \cap B$ and $X \setminus B$ are computable, as reductions applied to the column $\mathrm{join}(a, c)$.

:::lemma{#lem-d22-infinity}
Assume (H). For every condition $(\sigma_0, \sigma_1, X)$, every $i < 2$ and every $m$, the set $X \cap A^i$ has an element $n \geq m$. The oracle $\emptyset'$ computes, from a code of the condition and $i$, $m$, a code of an extension $(\tau_0, \tau_1, Y)$ such that $\tau_i$ has an element $n \geq m$.
:::

:::proof{of=lem-d22-infinity}
If $X \cap A^i$ were contained in $\{0, \dots, m - 1\}$, the set $X \setminus \{0, \dots, m\}$ would be an infinite subset of $A^{1-i}$, and a member of $\mathcal M$, contradicting (H). The oracle $\emptyset'$ computes $M$, since $M$ is low, hence $X$; and it computes $A$, which is $\Delta^0_2$. So it finds the least $n \geq \max(m, |\sigma_i|)$ in $X \cap A^i$. Let $\tau_i$ extend $\sigma_i$ by zeros up to position $n$ and have $\tau_i(n) = 1$, let $\tau_{1-i} = \sigma_{1-i}$, and $Y = X \setminus \{0, \dots, n\}$. The new element $n$ lies in $X \cap A^i$, so this is an extension, with a code computed from the old one.
:::

The jump is decided by the disjunctive question of Dzhafarov and Jockusch, about all partitions of the reservoir. Recall that a string $\tau$ is compatible with a pair $(\sigma, W)$ if $\sigma \preceq \tau$ and every element of $\tau$ that is at least $|\sigma|$ belongs to $W$; this makes sense even if $W$ is finite.

:::lemma{#lem-d22-decision}
Assume (H), and let $P$ be PA relative to $\emptyset'$. For every condition $c = (\sigma_0, \sigma_1, X)$ and all $e_0, e_1$, there are an extension $d$ of $c$, a side $i < 2$ and a bit $b$ such that every member $G$ of side $i$ of $d$ satisfies $\Phi^G_{e_i}(e_i)\converge$ if $b = 1$, and $\Phi^G_{e_i}(e_i)\diverge$ if $b = 0$. The oracle $P$ computes a code of $d$, the side $i$ and the bit $b$ from a code of $c$ and $e_0$, $e_1$.
:::

:::proof{of=lem-d22-decision}
For a set $B$, write $B^0 = B$ and $B^1 = \overline B$. Let $\mathcal Q$ be the class of sets $B$ such that, for each $i < 2$, no string $\tau$ compatible with $(\sigma_i, X \cap B^i)$ satisfies $\Phi^\tau_{e_i}(e_i)\converge$. A set $B$ fails to belong to $\mathcal Q$ if and only if some $i$, some $\tau$ and some stage $s$ witness a convergence $\Phi^\tau_{e_i}(e_i)[s]\converge$ with $\tau$ compatible with $(\sigma_i, X \cap B^i)$, and this compatibility depends only on $X$ and on $B \uh |\tau|$. So $\mathcal Q$ is a $\Pi^0_1(X)$ class, with an index computable from the code of $c$ and $e_0$, $e_1$. Its emptiness is a $\Sigma^0_1(X)$ property, uniformly in the $M$-index of $X$ (:ref{#prop-pi01-emptiness}), so $\emptyset'$ decides it, since $M$ is low (:ref{#prop-low-code-questions}).

*Case 1: $\mathcal Q$ is empty.* In particular $A \notin \mathcal Q$: there are $i < 2$ and a string $\tau$ compatible with $(\sigma_i, X \cap A^i)$ with $\Phi^\tau_{e_i}(e_i)\converge$. The oracle $\emptyset'$, which computes $X$ and $A$, finds such $i$ and $\tau$ by a search, which terminates. Let $d = (\tau_0, \tau_1, Y)$ with $\tau_i = \tau$, $\tau_{1-i} = \sigma_{1-i}$, and $Y = X \setminus \{0, \dots, |\tau|\}$. The elements of $\tau$ added to $\sigma_i$ lie in $X \cap A^i$, so $d$ is an extension of $c$. Every member of side $i$ of $d$ extends $\tau$, so it satisfies $\Phi^G_{e_i}(e_i)\converge$. Return $d$, $i$ and $b = 1$.

*Case 2: $\mathcal Q$ is non-empty.* The class $\mathcal Q$ is the set of paths of an infinite tree computable in $X$, with an index computable from the code of $c$; by :ref{#lem-scott-paths}, an $M$-index of a member $B \in \mathcal Q \cap \mathcal M$ is computable from the code of $c$. The sentences "$X \cap B^0$ is infinite" and "$X \cap B^1$ is infinite" are $\Pi^0_2(M)$, uniformly in the indices of $X$ and $B$, hence $\Pi^0_2$ by :ref{#prop-low-code-questions}, with codes computable from the code of $c$. At least one is true, since $X$ is infinite. By :ref{#lem-pa-choice}, $P$ names an $i$ such that $X \cap B^i$ is infinite. Let $d = (\sigma_0, \sigma_1, X \cap B^i)$, an extension of $c$, whose reservoir has an $M$-index computable from those of $X$ and $B$. If a member $G$ of side $i$ of $d$ had $\Phi^G_{e_i}(e_i)\converge$ with use $u$, the prefix of $G$ of length $\max(u, |\sigma_i|)$ would be compatible with $(\sigma_i, X \cap B^i)$ and make the computation converge, contradicting $B \in \mathcal Q$. Return $d$, $i$ and $b = 0$.
:::

The relation "$c \mathrel{?{\vdash}} \Phi^{G_0}_{e_0}(e_0)\converge \vee \Phi^{G_1}_{e_1}(e_1)\converge$ if $\mathcal Q$ is empty" is the disjunctive forcing question of the Dzhafarov–Jockusch notion (first exercise of :ref{to="avoidance/disjunctive-question"}), and it is $\Sigma^0_1(X)$ for a reservoir $X$ of $\mathcal M$. For cone avoidance, this definability was used to obtain a contradiction from a reduction of $C$ to $X$; here it is used, through the lowness of the code, to let $\emptyset'$ answer the question. The set $P$ is used once: to name an infinite side of the partition $B$ in case 2.

:::proof{of=thm-d22-weakly-low}
Let $a_0$ be an $M$-index of $\nat$, which is computable: $\nat = Z_{\mathrm{red}(0, e)}$ for an index $e$ of the constant function $1$. Starting from the condition $c_0 = (\varepsilon, \varepsilon, \nat)$, build a $P$-computable sequence of codes of conditions $c_0 \geq c_1 \geq \cdots$: at step $s$, apply :ref{#lem-d22-infinity} for $i = 0$ and $i = 1$ with $m = s$, and then :ref{#lem-d22-decision} for the pair $(e_0, e_1)$ with $\langle e_0, e_1 \rangle = s$. Record the side $f(e_0, e_1)$ and the bit $h(e_0, e_1)$ given by the second lemma; $f$ and $h$ are computable in $P$.

Write $c_s = (\sigma_{0,s}, \sigma_{1,s}, X_s)$, and let $G_i = \bigcup_s \sigma_{i,s}$. Each $G_i$ is infinite, by the infinity steps, and contained in $A^i$, since every $\sigma_{i,s}$ is. For every $s$, $G_i$ is a member of side $i$ of $c_s$: it extends $\sigma_{i,s}$, and its later elements are taken from the reservoirs $X_t \subseteq X_s$, $t \geq s$. So if the pair $(e_0, e_1)$ is decided on side $i = f(e_0, e_1)$, then $G_i' (e_i) = h(e_0, e_1)$.

Two cases remain, illustrated by the figure. If for every $e_0$ there is $e_1$ with $f(e_0, e_1) = 0$, then $G_0'$ is computable in $P$: given $e$, search for $e_1$ with $f(e, e_1) = 0$, and output $h(e, e_1)$. Otherwise there is $e_0^*$ with $f(e_0^*, e_1) = 1$ for every $e_1$, and $G_1'(e) = h(e_0^*, e)$ for every $e$, so $G_1'$ is computable in $P$. In either case some $G_i$ is an infinite subset of $A^i$ with $G_i' \leq_T P$.
:::

The final side is not computed by the construction. The construction is uniform in $P$, but which of $G_0$ and $G_1$ has a jump computed by $P$ depends on the whole table $f$, a $\Sigma^0_2(P)$ property. This is the same non-uniformity as in Lachlan's disjunction for cone avoidance (:ref{#lem-disjunctive-construction}).

For a set $Z$, the same proof works with a code $M$ of a Scott ideal containing $Z$ with $M' \leq_T Z'$ (:ref{#cor-scott-codes}), a $\Delta^0_2(Z)$ set $A$, functionals with oracle $G \oplus Z$, and the oracles $Z'$ and $P$ in place of $\emptyset'$ and $P$: :ref{#prop-low-code-questions} and :ref{#lem-pa-choice} relativize. If (H) fails, an infinite subset $H$ of $A$ or $\overline A$ in $\mathcal M$ satisfies $(H \oplus Z)' \leq_T M' \leq_T Z' \leq_T P$.

## Exercises

::::exercise{difficulty=3}
Hirschfeldt presents the proof of :ref{#thm-d22-weakly-low} with **low reservoirs** instead of a low code: conditions $(\sigma_0, \sigma_1, X)$ with $X$ low and infinite, coded by a lowness index of $X$, under the assumption that no infinite low set is contained in $A$ or in $\overline A$. Adapt the proof of :ref{#lem-d22-decision} to this setting.

:::solution
Given a lowness index of $X$, the class $\mathcal Q$ is $\Pi^0_1(X)$ with an index computable from the code, and its emptiness is a $\Sigma^0_1(X)$ question, which $\emptyset'$ answers through the lowness index, by item 2 of :ref{#thm-jump}. In case 1, the search is the same. In case 2, :ref{#prop-uniform-low-basis} gives a lowness index of $X \oplus B$ for some $B \in \mathcal Q$, computably from the code; by the first exercise of :ref{to="lowness/forcing-the-jump"}, lowness indices of $X \cap B^0$ and $X \cap B^1$ are computable from it. The sentences "$X \cap B^i$ is infinite" are $\Pi^0_2(X \oplus B)$, hence $\Pi^0_1((X \oplus B)')$, and the lowness index turns them into $\Pi^0_1(\emptyset')$, that is, $\Pi^0_2$ sentences with computable codes. $P$ chooses an infinite side by :ref{#lem-pa-choice}, and its lowness index is the code of the new reservoir. The infinity lemma uses the new assumption: if $X \cap A^i$ were finite, a cofinite part of the low set $X$ would be an infinite low subset of $A^{1-i}$.
:::

## Notes and references

:ref{#thm-d22-weakly-low} is due to Cholak, Jockusch and Slaman (2001), who gave two proofs of its low₂ consequence: one by control of the second jump, and one by control of the first jump relative to a set PA relative to $\emptyset'$, the one given here. Hirschfeldt presents the second as Theorems 6.52 and 6.57, with low reservoirs; Dzhafarov and Mummert as Lemma 8.5.12, also with low reservoirs. The version with a low code of a Scott ideal follows the section *Ramsey's theorem for pairs* of the chapter *Lowness* of Patey's monograph. The forcing is that of Dzhafarov and Jockusch (2009), studied as a disjunctive notion of forcing in :ref{to="avoidance/disjunctive-question"}.

- D. D. Dzhafarov and C. G. Jockusch, Jr., *Ramsey's theorem and cone avoidance*, Journal of Symbolic Logic 74 (2009), 557–578.
- D. R. Hirschfeldt, *Slicing the Truth*, World Scientific, 2014, Section 6.6.
- D. D. Dzhafarov and C. Mummert, *Reverse Mathematics: Problems, Reductions, and Proofs*, Springer, 2022, Section 8.5.
- L. Patey, *Lowness and avoidance*, monograph, chapter *Lowness*, section *Ramsey's theorem for pairs*.
