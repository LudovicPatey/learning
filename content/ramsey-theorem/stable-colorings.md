The previous lesson studied the first of the two tasks into which Cholak, Jockusch and Slaman (2001) split Ramsey's theorem for pairs: finding a cohesive set. This lesson studies the second, finding a homogeneous set for a **stable** coloring, and shows that the two tasks together are equivalent to Ramsey's theorem for pairs. The decomposition reduces many questions about $\mathsf{RT}^2_2$ to simpler ones, and it is the starting point of the proof of Seetapun's theorem in the last two lessons.

## Stable colorings

:::definition{#def-stable-coloring}
A coloring $f : [\nat]^2 \to 2$ is **stable** if for every $x$, the limit $\lim_y f(\{ x, y \})$ exists. Its **limit set** is $A_f = \{ x : \lim_y f(\{ x, y \}) = 1 \}$. $\mathsf{SRT}^2_2$ is $\mathsf{RT}^2_2$ restricted to stable colorings.
:::

The limit set of a stable coloring is $\Delta^0_2(f)$, by the limit lemma; conversely, every $\Delta^0_2$ set is the limit set of a computable stable coloring: if $A(x) = \lim_s A_s(x)$, let $f(\{ x, y \}) = A_y(x)$ for $x < y$.

:::example{#ex-stable}
1. The parity coloring of :ref{#ex-rt-colorings} is not stable: for each $x$, the color of $\{ x, y \}$ alternates with the parity of $y$.
2. The coloring $h(\{ x, y \}) = 1$ if $y > m(x)$ in the proof of :ref{#prop-rt32-jump} is stable, with limit set $\nat$. It is computable in $\emptyset'$, and all its infinite homogeneous sets compute $\emptyset'$.
3. A computable stable coloring $f$ always has an infinite $\Delta^0_2$ homogeneous set, by :ref{#prop-srt-d22} below: one of $A_f$ and $\overline{A_f}$ is infinite, and both are $\Delta^0_2$. So the coloring of :ref{#thm-jockusch-no-delta2} is not stable.
:::

Item 3 shows that $\mathsf{SRT}^2_2$ is computably weaker than $\mathsf{RT}^2_2$: its computable instances have solutions below $\emptyset'$, which is not true for $\mathsf{RT}^2_2$.

## Stable Ramsey's theorem and Δ⁰₂ sets

For a stable coloring, a homogeneous set is essentially a set of elements with the same limit color.

:::definition{#def-d22}
$\mathsf D^2_2$ is the statement: for every stable coloring $f$, there is an infinite set $H$ contained in $A_f$ or in $\overline{A_f}$. Equivalently, every $\Delta^0_2$ set, given by an approximation, has an infinite subset of itself or of its complement.
:::

:::proposition{#prop-srt-d22}
For every stable coloring $f$:

1. every infinite set homogeneous for $f$ is contained in $A_f$ or in $\overline{A_f}$;
2. every infinite set $H \subseteq A_f$, or $H \subseteq \overline{A_f}$, computes together with $f$ an infinite homogeneous set for $f$.
:::

:::proof{of=prop-srt-d22}
1. Let $H$ be homogeneous with color $i$. For $x \in H$, $f(\{ x, y \}) = i$ for all $y \in H$ above $x$, so the limit of $f(\{ x, y \})$, which exists, is $i$: $x \in A_f$ if $i = 1$ and $x \in \overline{A_f}$ if $i = 0$.
2. Suppose $H \subseteq A_f$, the other case being symmetric. Define $x_0 = \min H$ and $x_{j+1}$ the least $y \in H$ with $y > x_j$ and $f(\{ x_i, y \}) = 1$ for all $i \leq j$. It exists: each $x_i$ belongs to $A_f$, so $f(\{ x_i, y \}) = 1$ for all large $y$, and there are finitely many $i \leq j$. The sequence is computable in $f \oplus H$, and $\{ x_0 < x_1 < \cdots \}$ is homogeneous with color $1$.
:::

So, on ω-models, $\mathsf{SRT}^2_2$ and $\mathsf D^2_2$ are equivalent. The equivalence over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ requires more care in item (2), where the existence of $x_{j+1}$ uses a bounding principle for $\Sigma^0_2$ formulas; Chong, Lempp and Yang (2010) showed that $\mathsf D^2_2$ implies this principle over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$, so the equivalence nevertheless holds.

From the point of view of combinatorics, $\mathsf D^2_2$ is the infinite pigeonhole principle $\mathsf{RT}^1_2$ applied to the limit set: the only difference is that the instance is given by an approximation, not by the set itself. Seetapun's theorem will be proved by showing that the pigeonhole principle has weak solutions for **every** instance, of any complexity.

:::quiz[stable colorings]

- [x] To guarantee that each chosen $x_i \in A_f$ has $f(\{ x_i, y \}) = 1$ for all large $y$, so that a common later element $y \in H$ exists.
- [ ] To show that $H$ is infinite.

  $H$ is infinite by hypothesis.
- [ ] To compute $A_f$ from $f$.

:::

## The decomposition

A cohesive set for the rows of a coloring makes it stable.

:::theorem[Cholak, Jockusch, Slaman]{#thm-rt22-decomposition}
For every coloring $f : [\nat]^2 \to 2$ and every set $C$ cohesive for the sequence $R_x = \{ y : f(\{ x, y \}) = 1 \}$, the restriction of $f$ to $[C]^2$ is stable. Consequently, over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$, $\mathsf{SRT}^2_2 + \mathsf{COH}$ implies $\mathsf{RT}^2_2$.
:::

:::proof{of=thm-rt22-decomposition}
Let $x \in C$. Since $C \subseteq^* R_x$ or $C \subseteq^* \overline{R_x}$, $f(\{ x, y \})$ is constant for all large $y \in C$: the limit along $C$ exists.

For the second statement, let $f$ be a coloring, and let $C$ be cohesive for the sequence $(R_x)$, which exists by $\Delta^0_1$ comprehension. Let $p$ be the increasing enumeration of $C$, and $g(\{ i, j \}) = f(\{ p(i), p(j) \})$; it is stable by the first part. By $\mathsf{SRT}^2_2$, $g$ has an infinite homogeneous set $H$, and $p[H] \subseteq C$ is homogeneous for $f$.
:::

:::quiz[the decomposition]
In the proof of :ref{#thm-rt22-decomposition}, why is the restriction of $f$ to a cohesive set stable?

- [x] Because cohesiveness for the set $R_x$ of $y$ with $f(\{ x, y \}) = 1$ means that the color of $\{ x, y \}$ is eventually constant for $y \in C$.
- [ ] Because $C$ is homogeneous for $f$.

  A cohesive set need not be homogeneous: different $x$ may have different limit colors.
- [ ] Because $C$ is computable in $f''$.

  The complexity of $C$ plays no role in stability.
:::

Conversely, $\mathsf{RT}^2_2$ implies $\mathsf{SRT}^2_2$ trivially, and it implies $\mathsf{COH}$ through a coloring that compares elements **lexicographically**. Given sets $R_0, R_1, \dots$, associate with each number $x$ the infinite sequence of bits $(R_0(x), R_1(x), \dots)$, and color a pair $x < y$ according to whether the sequence of $x$ is lexicographically larger or smaller than that of $y$. On a homogeneous set, the sequences of bits are lexicographically monotone, and a monotone sequence of bit sequences converges coordinate by coordinate: the first bit can change only once, the second only when the first changes, and so on. A set along which every bit converges is cohesive.

:::theorem[Cholak, Jockusch, Slaman; Mileti; Jockusch, Lempp]{#thm-rt22-coh}
Over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$, $\mathsf{RT}^2_2$ implies $\mathsf{COH}$.
:::

:::proof{of=thm-rt22-coh}
Let $\vec R$ be a sequence of sets. Replacing it by the sequence $R_0, \{ 0 \}, R_1, \{ 1 \}, R_2, \dots$, which exists by $\Delta^0_1$ comprehension and whose cohesive sets are $\vec R$-cohesive, we may assume that for all $x \neq y$ there is $i$ with $R_i(x) \neq R_i(y)$. Let $i(x, y)$ be the least such $i$, found by a bounded search. For $x < y$, let $f(\{ x, y \}) = 0$ if $x \in R_{i(x, y)}$, and $1$ otherwise. By $\mathsf{RT}^2_2$, let $H = \{ a_0 < a_1 < \cdots \}$ be infinite and homogeneous; suppose its color is $0$, the other case being symmetric with the roles of $0$ and $1$ exchanged.

Let $j_n = i(a_n, a_{n+1})$. Homogeneity gives $R_{j_n}(a_n) = 1$ and $R_{j_n}(a_{n+1}) = 0$, while $R_i(a_n) = R_i(a_{n+1})$ for $i < j_n$. Say that **bit $k$ drops at $n$** if $R_k(a_n) = 1$ and $R_k(a_{n+1}) = 0$, and **rises at $n$** if $R_k(a_n) = 0$ and $R_k(a_{n+1}) = 1$. If bit $k$ rises at $n$, then $k \neq j_n$, since bit $j_n$ drops, and $k \not< j_n$, since earlier bits do not change; so $j_n < k$, and bit $j_n$ drops at $n$.

We prove by induction on $k$ the statement $\varphi(k)$: for every $j \leq k$ and every finite set $F$, if bit $j$ rises at every $n \in F$ then $|F| < 2^j$, and if bit $j$ drops at every $n \in F$ then $|F| \leq 2^j$. It is a $\Pi^0_1$ formula with parameters $\vec R$ and $H$, since finite sets are coded by numbers, so the induction is available in $\mathsf{RCA}_0$. Assume $\varphi(j)$ for $j < k$.

- *Rises.* Let $F$ be a finite set of positions where bit $k$ rises. For $j < k$, the set $F_j = \{ n \in F : j_n = j \}$ consists of positions where bit $j$ drops, so $|F_j| \leq 2^j$. Since $F = \bigcup_{j < k} F_j$, $|F| \leq \sum_{j < k} 2^j < 2^k$.
- *Drops.* Let $n_0 < \cdots < n_{l-1}$ be positions where bit $k$ drops. Between two consecutive ones, bit $k$ goes from $0$ at $a_{n_t + 1}$ back to $1$ at $a_{n_{t+1}}$, so it rises at some $m$ with $n_t < m < n_{t+1}$. This gives $l - 1$ distinct rises, so $l - 1 < 2^k$ and $l \leq 2^k$.

So for each $k$, the set of positions $n$ at which bit $k$ changes, which exists by $\Delta^0_1$ comprehension, has fewer than $2^{k+1}$ elements in each of its finite subsets; it is therefore bounded, by some $N$. For $n > N$, $R_k(a_n) = R_k(a_{N+1})$. Hence $H \subseteq^* R_k$ or $H \subseteq^* \overline{R_k}$, and $H$ is cohesive.
:::

The natural argument would prove, by induction on $k$, that bit $k$ is eventually constant along $H$. That statement, "there is $N$ such that for all $n > N$ …", is $\Sigma^0_2$, and $\Sigma^0_2$ induction is not available in $\mathsf{RCA}_0$. Counting the changes replaces it by a $\Pi^0_1$ statement with an explicit bound. The same pattern, replacing an induction on a $\Sigma^0_2$ property by an induction on a bounded quantity, recurs in the course on induction and conservation.

:::quiz[RT²₂ implies COH]
In the proof of :ref{#thm-rt22-coh}, why can bit $k$ rise at a position $n$ only if some bit $j < k$ drops at $n$?

- [x] Because $j_n$ is the first index at which $a_n$ and $a_{n+1}$ differ, and homogeneity with color $0$ makes bit $j_n$ drop; a rise of bit $k$ is a difference, so $k > j_n$.
- [ ] Because $H$ is cohesive.

  Cohesiveness is the conclusion of the proof, not a hypothesis.
- [ ] Because each bit changes at most once.

  Bit $k$ may change up to $2^{k+1} - 1$ times; only bit $0$ drops at most once.
:::

:::corollary[Cholak, Jockusch, Slaman]{#cor-rt22-equivalence}
Over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$, $\mathsf{RT}^2_2$ is equivalent to $\mathsf{SRT}^2_2 + \mathsf{COH}$.
:::

:::proof{of=cor-rt22-equivalence}
:ref{#thm-rt22-decomposition} and :ref{#thm-rt22-coh}, with the trivial implication from $\mathsf{RT}^2_2$ to $\mathsf{SRT}^2_2$.
:::

## Exercises

::::exercise{difficulty=2}
Show that for every computable coloring $f : [\nat]^2 \to 2$, there is an infinite homogeneous set computable in $\emptyset''$, using the decomposition: a cohesive set computable in $\emptyset''$ and a solution to the induced stable coloring.

:::solution
By :ref{#prop-cohesive-exists}, the computable sequence $R_x = \{ y : f(\{ x, y \}) = 1 \}$ has a cohesive set $C \leq_T \emptyset''$. Its construction also tells, computably in $\emptyset''$, for each $x$ whether $C \subseteq^* R_x$ or $C \subseteq^* \overline{R_x}$: this is the decision $P(x)$. So the set $A$ of $x \in C$ such that $f(\{ x, y \}) = 1$ for almost all $y \in C$ is computable in $\emptyset''$, and so is $C \setminus A$. One of them is infinite; call it $H$. The restriction of $f$ to $C$ is stable (:ref{#thm-rt22-decomposition}), $H$ is contained in its limit set or in the complement, and :ref{#prop-srt-d22} (2), applied to the restriction of $f$ to $C$, gives an infinite homogeneous set computable in $f \oplus C \oplus H \leq_T \emptyset''$.
:::
::::

::::exercise{difficulty=1}
Show that every infinite homogeneous set for the coloring in the proof of :ref{#thm-rt22-coh} is cohesive directly, without counting, when the first-order part is the standard $\nat$.

:::solution
Suppose $H$ has color $0$. By induction on $k$, bit $k$ is eventually constant along $a_0 < a_1 < \cdots$. Assume bits $j < k$ are constant for $n \geq N$. A rise of bit $k$ at $n \geq N$ would require a drop of some bit $j < k$ at $n$, which does not happen. So after $N$, bit $k$ only drops, at most once, and is eventually constant. Each bit is eventually constant, so $H$ is cohesive. This induction is on a $\Sigma^0_2$ statement, which is why the proof over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ counts the changes instead.
:::

## Notes and references

Stable colorings and the decomposition are due to Cholak, Jockusch and Slaman (2001), whose paper initiated the modern reverse mathematics of Ramsey's theorem; the correspondence between stable colorings and $\Delta^0_2$ sets goes back to Jockusch (1972) (Hirschfeldt, §6.4, Definition 6.28 and Exercises 6.29 and 6.31; Dzhafarov–Mummert, §§8.4.1 and 8.5, Theorem 8.5.1). The proof that $\mathsf{RT}^2_2$ implies $\mathsf{COH}$ given by Cholak, Jockusch and Slaman used $\Sigma^0_2$ induction; Mileti, and independently Jockusch and Lempp, removed it (Hirschfeldt, Theorem 6.32, whose proof we follow; Monin and Patey, section *Cohesiveness principle*). Chong, Lempp and Yang proved that $\mathsf D^2_2$ implies $\Sigma^0_2$ bounding (Dzhafarov–Mummert, Theorem 8.4.9 and Corollary 8.4.11).
