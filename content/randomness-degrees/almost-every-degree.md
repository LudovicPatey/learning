A property of sequences holds for **almost every degree** if the class of sequences whose degree has it has measure $1$. Such properties hold for all sufficiently random sequences, and randomness gives a measure of how random a sequence must be. Two examples are proved here. Martin showed that almost every degree is hyperimmune, and Kautz that every 2-random sequence has hyperimmune degree; since some random sequences are computably dominated, 1-randomness is not enough. Kautz also showed that every 2-random sequence is **generalized low**: its jump is computable from the sequence and $\emptyset'$.

The tools are 0-1 laws. Kolmogorov's 0-1 law says that a class closed under finite changes has measure $0$ or $1$; classes defined by Turing degrees are of this kind. Its effective version, due to Kučera for $\Pi^0_1$ classes and to Kautz for $\Pi^0_2$ classes, says that a sufficiently random sequence has a **translate**, obtained by removing a finite prefix, in every effectively closed class of positive measure of the right complexity. A construction that succeeds on a class of positive measure then succeeds, up to a finite change, on every sufficiently random sequence.

## Translates

A sequence $B$ is a **translate** of $A$ if $A = \sigma B$ for a string $\sigma$. A translate of $A$ is Turing equivalent to $A$.

:::fact[Kolmogorov's 0-1 law]{#fact-kolmogorov-01}
A Borel class $\mathcal C \subseteq \cs$ that is closed under finite changes of its members has measure $0$ or $1$.
:::

This is admitted (see Downey–Hirschfeldt, Theorem 1.2.4, or any textbook of probability). A class defined by a property of Turing degrees, such as the class of sequences of hyperimmune degree, is closed under finite changes. So if it is Borel and has positive measure, it has measure $1$.

:::lemma[Kučera]{#lem-kucera-translate}
Let $\mathcal P$ be a $\Pi^0_1$ class of positive measure. Every random sequence has a translate in $\mathcal P$. The same holds relative to an oracle: if $\mathcal P$ is $\Pi^0_1(X)$ and $A$ is $X$-random, then $A$ has a translate in $\mathcal P$.
:::

:::proof{of=lem-kucera-translate}
Let $W$ be a c.e. prefix-free set of strings generating the complement $\mathcal U = [W]^{\prec}$ of $\mathcal P$ (:ref{#lem-prefix-free-generator}), so $\lambda(\mathcal U) < 1$. Let $S_0 = W$ and $S_{n+1} = \{ \sigma\tau : \sigma \in S_n, \tau \in W \}$. The sets $S_n$ are uniformly c.e. and prefix-free, and the weight of $S_{n+1}$ is the weight of $S_n$ times that of $W$, so $\lambda([S_n]^{\prec}) = \lambda(\mathcal U)^{n+1}$.

Suppose that no translate of $A$ is in $\mathcal P$. Then $A \in \mathcal U$, so $A = \sigma_0 A_1$ with $\sigma_0 \in W$. The translate $A_1$ is not in $\mathcal P$, so $A_1 = \sigma_1 A_2$ with $\sigma_1 \in W$, and so on: $A \in [S_n]^{\prec}$ for every $n$. Let $r < 1$ be a rational with $\lambda(\mathcal U) \leq r$, and $n_i$ a computable sequence with $r^{n_i + 1} \leq 2^{-i}$. The classes $[S_{n_i}]^{\prec}$ form a Martin-Löf test capturing $A$, so $A$ is not random. The proof relativizes.
:::

To use the lemma for 2-randomness, we need closed classes relative to $\emptyset'$ inside $\Pi^0_2$ classes.

:::lemma{#lem-pi02-inner}
Every $\Pi^0_2$ class $\mathcal P$ of positive measure contains a $\Pi^0_1(\emptyset')$ class of positive measure.
:::

:::proof{of=lem-pi02-inner}
Write $\mathcal P = \bigcap_n \mathcal U_n$ with $(\mathcal U_n)$ uniformly $\Sigma^0_1$, generated by uniformly c.e. sets of strings $W_n$. Fix a rational $\varepsilon$ with $0 < \varepsilon < \lambda(\mathcal P)$. The measures $\lambda(\mathcal U_n)$ are left-c.e., hence computable from $\emptyset'$ uniformly in $n$ (:ref{#prop-measure-effective}). With oracle $\emptyset'$, find for each $n$ a stage $s$ with $\lambda([W_{n, s}]^{\prec}) > \lambda(\mathcal U_n) - \varepsilon 2^{-n-1}$, and let $F_n = W_{n, s}$, a finite set. The class $\mathcal V = \bigcap_n [F_n]^{\prec}$ is $\Pi^0_1(\emptyset')$, as an intersection of clopen classes computable from $\emptyset'$. It is contained in $\mathcal P$, and $\lambda(\mathcal P \setminus \mathcal V) \leq \sum_n \lambda(\mathcal U_n \setminus [F_n]^{\prec}) \leq \varepsilon$, so $\lambda(\mathcal V) \geq \lambda(\mathcal P) - \varepsilon > 0$.
:::

:::theorem[Kautz]{#thm-kautz-01}
Let $\mathcal P$ be a $\Pi^0_2$ class of positive measure. Every 2-random sequence has a translate in $\mathcal P$. In particular, $\mathcal P$ contains a member of every 2-random degree.
:::

:::proof{of=thm-kautz-01}
By :ref{#lem-pi02-inner}, $\mathcal P$ contains a $\Pi^0_1(\emptyset')$ class $\mathcal V$ of positive measure. A 2-random sequence is $\emptyset'$-random, so it has a translate in $\mathcal V$ by :ref{#lem-kucera-translate} relativized to $\emptyset'$.
:::

:::quiz[translates]
In the proof of :ref{#lem-kucera-translate}, why is the measure of $[S_{n+1}]^{\prec}$ the measure of $[S_n]^{\prec}$ times $\lambda(\mathcal U)$?

- [x] Because $S_n$ and $W$ are prefix-free, the strings $\sigma\tau$ are pairwise distinct and form a prefix-free set, and $2^{-|\sigma\tau|} = 2^{-|\sigma|} 2^{-|\tau|}$; summing gives the product of the weights.
- [ ] Because $\mathcal U$ is closed under finite changes.

  $\mathcal U$ is an arbitrary $\Sigma^0_1$ class; the computation uses only prefix-freeness.
- [ ] Because $A$ is random.

  The measure computation does not involve $A$.

---

Why does :ref{#thm-kautz-01} need 2-randomness, and not 1-randomness?

- [x] The closed class of positive measure inside $\mathcal P$ is only closed relative to $\emptyset'$, so the translation lemma must be applied relative to $\emptyset'$.
- [ ] Because $\Pi^0_2$ classes have measure $0$ or $1$.

  They can have any measure; the theorem concerns those of positive measure.
- [ ] Because 1-random sequences have no translates.

  Every sequence has translates; the question is whether one of them lies in $\mathcal P$.
:::

## Hyperimmunity

:::theorem[Martin; Kautz]{#thm-martin-hyperimmune}
There is a Turing functional $\Xi$ such that the class $\mathcal G$ of sequences $A$ for which $\Xi^A$ is total has measure at least $\tfrac12$, and $\Xi^A$ is hyperimmune for every $A \in \mathcal G$. Consequently:

1. (Martin) almost every sequence has hyperimmune degree;
2. (Kautz) every 2-random sequence has hyperimmune degree.
:::

For one computable function $\Phi_e$, it is easy to make $\Xi^A$ escape it at one argument $w$, by defining $\Xi^A(w) = \Phi_e(w) + 1$. The difficulty is that $\Phi_e(w)$ may never converge, and $\Xi^A$ must be total. The solution is to risk only a small part of the oracles. The requirement for $\Phi_e$ works on the $2^{e+2}$ cylinders of length $e + 2$, one at a time: on the current cylinder, it keeps $\Xi^A(w)$ undefined until $\Phi_e(w)$ converges. If $\Phi_e$ is total, every cylinder is handled; otherwise the requirement is stuck on one cylinder of measure $2^{-e-2}$, but then $\Phi_e$ is not total and needs no escape. The stuck cylinders have total measure at most $\sum_e 2^{-e-2} = \tfrac12$.

:::proof{of=thm-martin-hyperimmune}
For each $e$, let $\nu^e_0, \dots, \nu^e_{m_e - 1}$ be the strings of length $e + 2$ in lexicographic order, $m_e = 2^{e+2}$. We enumerate axioms $(\tau, x, y)$, meaning $\Xi^A(x) = y$ for every $A$ extending $\tau$. The requirement $R_e$ has a current index $j_e$, initially $0$, and a current witness $w_e$. A number is **fresh** at stage $s$ if it is larger than $s$ and than every number used so far.

At stage $s$:

1. If $e = s$, set $j_e = 0$ and appoint a fresh witness $w_e$ for $(e, 0)$.
2. For each $e < s$ with $j_e < m_e$: if $\Phi_e(w_e)[s]\converge$, enumerate the axiom $(\nu^e_{j_e}, w_e, \Phi_e(w_e) + 1)$, increase $j_e$ by one, and if $j_e < m_e$, appoint a fresh witness $w_e$ for $(e, j_e)$.
3. If $s$ has been appointed as witness for some $(e, j)$, enumerate the axioms $(\tau, s, 0)$ for the strings $\tau \neq \nu^e_j$ of length $e + 2$. Otherwise, enumerate the axiom $(\varepsilon, s, 0)$.

Witnesses are fresh, so each number is appointed at most once, before it is reached as a stage in step 3; the axioms are consistent, and $\Xi$ is a Turing functional. For every $A$ and every number $x$, $\Xi^A(x)$ is defined by step 3, unless $x$ is a witness for some $(e, j)$ with $\nu^e_j \prec A$; in that case, $\Xi^A(x)$ is defined if and only if $\Phi_e(x)$ converges, and then $\Xi^A(x) = \Phi_e(x) + 1$.

*The measure of $\mathcal G$.* If $\Xi^A$ is partial, there are $e$ and $j$ such that $\nu^e_j \prec A$ and $\Phi_e$ diverges on the witness for $(e, j)$; then $j_e$ stays equal to $j$ forever. So $A$ belongs to the cylinder $[\nu^e_j]$ for the final value $j$ of $j_e$, for some $e$ with $j_e$ eventually constant below $m_e$. There is at most one such cylinder for each $e$, so the complement of $\mathcal G$ has measure at most $\sum_e 2^{-e-2} = \tfrac12$.

*Hyperimmunity.* Let $A \in \mathcal G$, and let $\Phi_e$ be total. Then $R_e$ handles all its cylinders, and for the $j$ with $\nu^e_j \prec A$ and the witness $w$ for $(e, j)$, $\Xi^A(w) = \Phi_e(w) + 1 > \Phi_e(w)$. So no total computable function majorizes $\Xi^A$, that is, is at least $\Xi^A$ at every argument. A computable function that dominated $\Xi^A$, that is, was at least $\Xi^A$ at almost every argument, could be modified at finitely many arguments into one that majorizes it. So $\Xi^A$ is hyperimmune.

*Consequences.* The class $\mathcal G$ is $\Pi^0_2$: $\Xi^A$ is total if and only if for every $x$ there is an axiom for $x$ compatible with $A$. (1) The class of sequences of hyperimmune degree is closed under finite changes, Borel since it is arithmetical, and contains $\mathcal G$, of measure at least $\tfrac12$; by Kolmogorov's 0-1 law, it has measure $1$. (2) If $A$ is 2-random, it has a translate $B \in \mathcal G$ by :ref{#thm-kautz-01}, and $B \equiv_T A$ computes the hyperimmune function $\Xi^B$.
:::

The hypothesis of 2-randomness cannot be weakened to 1-randomness: the computably dominated basis theorem gives a random sequence of computably dominated degree, that is, of hyperimmune-free degree (:ref{#cor-mlr-structure}). Nor to weak 2-randomness: a computably dominated random sequence is Kurtz random, hence weakly 2-random by Yu's theorem (:ref{#thm-kurtz-w2r-dominated}), and it has hyperimmune-free degree; so :ref{#thm-kautz-01} also fails for weak 2-randomness.

## Generalized lowness

A set $A$ is **generalized low** if $A' \leq_T A \oplus \emptyset'$. Low sets are generalized low. No set $A \geq_T \emptyset'$ is generalized low, since then $A \oplus \emptyset' \equiv_T A <_T A'$.

:::theorem[Kautz]{#thm-kautz-gl1}
Every 2-random sequence is generalized low.
:::

The jump $A'$ asks, for each $e$, whether $A$ belongs to the $\Sigma^0_1$ class $\mathcal C_e = \{ X : \Phi^X_e(e)\converge \}$. With $\emptyset'$, the class $\mathcal C_e$ can be approximated from inside by a clopen class up to a small measure. A sequence in the small difference is caught by a test relative to $\emptyset'$; a 2-random sequence avoids almost all of these differences, and for the others the clopen class answers the question, which $A \oplus \emptyset'$ can decide.

:::proof{of=thm-kautz-gl1}
The classes $\mathcal C_e$ are uniformly $\Sigma^0_1$, generated by uniformly c.e. sets of strings $W_e$. Their measures are computable from $\emptyset'$, uniformly. With oracle $\emptyset'$, find for each $e$ a stage $s$ with $\lambda([W_{e, s}]^{\prec}) > \lambda(\mathcal C_e) - 2^{-e}$, and let $F_e = W_{e, s}$. The classes $\mathcal D_e = \mathcal C_e \setminus [F_e]^{\prec}$ are uniformly $\Sigma^0_1(\emptyset')$, as intersections of $\Sigma^0_1$ classes with clopen classes computable from $\emptyset'$, and $\lambda(\mathcal D_e) < 2^{-e}$. So $(\mathcal D_e)$ is a Solovay test relative to $\emptyset'$.

Let $A$ be 2-random. By :ref{#thm-solovay} relativized to $\emptyset'$, $A$ belongs to only finitely many $\mathcal D_e$. For the other $e$, since $[F_e]^{\prec} \subseteq \mathcal C_e$, we have $e \in A'$ if and only if $A \in \mathcal C_e$ if and only if $A \in [F_e]^{\prec}$. The last condition is decided by $A \oplus \emptyset'$: compute $F_e$ with $\emptyset'$, then check whether $A$ extends one of its strings. So $A'$ differs from a set computable in $A \oplus \emptyset'$ at finitely many numbers, and $A' \leq_T A \oplus \emptyset'$.
:::

Again 1-randomness is not enough: $\Omega$ is random and Turing equivalent to $\emptyset'$, so it is not generalized low (:ref{#ex-kucera-jump}).

::::exercise[Generalized lowness below the halting problem]{#ex-kucera-jump difficulty=1 objective="Compare lowness and generalized lowness for sequences computable in the halting problem."}
Show that a set $A \leq_T \emptyset'$ is generalized low if and only if it is low. Deduce that $\Omega$ is not generalized low, while some random sequences computable in $\emptyset'$ are.

:::solution
If $A \leq_T \emptyset'$, then $A \oplus \emptyset' \equiv_T \emptyset'$, so $A' \leq_T A \oplus \emptyset'$ if and only if $A' \leq_T \emptyset'$, that is, $A$ is low. $\Omega \equiv_T \emptyset'$ (:ref{#thm-omega}) is not low, since $\Omega' \equiv_T \emptyset'' >_T \emptyset'$. A low random sequence exists by the low basis theorem (:ref{#cor-mlr-structure}); it is computable in $\emptyset'$ and generalized low. So among the random sequences below $\emptyset'$, the generalized low ones are exactly the low ones, and 2-random sequences are never below $\emptyset'$.
:::

## Notes and references

Kolmogorov's 0-1 law is from his 1933 monograph. The translation lemma is Kučera's (1985), and the effective 0-1 law for $\Pi^0_n$ classes and $n$-randomness is Kautz's (1991). Martin's theorem that almost every degree is hyperimmune is unpublished; Kautz observed that the proof gives every 2-random degree, and proved that every 2-random sequence is generalized low. Downey and Hirschfeldt present these results in Sections 6.10, 8.14 and 8.21 (Lemma 6.10.1, Theorems 6.10.2, 8.14.1, 8.21.1 and 8.21.2). Kurtz proved that almost every degree computes a 1-generic set and is computably enumerable in a strictly smaller degree, and Kautz that 2-randomness suffices for both (admitted; Downey–Hirschfeldt, Section 8.21).

- A. Kučera, *Measure, $\Pi^0_1$-classes and complete extensions of PA*, in: Recursion Theory Week, Lecture Notes in Mathematics 1141, Springer, 1985, 245–259.
- S. M. Kautz, *Degrees of random sets*, PhD thesis, Cornell University, 1991.
- S. A. Kurtz, *Randomness and genericity in the degrees of unsolvability*, PhD thesis, University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign, 1981.
- R. G. Downey and D. R. Hirschfeldt, *Algorithmic Randomness and Complexity*, Springer, 2010, Sections 6.10, 8.14 and 8.21.

:::::exercises
::::exercise{#ex-01-degree-invariant difficulty=2 objective="Apply Kautz's 0-1 law to degree-invariant classes."}
Let $\mathcal C$ be a $\Sigma^0_3$ class closed under finite changes. Show that $\mathcal C$ contains every 2-random sequence or no 2-random sequence.

:::solution
By Kolmogorov's 0-1 law, $\lambda(\mathcal C)$ is $0$ or $1$. Write $\mathcal C = \bigcup_n \mathcal P_n$ with uniformly $\Pi^0_2$ classes $\mathcal P_n$. If $\lambda(\mathcal C) = 1$, some $\mathcal P_n$ has positive measure, and every 2-random sequence $A$ has a translate in $\mathcal P_n \subseteq \mathcal C$ by :ref{#thm-kautz-01}; since $\mathcal C$ is closed under finite changes, $A \in \mathcal C$. If $\lambda(\mathcal C) = 0$, each $\mathcal P_n$ is a null $\Pi^0_2$ class. Write $\mathcal P_n = \bigcap_k \mathcal U_{n,k}$ with uniformly $\Sigma^0_1$ classes, which we may take decreasing in $k$; then $\lambda(\mathcal U_{n,k}) \to 0$, and with $\emptyset'$, which computes these measures, one finds $k_i$ with $\lambda(\mathcal U_{n,k_i}) \leq 2^{-i}$. This is a Martin-Löf test relative to $\emptyset'$ capturing $\mathcal P_n$, so no 2-random sequence is in $\mathcal P_n$, nor in $\mathcal C$. (Kautz; Downey–Hirschfeldt, Corollary 6.10.3.)
:::
