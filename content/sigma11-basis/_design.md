# Design log: Σ¹₁ classes and the hyperjump

This file is the memory of the course (see "Designing tracks and courses" in
`AGENTS.md`). It is not rendered.

## 1. Objectives

After the course, a student can:

- work with hyperarithmetic reducibility and the hyperjump, and use morphisms of trees to
  compare ranks;
- prove that Σ¹₁(Y) well-founded trees have rank below ω₁^Y, that ≤_h preserves the
  Church–Kleene ordinal, and Spector's theorem (O ≤_h Y iff ω₁^Y > ω₁ᶜᵏ);
- prove the Gandy basis theorem with trees of tuples carrying witnesses;
- use forcing with Σ¹₁ classes (Gandy's Baire category theorem) and prove Kreisel's
  hyperarithmetic cone avoidance basis theorem;
- show that O and its hyperjumps are Π¹₁ singletons and prove the gap theorem.

**Role.** Fourth core course of the higher computability track, added on 2026-11-17
(`content/tracks-design.md`). It replaces the admitted Gandy basis theorem of
`hyperarithmetic/sigma11-classes` by a proof, and proves Spector's theorem, which
`higher-randomness/hyperdegrees` quotes from Nies.

## 2. Prerequisites assumed

- **Computable ordinals:** ranks of trees (`def-tree-rank`, `prop-rank-computable`), the
  tree of descending sequences (`prop-descending-tree`), the normal form theorem and the
  completeness of O (`thm-pi11-normal-form`, `thm-o-complete`), O not Σ¹₁
  (`prop-pi11-not-sigma11`), ω₁^O > ω₁ᶜᵏ (`prop-omega1-relative`), Markwald–Kleene
  (`thm-markwald`), the c.e. relation R (`prop-kleene-o-basic`).
- **Hyperarithmetic sets:** H-sets, Spector uniqueness, Kleene's theorem and its
  relativization, `prop-h-delta11`, the Kleene basis theorem.
- **Descriptive set theory:** Σ¹₁ singletons are hyperarithmetic and HYP is Π¹₁
  (`prop-sigma11-singleton`), Kreisel uniformization (cited).

## 3. Results, lesson by lesson

1. **hyperjump** — ≤_h and the hyperjump (`def-hyperarithmetic-in`, `prop-hyperjump`),
   morphisms (`lem-tree-morphism`), the boundedness of Σ¹₁(Y) well-founded trees
   (`thm-sigma11-tree-bound`), `cor-h-omega1`, Spector's theorem
   (`thm-spector-collapse`).
2. **gandy-basis** — trees of tuples, the Gandy basis theorem (`thm-gandy-basis`).
3. **kreisel-basis** — the collapsing sets form a Π¹₁ class (`prop-collapse-pi11`),
   ≤_h below non-collapsing sets through H^Y_a with a ∈ O, Gandy forcing
   (`thm-gandy-forcing`), Kreisel's basis theorem (`thm-kreisel-basis`).
4. **pi11-singletons** — O is a Π¹₁ singleton (`prop-o-pi11-singleton`), so are its
   hyperjumps (`prop-hyperjump-singleton`), morphisms bound ranks (`lem-morphism-rank`), the
   gap theorem (`thm-pi11-singleton-gap`).

## 4. Proof techniques

- Morphisms of trees and the boundedness argument (definability of O from a tree of large
  rank).
- Building witnesses with the member (trees of tuples): the substitute for compactness in
  Baire space.
- Forcing with Σ¹₁ classes, density, and the Σ¹₁ singleton argument for avoidance.

## 5. Choices

- **Boundedness through morphisms** (Monin–Patey) rather than Spector's Σ¹₁ boundedness for
  well-orderings, which is in `computable-ordinals/boundedness`; the tree version gives
  Spector's collapse theorem and the gap theorem with the same lemma.
- **Trees of tuples** make the witnesses of Monin–Patey's proof explicit: one coordinate per
  step, with a constant coordinate when no witness is added, so that all trees have the same
  shape.
- **The gap theorem** (Π¹₁ singletons are hyperarithmetic or above O) is proved with
  morphisms; it is not stated in this form in Monin–Patey, whose proofs of the basis theorem
  for Π¹₁ classes (Guaspari) and of the Kondo–Addison uniformization are admitted as too long
  for the course.
- **Excluded:** the class C of Guaspari and the Mansfield–Solovay theorem; the hierarchy of
  hyperjumps beyond a mention; HYP and ATR₀ (in `hyperarithmetic/pseudo-well-orderings` and
  `big-five`).

## 6. Comparison with the literature

- **Monin–Patey**, chapters "Beyond hyperarithmetic" (other boundedness theorems,
  hyperarithmetic reducibility) and "Σ¹₁ and Π¹₁ classes" (canonical representation, basis
  theorems, topological space generated by the Σ¹₁ classes, Kreisel's basis theorem,
  emblematic Π¹₁ classes, Π¹₁ singletons), checked in `external-data/monin-patey`.
- **Sacks, Part A**, and **Chong–Yu, Chapters 2–5**: not available locally; the
  correspondence of sections is not checked (see open points).

## 7. Pedagogical plan

The low basis and cone avoidance theorems for Π⁰₁ classes are the model of each lesson; the
difference (no compactness, relativization of ω₁ᶜᵏ) is stated before the proof. One figure:
`scripts/figures/sigma11-basis.py` (trees of tuples).

- Gandy–Harrington forcing is now a standard tool in descriptive set theory (Harrington's
  proof of Silver's theorem; Kanovei's monographs), and in higher randomness (Chong–Nies–Yu,
  Monin). Not developed further here.
