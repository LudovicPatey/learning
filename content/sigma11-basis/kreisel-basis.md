The low basis theorem for $\Pi^0_1$ classes has a companion, the cone avoidance basis theorem: a non-empty $\Pi^0_1$ class has a member that does not compute a given non-computable set. Its higher analogue is due to Kreisel: a non-empty $\Sigma^1_1$ class has a member that is not hyperarithmetically above a given non-hyperarithmetic set. The proof is a forcing argument with $\Sigma^1_1$ classes as conditions, **Gandy forcing**. The witness technique of the previous lesson turns into a general principle: in the topology generated by the $\Sigma^1_1$ classes, a countable intersection of dense open sets is dense, as in the Baire category theorem.

Two facts make the forcing work. First, the sets that do not collapse $\omega_1^{\mathrm{CK}}$ form a $\Sigma^1_1$ class; restricting to it, $X \leq_h Y$ means that $X$ is computable in some $H^Y_a$ with $a$ a notation of $\mathcal O$, a countable list of conditions that can be diagonalized one at a time. Second, a $\Sigma^1_1$ class with a single member has a hyperarithmetic member; so if every member of a condition computed $X$ in the same way, $X$ would be hyperarithmetic.

## The class of collapsing sets

:::proposition{#prop-collapse-pi11}
The class $\{ Y : \omega_1^Y > \omega_1^{\mathrm{CK}} \}$ is $\Pi^1_1$. Equivalently, the class $\{ Y : \omega_1^Y = \omega_1^{\mathrm{CK}} \}$ is $\Sigma^1_1$.
:::

:::proof{of=prop-collapse-pi11}
We show that $\omega_1^Y > \omega_1^{\mathrm{CK}}$ if and only if there is $a \in \mathcal O^Y$ such that the order $<_{\mathcal O^Y}$ restricted to the notations below $a$ is isomorphic to no computable linear order.

If $\omega_1^Y > \omega_1^{\mathrm{CK}}$, the ordinal $\omega_1^{\mathrm{CK}}$ is $Y$-computable, so it has a notation $a \in \mathcal O^Y$, by the Markwald–Kleene theorem relativized (:ref{#thm-markwald}). The notations below $a$ are ordered with type $\omega_1^{\mathrm{CK}}$. A computable linear order is either ill-founded or a well-ordering of computable type, below $\omega_1^{\mathrm{CK}}$; in both cases it is not isomorphic to this order. Conversely, if $\omega_1^Y = \omega_1^{\mathrm{CK}}$, every $a \in \mathcal O^Y$ has $|a| < \omega_1^{\mathrm{CK}}$, and the notations below $a$ are ordered with type $|a|$, a computable ordinal, isomorphic to a computable well-ordering.

The condition "$a \in \mathcal O^Y$" is $\Pi^1_1(Y)$, uniformly. For $a \in \mathcal O^Y$, the set of notations below $a$ and their order are c.e. in $Y$, uniformly in $a$ (:ref{#prop-kleene-o-basic} relativized), so "for every index $e$ of a computable linear order and every function $f$, $f$ is not an isomorphism between it and the notations below $a$" is $\Pi^1_1(Y)$. A number quantifier $\exists a$ in front preserves $\Pi^1_1$.
:::

The $\Pi^1_1$ class of the proposition is not $\Sigma^1_1$: by the Gandy basis theorem, every non-empty $\Sigma^1_1$ class has a member that does not collapse $\omega_1^{\mathrm{CK}}$, so a non-empty $\Sigma^1_1$ class is never included in it.

For a set $Y$ with $\omega_1^Y = \omega_1^{\mathrm{CK}}$, the sets hyperarithmetic in $Y$ are the sets computable in $H^Y_a$ for some $a \in \mathcal O$. Indeed, by Kleene's theorem relativized, $X \leq_h Y$ if and only if $X \leq_T H^Y_b$ for some $b \in \mathcal O^Y$; then $|b| < \omega_1^Y = \omega_1^{\mathrm{CK}}$ has a notation $a \in \mathcal O$. A notation of $\mathcal O$ is also a notation relative to $Y$, reading each index of a computable fundamental sequence as an index of the same sequence computed with an oracle that is not consulted, and Spector's uniqueness theorem relativized (:ref{#thm-spector-uniqueness}) gives $H^Y_a \equiv_T H^Y_b$. Moreover the relation $H = H^Y_a$, for $a \in \mathcal O$, is $\Delta^1_1$ in $(H, Y)$, uniformly in $a$, by :ref{#prop-h-delta11} relativized.

## Gandy forcing

A family $\mathfrak D$ of $\Sigma^1_1$ classes is **dense below** a $\Sigma^1_1$ class $\mathcal A$ if every non-empty $\Sigma^1_1$ class $\mathcal C \subseteq \mathcal A$ meets some member of $\mathfrak D$.

:::theorem[Gandy]{#thm-gandy-forcing}
Let $\mathcal A$ be a non-empty $\Sigma^1_1$ class, and let $\mathfrak D_0, \mathfrak D_1, \dots$ be countable families of $\Sigma^1_1$ classes, each dense below $\mathcal A$. Then there is $X \in \mathcal A$ that belongs, for every $n$, to some member of $\mathfrak D_n$.
:::

:::proof{of=thm-gandy-forcing}
As in the proof of :ref{#thm-gandy-basis}, we build computable trees of tuples $R_n$, of $(n+2)$-tuples, and nodes $\rho_n \in R_n$ of length $n$, satisfying conditions 1 to 4 of that proof, with $R_0$ a computable tree of pairs whose projection is $\mathcal A$ and $\rho_0 = (\varepsilon, \varepsilon)$.

*Step $n$.* The class $\mathcal C_n$ of the first coordinates $f_1$ of the paths of $R_n$ extending $\rho_n$ is $\Sigma^1_1$, non-empty by condition 4, and included in $\mathcal A$, by condition 2 applied down to $R_0$. By density, it meets some $\mathcal D \in \mathfrak D_n$. Let $W$ be a computable tree of pairs whose projection is $\mathcal D$, and let $R_{n+1}$ be the tree of the tuples $(\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_{n+2}, \tau)$ with $(\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_{n+2}) \in R_n$ and $(\sigma_1, \tau) \in W$. It has a path whose first coordinates extend $\rho_n$, since $\mathcal C_n \cap \mathcal D \neq \emptyset$. Let $\rho_{n+1}$ be a node of length $n + 1$ of $R_{n+1}$ whose first $n + 2$ coordinates extend $\rho_n$ and through which $R_{n+1}$ has a path.

*The limit.* As in the proof of :ref{#thm-gandy-basis}, the coordinates of the nodes $\rho_n$ converge to functions $f_1, f_2, \dots$, and $(f_1, \dots, f_{k+2})$ is a path of $R_k$ for every $k$. So $X = f_1 \in \mathcal A$, and $(f_1, f_{n+3})$ is a path of the tree $W$ chosen at step $n$, so $X$ belongs to the chosen $\mathcal D \in \mathfrak D_n$.
:::

In the language of forcing, the conditions are the non-empty $\Sigma^1_1$ classes, a condition extends another if it is included in it, and each family $\mathfrak D_n$ defines a dense set of conditions, those included in a member of $\mathfrak D_n$. The theorem says that a sufficiently generic filter has a member, which is not automatic for $\Sigma^1_1$ classes, and it is the witnesses that provide it. Neither the families nor the choices need to be effective.

:::quiz[Gandy forcing]
In the proof of :ref{#thm-gandy-forcing}, why is the class $\mathcal C_n$ a subclass of $\mathcal A$?

- [x] Every path of $R_n$ restricts, by condition 2 applied $n$ times, to a path of $R_0$, whose first coordinate is in $\mathcal A$.
- [ ] Because $\mathfrak D_n$ is dense below $\mathcal A$.

  Density is applied to $\mathcal C_n$, and requires that $\mathcal C_n$ be a subclass of $\mathcal A$; it does not prove it.
- [ ] Because the nodes $\rho_n$ are compatible.

  Compatibility of the nodes gives the limit; inclusion in $\mathcal A$ comes from the trees.
:::

## Kreisel's basis theorem

:::theorem[Kreisel]{#thm-kreisel-basis}
Let $\mathcal A$ be a non-empty $\Sigma^1_1$ class and $X$ a set that is not hyperarithmetic. Then $\mathcal A$ has a member $Y$ with $X \not\leq_h Y$ and $\omega_1^Y = \omega_1^{\mathrm{CK}}$.
:::

:::proof{of=thm-kreisel-basis}
By :ref{#prop-collapse-pi11}, the class $\mathcal A' = \mathcal A \cap \{ Y : \omega_1^Y = \omega_1^{\mathrm{CK}} \}$ is $\Sigma^1_1$, and it is non-empty by :ref{#thm-gandy-basis}. For $Y \in \mathcal A'$, $X \leq_h Y$ if and only if $\Phi^{H^Y_a}_e = X$ for some $e$ and some $a \in \mathcal O$, by the remarks of the first section.

For $e \in \nat$ and $a \in \mathcal O$, let $\mathfrak D_{e, a}$ be the family of the classes
$$
\mathcal D_{e, a, m} = \{ Y : \Phi^{H^Y_a}_e(m) \text{ is undefined or different from } X(m) \}, \qquad m \in \nat.
$$
Each $\mathcal D_{e, a, m}$ is $\Sigma^1_1$: $Y$ belongs to it if and only if there is $H$ with $H = H^Y_a$ and $\Phi^H_e(m)$ undefined or different from the bit $X(m)$, a relation that is $\Delta^1_1$ in $(H, Y)$.

*Density.* Let $\mathcal C \subseteq \mathcal A'$ be a non-empty $\Sigma^1_1$ class, and suppose that it meets no $\mathcal D_{e, a, m}$. Then $\Phi^{H^Y_a}_e = X$ for every $Y \in \mathcal C$, and
$$
\{ X \} = \{ Z : \exists Y \in \mathcal C\ \exists H\ (H = H^Y_a \wedge \Phi^H_e = Z) \},
$$
a $\Sigma^1_1$ class with a single member. By :ref{#prop-sigma11-singleton}, $X$ would be hyperarithmetic, a contradiction. So $\mathfrak D_{e, a}$ is dense below $\mathcal A'$.

By :ref{#thm-gandy-forcing}, applied to $\mathcal A'$ and the countably many families $\mathfrak D_{e, a}$, there is $Y \in \mathcal A'$ such that for every $e$ and every $a \in \mathcal O$, $\Phi^{H^Y_a}_e(m)$ is undefined or different from $X(m)$ for some $m$. So $\Phi^{H^Y_a}_e \neq X$ for all $e$ and $a \in \mathcal O$, and since $\omega_1^Y = \omega_1^{\mathrm{CK}}$, $X \not\leq_h Y$.
:::

:::quiz[Kreisel's basis theorem]
Why is the class restricted to the sets $Y$ with $\omega_1^Y = \omega_1^{\mathrm{CK}}$ at the beginning of the proof of :ref{#thm-kreisel-basis}?

- [x] For such $Y$, the sets hyperarithmetic in $Y$ are computable in the sets $H^Y_a$ with $a \in \mathcal O$, a list fixed in advance; for a collapsing $Y$, notations relative to $Y$ beyond $\omega_1^{\mathrm{CK}}$ would give new sets, such as $\mathcal O$, which the forcing does not control.
- [ ] Because the class $\mathcal A$ might be empty otherwise.

  $\mathcal A$ is non-empty by hypothesis; the restriction can only make it smaller, and the Gandy basis theorem shows that it stays non-empty.
- [ ] Because $X$ is not hyperarithmetic.

  This hypothesis is used in the density argument, not for the restriction.
:::

::::exercise[No least non-hyperarithmetic hyperdegree]{#ex-no-least-nonhyp difficulty=1 objective="Apply Kreisel's basis theorem to the class of non-hyperarithmetic sets."}
Show that for every non-hyperarithmetic set $X$, there is a non-hyperarithmetic set $Y$ with $X \not\leq_h Y$. Deduce that there is no least non-zero hyperdegree.

:::solution
Apply :ref{#thm-kreisel-basis} to the non-empty $\Sigma^1_1$ class of non-hyperarithmetic sets (:ref{#prop-sigma11-singleton} (2)) and to $X$. It gives a non-hyperarithmetic $Y$ with $X \not\leq_h Y$. If the hyperdegree of $X$ were the least non-zero one, every non-hyperarithmetic $Y$ would satisfy $X \leq_h Y$, which fails for this $Y$. (There are, however, minimal non-zero hyperdegrees, by a theorem of Gandy and Sacks.)
:::

## Notes and references

Kreisel's basis theorem is from his 1962 paper on the axiom of choice and the class of hyperarithmetic functions. Gandy's theorem on intersections of dense families is the effective Baire category theorem for the Gandy–Harrington topology, generated by the $\Sigma^1_1$ classes; Harrington used this topology for his proof of Silver's theorem on co-analytic equivalence relations. The presentation follows Monin and Patey (chapter "$\Sigma^1_1$ and $\Pi^1_1$ classes", sections "Topological space generated by the $\Sigma^1_1$ classes" and "Hyperarithmetic cone avoidance basis theorem", and the proposition on the class of collapsing sets).

- G. Kreisel, *The axiom of choice and the class of hyperarithmetic functions*, Indagationes Mathematicae 24 (1962), 307–319.
- R. O. Gandy, *On a problem of Kleene's*, Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society 66 (1960), 501–502.
- B. Monin and L. Patey, *Computability theory*, chapter "$\Sigma^1_1$ and $\Pi^1_1$ classes".
- C. T. Chong and L. Yu, *Recursion Theory: Computational Aspects of Definability*, De Gruyter, 2015.

:::::exercises
::::exercise{#ex-collapse-cone difficulty=1 objective="Restate the class of collapsing sets as a cone, and locate it in the projective hierarchy."}
Show that $\{ Y : \mathcal O \leq_h Y \}$ is a $\Pi^1_1$ class that is not $\Sigma^1_1$, and that it contains no non-empty $\Sigma^1_1$ class.

:::solution
By :ref{#thm-spector-collapse}, it is the class $\{ Y : \omega_1^Y > \omega_1^{\mathrm{CK}} \}$, which is $\Pi^1_1$ by :ref{#prop-collapse-pi11}. A non-empty $\Sigma^1_1$ subclass would have a member $X$ with $\omega_1^X = \omega_1^{\mathrm{CK}}$ by :ref{#thm-gandy-basis}, which is impossible. The class itself is non-empty, since it contains $\mathcal O$; so it is not $\Sigma^1_1$.
:::

::::exercise{#ex-incomparable-hyperdegrees difficulty=2 objective="Add dense families to Gandy forcing to build incomparable hyperdegrees."}
Show that there are sets $X$ and $Y$ with $\omega_1^X = \omega_1^Y = \omega_1^{\mathrm{CK}}$ such that $X \not\leq_h Y$ and $Y \not\leq_h X$.

:::solution
Let $X$ be the set of :ref{#ex-hyperlow-nonhyp}: not hyperarithmetic, $\mathcal O^X \leq_T \mathcal O$, $X \leq_T \mathcal O$ and $\omega_1^X = \omega_1^{\mathrm{CK}}$. Let $\mathcal A'$ be the class of the non-hyperarithmetic sets $Y$ with $\omega_1^Y = \omega_1^{\mathrm{CK}}$, which is $\Sigma^1_1$ by :ref{#prop-sigma11-singleton} and :ref{#prop-collapse-pi11}, and non-empty since it contains $X$.

Take the families $\mathfrak D_{e, a}$ of the proof of :ref{#thm-kreisel-basis}, for $X$, which are dense below $\mathcal A'$. Add, for each set $Z \leq_h X$, the family $\mathfrak E_Z$ of the classes $\{ Y : Y(m) \neq Z(m) \}$, $m \in \nat$; there are countably many such $Z$. Each of these classes is $\Sigma^1_1$, being defined by a single bit. The family $\mathfrak E_Z$ is dense below $\mathcal A'$: a non-empty $\Sigma^1_1$ class $\mathcal C \subseteq \mathcal A'$ that meets none of its members is included in $\{ Z \}$, so it equals $\{ Z \}$, and $Z$ would be a hyperarithmetic member of $\mathcal A'$ (:ref{#prop-sigma11-singleton}), which is impossible. By :ref{#thm-gandy-forcing}, there is $Y \in \mathcal A'$ with $X \not\leq_h Y$ and $Y \neq Z$ for every $Z \leq_h X$, that is, $Y \not\leq_h X$.
:::
::::

::::exercise{#ex-gandy-forcing-generic-collapse difficulty=1 objective="See that the generic member of Gandy forcing can be required to preserve ω₁ᶜᵏ."}
Show that in :ref{#thm-gandy-forcing} one may also require $\omega_1^X = \omega_1^{\mathrm{CK}}$.

:::solution
Apply the theorem to $\mathcal A \cap \{ Y : \omega_1^Y = \omega_1^{\mathrm{CK}} \}$, which is $\Sigma^1_1$ by :ref{#prop-collapse-pi11} and non-empty by :ref{#thm-gandy-basis}. The families $\mathfrak D_n$ are dense below $\mathcal A$, hence below this subclass, since every $\Sigma^1_1$ subclass of the subclass is a subclass of $\mathcal A$.
:::

::::exercise{#ex-kreisel-countable-avoid difficulty=2 objective="Extend Kreisel's basis theorem to countably many sets."}
Let $X_0, X_1, \dots$ be sets that are not hyperarithmetic. Show that every non-empty $\Sigma^1_1$ class has a member $Y$ with $X_i \not\leq_h Y$ for every $i$.

:::solution
Run the proof of :ref{#thm-kreisel-basis} with the families $\mathfrak D_{i, e, a}$, for $i, e \in \nat$ and $a \in \mathcal O$, made of the classes $\{ Y : \Phi^{H^Y_a}_e(m) \text{ is undefined or different from } X_i(m) \}$. Each family is dense below $\mathcal A'$, by the argument of the proof with $X_i$ in place of $X$, and there are countably many families. :ref{#thm-gandy-forcing} gives $Y \in \mathcal A'$ in a member of each family, so $\Phi^{H^Y_a}_e \neq X_i$ for all $i$, $e$ and $a \in \mathcal O$, and $X_i \not\leq_h Y$.
:::
